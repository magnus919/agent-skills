# Tactical practice: parklets, guerrilla gardening and contested space

## Keep the punk

Solarpunk's relationship to infrastructure can be oppositional, as Flynn explicitly argues.
The manifesto also makes rebellion part of its framing. These texts support taking the
politics seriously without making every user's work follow one program. [S01–S02]

Related practices can proceed with, around, or against official institutions. John Bela's
first-person account calls the original 2005 Park(ing) installation guerrilla art; the
Tactical Urbanism guide landing page explicitly includes sanctioned and unsanctioned
approaches. Do not rewrite this history as exclusively a municipal permission process.
Neither source establishes the legal status of every intervention. [S31–S32]

## A small park in a parking space

A parklet turns a curbside parking footprint into a place to sit, talk and linger, often
with planting. Toronto documents temporary platforms and seasonal removal; NACTO describes
public seating and maintenance arrangements. GDCI supplies examples beyond North America,
including São Paulo and Lima. These are public design/program references, not proof of
the current status or outcomes of a specific site. [S28–S30]

**Illustrative scene:** a tiny neighborhood room outdoors, reclaimed from vehicle storage;
friends sit amid raised planters, with shade and an open connection to the sidewalk.
This image can celebrate occupation and collective imagination without pretending that
a planter by itself is a certified traffic barrier.

**Planning synthesis:** ask who can use it without buying anything, how it connects to the
sidewalk, who maintains it, and what other uses of the curb are affected. Study sightlines,
accessibility, drainage, utilities, deliveries, emergency access and seasonal conditions
through current local guidance. Do not copy a foreign city's dimensions as universal law.

## Guerrilla gardening and seed bombs

GuerrillaGardening.org documents seed bombs as a means of sowing hard-to-reach locations,
including spontaneous interventions. Its practitioner guide also says sustained attention
can produce better results and that hardened clay balls need rain. This is practitioner
knowledge, not a controlled comparison. [S33]

University of Arizona extension describes possible advantages for seed protection and
contact in arid landscapes. A 2026 field-study abstract reports enhanced initial emergence
but no improvement in two-year establishment in its experiment. These findings support
neither “seed bombs always work” nor “seed bombs are useless.” [S34–S35]

For practical help, determine the region, site, intended plants and desired outcome before
choosing seeds or a method. Compare germination, emergence and establishment as different
outcomes. Treat species/site fit, rainfall and subsequent care as research questions,
rather than add an unsupported warning or promise. If asked for a recipe, the practitioner
guide supplies one clay/compost/seed formulation; cite it as one method, not an empirically
optimal universal recipe. Its classic formulation is five parts clay, one part compost
and one part seeds, bound with water; the source notes that hardened balls need rain.
Present this as the practitioner's formulation, not as a universal optimum. [S33]
Check suitability locally before recommending a seed mix.

An illustration of seeds bursting into flowers can use visual metaphor. A restoration
claim needs evidence beyond the picture, the number of seed balls thrown, or initial sprouts.

## Permission, law and agent authority are different questions

Describe unsanctioned practices accurately and discuss civil disobedience, legitimacy,
public-space allocation and political disagreement without automatically moralizing or
refusing because a practice may break a rule. Do not assume official approval establishes
justice, or that a neighborhood agrees merely because one participant says it does.

For an actual plan, identify what is known about permission/legal status and what is not;
use current local sources if the question depends on law. Do not invent universal legality
or instructions to evade accountability. Keep the agent's own authority separate: explaining,
writing fiction and drafting proposals do not authorize spending, posting, contacting
people, or physical alteration. The host's action gate is an agent boundary, not a demand
that all solarpunk actors request state consent.

## Deliver the requested work

For art or a story, retain audacity, delight and the chosen political stance. For history,
provide sources and disagreements. For practice, provide evidence-matched options and
unresolved local questions. Do not replace the user's intent with either an official
application checklist or an unsubstantiated claim that every intervention helps.
