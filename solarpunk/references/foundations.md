# Foundations: a plural umbrella

## What the anchor contributes

ReDes presents solarpunk as connected creative and practical activity: ecological futures,
social inclusion, reuse, local ingenuity, and change within living memory. Its strong
political language is one strand of the movement. Its 22 points are orientation, not an
empirical research paper or a membership test. [S01]

Flynn's 2014 essay brings infrastructure, repair, and ordinary human agency into the
foreground. Springett later describes a plural space where different futures can coexist.
These are participants' accounts, not a formal consensus or an exhaustive history. [S02–S03]

**Working interpretation for this skill:** solarpunk connects ecological possibility with
human agency and the experience of a life people would actually want. The connection can
appear in a textile, an apartment, a fable, a repair cooperative, a public service, or a
river settlement. Do not insist every output contain every dimension.

## Political posture

Start with a light preference for solidarity, ecological responsibility, shared agency,
and less extractive institutions. Recognize post-capitalist, anarchist, decolonial,
cooperative, and other positions as distinct traditions, not interchangeable labels.
Users may want municipal public services, social enterprise, markets, religious community,
mutual aid, or a deliberately apolitical image. Accept their framing and explore its
consequences without claiming it represents everyone's solarpunk.

When asked about the manifesto, explain its explicit politics accurately. When asked for
a scene, show care and agency through decisions about the scene. Do not append an essay
about ideological compliance. If a user wants corporate luxury with no collective scope,
you can produce the brief and, if classification matters, describe its ecological-futurist
qualities and tensions with some solarpunk traditions without withholding assistance.

## Hope without denial

The manifesto rejects dystopia; later publishing includes a solarpunk horror call and
accessible-futures work. This is evidence of divergent editorial practice, not proof that
any dark story is solarpunk. Useful questions are what can still be repaired, whose action
matters, and what the work invites its audience to imagine. [S01, S04]

Hope can be a repaired ferry returning to service, someone learning a skill, a community
reversing an exclusion, or a temporary shelter becoming a durable institution. Conflict,
anger, death, disability, difficult weather, and political disagreement need not disappear.
Do not promise inevitable progress or require cheerful affect from people living through harm.

## Cultural and geographic specificity

Do not treat European Art Nouveau as the compulsory visual grammar or the Global South
as a source of decorative improvisation. Start with named places, makers, material
traditions, and living communities. Research permissions around culturally restricted
knowledge and avoid claiming authentic representation from a few borrowed motifs.

Existing Indigenous and local ecological practices precede the label. A useful comparison
does not make their practitioners solarpunks or transfer their intellectual authority.
Design Justice offers a relevant participation ethic; it is an adjacent framework with its
own authorship and license, not a solarpunk doctrine. [S06]

## Explain at the right depth

- Beginner: give a concrete example connecting beauty, ecology, and agency.
- Historian: separate early texts, later reinterpretation, and uncertain origin claims.
- Critic: compare institutions, labor, extraction, and material limits rather than policing colors.
- Maker: begin with the asset and skill already present.
- Skeptic: offer bounded examples and uncertainty; do not argue that optimism is evidence.
