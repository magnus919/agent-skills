# Adaptive reuse and real-world examples

## From an existing asset to a different life

A retired industrial barge can become a bright planted home with rooftop solar and electric
propulsion. This is an illustrative composite: no particular vessel, owner, age, energy
performance or video is verified here. The appeal is continuity and transformation—useful
old steel, new comfort, cleaner operation, and life among plants—rather than a spotless
new building styled to look ecological.

The same design question applies to workshops, warehouses, shops, apartment blocks,
vehicles and discarded objects: what is worth retaining, what must change, and who can
keep it useful? Pleasure, craftsmanship, memory and belonging can matter alongside energy.

## Work the barge example at the right level

**Creative brief:** retain working-boat proportions and industrial traces; show plants,
daylight, domestic life, useful power equipment and repair. Avoid claiming a picture proves
full solar autonomy. See the visual reference for a complete prompt.

**Practical feasibility:** investigate hull condition and remaining service life, corrosion,
insulation/condensation, ventilation, daylight versus heat loss/gain, safe escape, water and
waste handling, mooring/occupancy rights, insurance and accessible boarding. Windows can
affect hull/cabin structure; wet planting media, panels and batteries add weight and change
stability. A competent marine professional must assess alterations before specifying them.

Electric propulsion is a system: motor/drive, batteries, controls, charging, protection,
maintenance and contingency. Solar, shore power and other sources can charge a battery;
quiet electric operation does not establish exclusively solar origin. The IWA describes
multiple charging options. UK boating guidance is geographically bounded; consult the
actual navigation authority elsewhere. [S24–S26]

For energy, compare realistic domestic and propulsion demand with seasonal generation,
usable storage, charging access and acceptable travel patterns. For ecological claims,
include hull upkeep, heating, sewage, embodied replacements and ecosystem effects.
Do not provide a guessed battery wiring diagram or range estimate for an unspecified boat.

## Public examples and transferable mechanisms

These projects are **relevant analogues**, not verified self-identifications as solarpunk.

| Public reference | What is supported | Mechanism to borrow | Limits to investigate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repair Café, originating in the Netherlands [S10] | Organized shared repair and learning | Skill exchange and keeping objects useful | Local participation, repair outcomes, safety and continuity |
| Barefoot College International [S15] | Describes women-centered solar training and rural electrification | Local technical capability and service ownership | Provider-reported; verify present program, financing, maintenance and outcomes |
| Groundswell International [S16] | Describes farmer-led agroecological innovation and exchange | Distributed experimentation grounded in growers' knowledge | Context, labor, tenure and independent outcome evidence |
| Community solar, US DOE [S09] | Describes shared generation and subscriber participation | Access without private roof ownership | Local tariffs, ownership, consumer protections and availability |
| Camden electric moorings, London [S27] | Council reports a completed electric-mooring project | Shared infrastructure enabling cleaner everyday operation | Council report; no independent long-term performance or transfer cost established |
| IWA electric boating guidance [S24] | Describes battery-electric propulsion and multiple charging sources | Converting useful vessels and making charging practical | Guidance is not a verified retrofit, emissions result or design approval |

The range of examples matters more than finding the single most spectacular success.
An ordinary repair institution may be more transferable than a costly demonstration home.

## Research a case before recommending replication

Record project name and location, primary source, date/status, original purpose, claimed
and measured results, governance/ownership, capital and ongoing costs if known, who benefits,
maintenance and failure evidence. Ask which mechanism could transfer and which local
conditions cannot. Identify missing independent evidence rather than turn advocacy into
proof. Do not infer that the whole locality is sustainable from one good component.

When a user shares an image or video, describe what is visible and ask for a source only
if identifying or validating the actual project matters. Unverified inspiration can still
be useful when clearly separated from an evidence-backed case study.
