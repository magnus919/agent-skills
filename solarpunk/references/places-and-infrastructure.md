# Places and infrastructure

## Start with the existing place

Map people, tenure, buildings, paths, utilities, drainage, habitat, services, governance,
and informal uses before designing a future view. Record which observations are supplied,
verified, assumed, or missing. Residents and workers are participants in the design, not
figures added to a rendering after decisions are made.

IPCC identifies connected strategies involving urban form, energy systems and green/blue
infrastructure. NACTO supplies concrete street-design references, primarily from a North
American context. Neither makes a single neighborhood template universally appropriate.
[S08, S11] Use local standards and locally accountable professionals for implementation.

## Work across scales

| Scale | Questions | Useful deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| Room or home | Comfort, light, damp, storage, access, energy demand? | Existing-condition sketch and ranked options |
| Building | Retrofit potential, shared facilities, tenant protections, maintenance? | Options with affected parties and approvals |
| Street | Shade, crossings, mobility aids, deliveries, transit, drainage? | Access-first concept and trial evaluation plan |
| Neighborhood | Housing affordability, daily services, public space and governance? | Service map, displacement risks and institutional pathway |
| Town/rural region | Long distances, watershed, productive land, transport and service access? | Network rather than dense-city copy |
| Coastal/river settlement | Flooding, erosion, water quality, mooring and livelihoods? | Place-specific adaptation questions and specialist scope |

## Climate-responsive form

Analyze overheating, cold, humidity, solar exposure, rain, wind, flood and future climate
before selecting glass, plants, insulation or ventilation. Solar gain is useful in some
conditions and a liability in others. UNFCCC's passive-building overview explicitly
recognizes heating/cooling tradeoffs; CSE offers India-based thermal-comfort resources.
[S13–S14] These are starting references, not a design calculation.

Avoid universal advice such as opening windows when outside air is hotter or polluted,
adding heavy planters to unknown roofs, or making all homes glasshouses. Identify thermal
comfort and health requirements before reducing mechanical systems. Compare both embodied
and operating impacts where evidence permits; preserving a shell can be valuable without
making every retrofit automatically preferable.

## Public space is an institution

For a proposed garden street, include the route a wheelchair user takes, delivery and
emergency access, water source, ownership, maintenance funding, seating and toilets where
relevant, and how objections are addressed. Attractive green upgrades can raise costs or
exclude existing users; assess tenure and affordability rather than claim that planting
causes equitable outcomes. Pair physical improvements with locally workable protections.

Where street trials are appropriate, define authority, accessible alternatives, baseline
observations, a reversible layout, review date, and success/harm indicators. Read tactical practice for sanctioned and unsanctioned approaches. Discuss them accurately;
do not imply the agent is authorized to change a street because it drafted a concept.

## Digital infrastructure

Consider public-interest digital systems when useful, including locally maintainable tools,
open interfaces, accessible information and offline alternatives. A sensor network is not
a substitute for community authority. UN-Habitat's people-centered framing is relevant,
but the retrieved page was search-excerpt only; consult the full playbook before attributing
specific practices to it. [S18]

Evaluate data collection purpose, consent, retention, security, procurement lock-in, and
who can contest a decision. Sometimes a noticeboard and a staffed desk deliver more value
than an app. Describe technical services as choices with operating costs, not magic backends.

## Completion

Deliver a concept matched to the requested scale, its assumptions and dependencies, a
community participation route, and the next feasibility question. Clearly label proposals
as proposals. A photorealistic rendering establishes neither planning permission nor physical viability.
