# Story and world: hope with consequences

## Start with dramatic desire

Ask what a character wants today, who wants something incompatible, what decision cannot
be postponed, and what changes because of it. Politics and ecology should affect the
choices and relationships. Avoid opening with an encyclopedic description of how perfect
the society is unless that form is deliberately requested.

Solarpunk can support mystery, romance, comedy, adventure, poetry, horror, children's
fiction, slice of life, and experimental forms. The useful distinction is the story's
relationship to possibility and agency, not a ban on every frightening event. A current
magazine's horror and accessible-futures calls demonstrate editorial breadth; verify any
submission policy anew before advising a writer. [S04]

## Worldbuilding through service and care

Answer only the questions needed by the story:

- How do people get water, food, shelter, mobility, communication and care?
- Who decides, who owns, who repairs, who teaches, and who can object?
- What happens in winter, drought, equipment failure, conflict, or illness?
- Who used to be excluded, who still is, and what can they change?
- What goods arrive from elsewhere, and what obligations accompany those imports?
- How do people celebrate, flirt, mourn, worship, play, and enjoy solitude?

Use the material answers as sources of events and sensory detail. Worldbuilding should
leave room for desire, surprise, and ambiguity. More-than-human perspectives can change
what counts as a conflict or resolution; *Multispecies Cities* offers an Asia-Pacific
reading lead without establishing that every story shares one politics or technique. [S05]

## Conflict engines

| Engine | Stakes | Hope can be earned through |
|---|---|---|
| A repair needs a scarce part | The last ferry trip matters to someone | Sharing expertise and changing the service arrangement |
| An accessible route disturbs habitat | Two legitimate needs collide | Co-design, a new route, a revisable compromise |
| A cooperative inherits a debt | Ownership alone did not remove scarcity | Renegotiation and accountable decisions |
| A cultural tradition conflicts with a new design | Belonging and authority | Listening, consent, adaptation, or principled refusal |
| A heatwave reveals an exclusion | Safety and trust | Affected people shaping the response |
| Restoration conceals a land grab | Who remains in the neighborhood | Collective defense and enforceable rights |

Do not resolve every disagreement with one benevolent inventor, effortless automation,
unpaid invisible labor, or a speech that magically changes all opponents' minds. Antagonists
may have understandable reasons. Better institutions can still fail and learn.

## Technology and plausibility levels

Agree on near-term realism, extrapolation, or deliberately fantastic invention. A fairy-tale
solar sail need not meet an engineering budget; a supposedly buildable neighborhood does.
Name the level when the distinction matters. Keep continuity across resource limitations,
character knowledge, geography, and institutional power.

If the user requests hard science, research the consequential mechanism rather than
confidently inventing efficiency. If the user requests lyrical fiction, let metaphor work;
do not interrupt the scene with caveats that belong in a short author note.

## Revision pass

Check agency, specific stakes, emotional movement, sensory texture, point of view, and
whether the ending has been earned. Then inspect the system: who maintained the apparent
abundance, whose loss was ignored, and whether disability or cultural difference was
conveniently erased. Favor a specific changed relationship over a generic declaration of hope.

For games, put the dilemma in choices and consequences: repair versus replacement, time
versus reliability, governance versus speed, private control versus shared obligation.
Allow multiple viable paths and negotiated outcomes. Do not award moral points for buying
the most expensive green object. For poetry, choose a material image and lived relationship;
political explanation need not replace the poem.
