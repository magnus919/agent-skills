# Everyday pathways: achievable now

## Begin with life as it is

Establish housing tenure, climate, disposable money, time, mobility and care constraints,
access to services, and the change the person actually wants. If unknown, offer a short
adaptable menu rather than assume a homeowner with a garden. Solarpunk is not a purity
competition or a requirement to leave cities, modern medicine, electricity, or paid work.

Assess services and well-being alongside possessions. IPCC's demand-side framing examines
social arrangements, infrastructure and technology together; it does not make personal
consumption the only lever. [S07] The following pathway is this skill's practical synthesis.

## Choose one small useful experiment

| Situation | First action | What to learn |
|---|---|---|
| Renter with little money | Mend one object, share a rarely used tool, or map a trusted repair service | Cost avoided, usefulness, accessibility |
| Flat with no outdoor space | Try a shared meal, repair circle, library resource or existing local group | Enjoyment, time burden, who can participate |
| Heat discomfort | Identify when/where heat enters; investigate permitted shading and a local cooling resource | Comfort under comparable conditions; unresolved safety needs |
| Limited transport options | Map one essential trip and test a realistic shared or public alternative | Reliability, cost, access, travel time |
| Food waste | Observe one week's avoidable waste and adjust buying/storage where safe | Money and food saved without extra burden |
| Interest in clean power | Check local shared-energy options and consumer terms | Eligibility, actual net cost, cancellation and who owns the asset |
| Rural distance and weak services | Combine trips or explore a shared repair/transport arrangement with willing neighbors | Reliability and equitable access rather than urban density |

Keep the plan voluntary and small enough to stop. Existing community knowledge often
beats a new platform. Repair Café is one public model for shared repair and learning; check
local availability instead of promising there is a nearby branch. [S10]

## A flexible horizon

**Now:** observe a problem, use what is present, try one reversible action.
**Next month:** review usefulness and effort, recruit willing collaborators if appropriate,
and remove barriers to access. Joining a functioning project may be better than founding one.
**Longer term:** pursue an institutional improvement such as a tenant retrofit agreement,
accessible transport service, shared workshop, or accountable energy project.

These are pacing choices, not a deadline for transforming a life. Offer a short version
for a tired person and a deeper version for someone ready to organize.

## Spending and environmental claims

Compare repair, borrowing, service access, and reduced demand before replacement. A new
purchase may be justified by function, health, access, or efficiency; longevity alone does
not make an unsafe object worth keeping. Avoid declaring any material universally green.
For consequential purchases, compare total cost, maintenance, expected lifetime, supply
chain and disposal, and a realistic baseline. Verify current pricing locally.

US community solar is an example of shared access, not a global entitlement. Check local
billing rules, contract protections, utility allowances where relevant, and the difference
between subscribed generation and power physically available during an outage. [S09]

## Care, disability and agency

Do not remove assistive technology, cooling, refrigeration, or necessary transport to meet
an aesthetic ideal. Account for fatigue, sensory access, caregiving, language and digital
exclusion. A low-energy activity can still be labor-intensive. Who has time to tend the
plants and how are they supported? Do not replace professional mental-health support with
optimism or diagnose climate distress; meet an expressed need with care and practical options.

Finish with one chosen action, approximate resources, an owner, a review point, and what
would justify adapting or stopping it. Claims about savings remain hypotheses until measured.
