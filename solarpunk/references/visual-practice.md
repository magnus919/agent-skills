# Visual practice: make the future tangible

## Build a scene before adding motifs

Identify subject, action, place, climate/season, social scale, medium, composition, and
mood. Then select a few visible mechanisms that explain how the space works. A bicycle
repair courtyard with shade and shared tools can communicate more than a skyline covered
in arbitrary vines. A purely decorative commission may legitimately prioritize atmosphere;
do not force it to prove an infrastructure thesis.

The open aesthetics discussed by Flynn and Springett are useful permission to vary the
look. Handwork, adaptation, color, graceful ornament, visible repair, and advanced systems
can coexist. Translate influences into describable choices rather than requiring imitation
of a named artist. [S02–S03]

## Prompt construction

Use this flexible sequence:

1. **Subject and event:** who/what is present and what is happening?
2. **Material continuity:** what earlier structure, craft, or object is being retained?
3. **Ecological logic:** what locally plausible shade, water, planting, or energy detail matters?
4. **Social use:** where can people gather, work, rest, pass through, or take ownership?
5. **Visual craft:** viewpoint, framing, light, palette, texture, medium, and atmosphere.
6. **Constraints:** preserve supplied identity/assets, exact requested copy, aspect ratio,
   access routes, and exclusions. Describe tiny labels as graphic shapes unless readable
   text is actually requested. Add alt text when useful.

For an edit, distinguish elements to preserve from changes to make and pass the actual
reference to the image tool. After generation, inspect requested details, identity/copy,
spatial plausibility, and unintended stereotypes. State if image inspection is unavailable.

## Climate and place variations

| Setting | Useful visual decisions | Avoid assuming |
|---|---|---|
| Hot and dry | Deep shade, repaired courtyards, sparse climate-suited planting, water stewardship | Lush irrigated towers prove sustainability |
| Hot and humid | Shaded circulation, ventilated forms, rain protection, maintenance access | Enclosed glass rooms stay comfortable unaided |
| Cold or high latitude | Insulated envelopes, compact sheltered routes, seasonal gardens, warm shared interiors | Endless sunny summer or dependence on solar alone |
| Dense apartment district | Retrofitted balconies, shared laundry, public transport, accessible ground-level space | Everyone owns a detached house or roof |
| Rural landscape | Repair sheds, community transport, locally governed energy, habitat mosaics | Cities are the only sites of innovation |
| Waterfront reuse | Retained riveted steel, protected access, modest planters, daylight and weathering | A vessel is a static building with unlimited deck loading |

These are creative directions; an illustration is not a thermal, ecological, or structural assessment.

## Worked prompt: converted barge

Illustrate a century-old industrial barge reimagined as a lived-in canal home. Preserve the
weathered steel hull and working-boat proportions. Low roof-mounted solar panels follow
the cabin line; a quiet electric drive is suggested by the compact helm and gently disturbed
water. Contained planting frames the deck without blocking access. Large cabin windows
reveal a bright interior with climbing plants and repaired timber furniture. Two residents
share tea beside tools from an unfinished repair. Use soft overcast light, water reflections,
muted rust and green, and intimate documentary framing. Keep safety rails and circulation
legible. This is an illustrative concept, not a depiction of a verified named vessel.

## Fashion, objects, interiors and moving images

Clothing can express repair, comfort, skilled making, modularity and personal identity.
Specify fabric behavior and useful details; do not prescribe sackcloth, compulsory rustic
costume, or a new shopping list. Reused objects can be elegant and technologically capable.
For interiors, show storage, ventilation, routes, acoustic comfort, and actual inhabitants.

For film, specify one scene's action and duration, camera movement, sound, and continuity.
Let joy and care show in gestures, repairs, and relationships. A community power room need
not resemble either a sterile showroom or a magical jungle. Use the host's media tools for
production; this guide supplies direction rather than a tool runbook.
