# Living systems: trace the flows

Treat water, food, energy, habitat and materials as interacting systems. For each,
identify source, use, storage, losses, waste, ownership, maintenance and seasonal limits.
Do not claim a closed loop while hiding imports, waste exports or unpaid work.

## Food and growing

FAO's agroecology framework includes ecological features alongside knowledge, governance,
culture and solidarity economies. It supplies a broader frame than growing vegetables
as decoration. [S12] Groundswell describes farmer-led experimentation and knowledge
exchange; its account is practice evidence from an interested organization. [S16]

For a garden or food project, investigate sunlight, water, soil/contaminants, climate,
land access, labor, crop preferences, storage, distribution and habitat. Ask local extension,
agroecology organizations or knowledgeable growers for region-specific planting guidance.
Use local names and research invasive species and biosecurity before recommending plants.
Do not infer food safety from a lush appearance, or that an urban garden can feed a whole
city. Distinguish supplementary production, livelihood, education, pleasure and food security.

## Water and sanitation

Start with the catchment and intended use. Rain capture, infiltration, greywater and reuse
have different treatment, ecological and legal implications. A diagram with arrows does
not demonstrate potable quality or safe discharge. Research local water authority and
public-health requirements before advice on actual plumbing or food irrigation. Design
for drought, overflow, vector control, maintenance and downstream users, not just average rain.

For fiction or images, plausible visible water paths enrich the scene; do not present those
paths as buildable specifications. Where evidence is missing, identify the design question.

## Energy and useful services

Reduce unnecessary demand while preserving needed services, then compare supply, storage,
controls, backup and maintenance. Distinguish power (kW) from energy (kWh). A useful rough
budget is `daily energy = sum(device power × hours used)` with consistent units. Compare
seasonal generation and losses, not just a sunny-day panel rating. Label all example
values illustrative; a rough budget is not installation design.

Community solar can widen participation where available. Grid-connected solar does not
by itself guarantee outage power; island-capable controls and the rest of the system matter.
Use current local grid and installer requirements. [S09, S23] Resilience includes repairs,
spares, skills, equitable access and dependable service, not only self-sufficiency.

For boats, investigate propulsion separately from domestic loads. Heating and winter
conditions can dominate a liveaboard budget; autonomy cannot be inferred from roof coverage.
Use the adaptive-reuse reference for marine constraints.

## Habitat and more-than-human life

Identify existing habitat before adding visible greenery. Prioritize locally appropriate
species, connectivity, soil, water and long-term care. Consider night lighting, nesting,
barriers, pets and disturbance. EPA's tree/vegetation page supports cooling as a general
mechanism, not a guaranteed local temperature reduction or biodiversity outcome. [S17]

A tree, wetland or garden has ecological relationships and maintenance needs. Compare
habitat measures with water demand and fire, pest or access constraints; do not use
biodiversity as a reason to exclude people without examining alternatives.

## Materials and waste

Inventory assets and failure modes. Try maintenance, repair, adaptation and sharing where
safe and useful; compare replacement when durability, efficiency, accessibility or hazard
warrants it. Account for batteries, electronics, fertilizers, replacement parts and end of
life. Reclaimed materials need assessment for contamination and suitability. Repair Café
illustrates shared practical skill, not universal repairability. [S10]

Finish with a simple flow map and its largest unknowns. Avoid unsupported universal claims
about carbon neutrality, zero waste, regenerative performance, or infinite abundance.
