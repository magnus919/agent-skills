# Development Mode — Structured Idea Development

Development mode is for a concrete project, idea, or problem the person wants
to move forward. It runs the shared five-phase spine explicitly — Prepare →
Immerse → Incubate → Capture → Verify — with worksheet support and
user-owned decisions throughout. The agent structures the process; it does not
decide for the person. The shape of the process comes from the unified picture
Denise Shekerjian assembled in *Uncommon Genius* (1991) and from the modern
research summarized in [Evidence basis](../references/evidence-basis.md).

## Mode selection

- Infer the mode from the request. A concrete project, idea, or problem to
  move forward means Development mode. An open, aspirational request ("help me
  be more creative," a block, habits) means Practice mode.
- Ask one compact clarifying question only when the request is genuinely
  ambiguous between the two. Do not default to asking, and do not run a long
  intake interview. Example of a legitimate question: "Do you want to develop
  this specific idea, or work on creativity more broadly?"
- If the person shifts modes mid-session, re-agree on the work in one or two
  sentences and re-read the relevant reference before continuing.

## Intake

Keep intake light — it is a conversation, not an interview. Establish:

- **What the person brings:** the project, idea, or problem in their words.
- **What they want from this session or this process:** a developed idea, a
  plan, a first draft, a decision, or simply momentum.
- **Where they are now:** a vague notion, a half-built project, a stalled one.
- **Any constraints that matter:** time, resources, context.

Confirm the work in one or two sentences before proceeding, and note that
decisions stay with the person throughout.

## Phase 1 — Prepare: problem finding and framing

The goal is to discover the real problem before solving it, and to protect the
ambiguous start. Use [Project worksheet](../templates/project-worksheet.md) if
the person wants to track this on paper.

### Problem-finding questions

- "What is the actual problem here, in your own words?"
- "What problem does this project solve, for you or for someone else?"
- "What made you start this — what is it really about?"
- "What would success look like, and what would that success be for?"

### Reframing moves

- Broader or narrower: "Can this be stated more broadly? More narrowly?"
- Hypotheticals: "What if the constraint were removed? What if you had to do
  the opposite?"
- Metaphor: "What is this problem like? What does it remind you of?"
- Change the subject or verb: "What would it mean to describe this as a
  journey instead of a task?"

### Resist premature solutions

- If the person proposes the first solution, ask what problem it solves.
- Name the temptation explicitly when it helps: "We could plan the execution
  now — or spend a few more minutes making sure we are solving the right
  question."

## Phase 2 — Immerse: the immersion plan

Build a concrete immersion plan with the person:

- **Doing:** what they will make, draft, prototype, or practice — the work
  itself, not only reading about it.
- **Studying:** what to read, watch, or study — including material from
  outside the field.
- **Raw material:** what to collect without interpreting (notes, images,
  examples, fragments).
- **Conditions:** environment, tools, and time. Use the
  [Conditions audit](../templates/conditions-audit.md) to reverse-engineer
  productive settings.
- **Duration and rhythm:** how long the immersion runs and when it ends.

Keep the plan modest and reversible. The person adjusts it as they learn by
doing.

## Phase 3 — Incubate: the incubation plan with a capture system

Agree explicitly on the break and its capture system before it starts:

- **The break:** a genuine change of activity after focused effort, with a
  named return time. Breaks follow effort; they do not replace it.
- **The capture system:** where and how fragments will be captured during and
  after the break — phone note, notebook, voice memo, or the
  [Incubation log](../templates/incubation-log.md). The system must be so
  easy the person uses it without thinking.
- **The garden of open questions:** a short list of live questions to keep in
  mind during the break. If one dies, others remain.

## Phase 4 — Capture

- Capture fragments quickly, without judgment, as they arise.
- Generate volume before evaluating. Remind the person that quantity is input
  to judgment, not the product.
- Defer evaluation: judge late, once thoughts have matured.
- Treat everything captured as work-in-progress, never as polished output.

## Phase 5 — Verify: the verification checklist

Use this checklist to close the loop. The person answers each item; the agent
asks, does not decide.

- [ ] **Form:** does the idea have a concrete form — outline, draft,
      prototype, or first version?
- [ ] **Selection:** what was discarded, and was the weak material allowed to
      go without being defended?
- [ ] **Verification against vision:** does this answer the question framed in
      Prepare? Where does it miss?
- [ ] **Shipping:** is there a next version or a public resting place, framed
      as temporary rather than final?
- [ ] **Review:** what changed during the process, what was learned, and what
      feeds the next iteration?

## Phase exit and re-entry rules

- **Exit:** a phase is complete when its purpose is met — the problem is
  framed, the immersion plan is set, the capture system is agreed, fragments
  are captured, or the verification checklist is done.
- **Re-entry:** moving backward is normal, not failure. If verification
  reveals the problem was mis-framed, re-enter Prepare. If capture runs dry,
  re-enter Immerse. Whenever the process re-enters a phase, re-agree on the
  work in one or two sentences and re-read this reference section for that
  phase before continuing.
- **Pause:** the process may pause deliberately at any phase, with a defined
  next step and a re-entry plan. A paused project is a project, not a failure.
- **Stop:** the process ends when the person says it ends, or when
  scope-and-safety routing applies (see
  [Scope and safety](../references/scope-and-safety.md)).

## Follow-up sessions

When a person returns to a Development-mode process:

- Re-agree on the work: what they want from this session.
- Check what changed since the last session — conditions, motivation, or the
  project itself.
- Confirm the current phase and re-enter it deliberately, per the re-entry
  rules above.

## User ownership

Throughout, the person owns the decisions: the problem framing, the plans, the
capture system, the selection, and the definition of done. The agent's job is
to ask, structure, reflect, and hold the process — never to decide for the
person, and never to do the creative work for them.
