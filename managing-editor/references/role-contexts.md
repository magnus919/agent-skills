# Role map by publishing context

The managing-editor title is not standardized. Use local job descriptions, contracts, policy, and the named editor's instructions to set authority. The common thread is coordinating people, deadlines, handoffs, and quality around a publication. The table gives patterns, not universal job requirements.

| Context | Common managing-editor work | Decisions often owned elsewhere | Extra controls to map |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Newsroom / wire service** | Run daily or shift workflows, assign and track stories, coordinate desks and formats, protect verification and correction processes, surface developing risks, and keep production moving. Some organizations give the managing editor broad newsroom authority. | Publisher/editor-in-chief may set coverage priorities; reporters and editors own reporting; legal counsel advises on legal exposure. | Accuracy, attribution, source protection, conflicts, fairness, sensitive imagery, embargoes, correction/withdrawal path. |
| **Magazine / periodical** | Maintain issue or channel calendar, commission and traffic work, manage word counts/assets/deadlines, coordinate freelance writers, copy desk, art, and production. | Editor-in-chief or section editor sets editorial direction and accepts work; art director owns design system; business/publisher owns budget. | Issue lock, flatplan, rights/releases, art captions/credits, proof rounds, print lead times. |
| **Books** | Coordinate manuscript-to-publication schedule, instructions and handoffs among acquiring editor, author, copyeditor, designer, proofreader, indexer, printer, marketing/publicity; track production costs and advance copies where assigned. | Acquiring editor and author shape the manuscript; publisher/imprint owns acquisition, budget and positioning; production specialist may own manufacturing or digital QA. | Word count and delivery, permissions, illustrations, jacket, index, page proofs, e-book/print variants, on-sale date. |
| **Academic / scientific journal** | Administer submissions, scope checks, peer-review invitations and deadlines, revision flow, author communication, house style, accepted-manuscript readiness, and production handoff. Smaller journals may combine several of these tasks in one person. | Handling editor, editor-in-chief, or board typically owns scholarly judgment and accept/reject decisions; reviewer advises; publisher production staff may own typesetting/release. Confirm locally. | Reviewer confidentiality/blinding, conflicts, research ethics, consent, authorship, appeals/complaints, corrections/retractions, version of record. |
| **Digital / organizational content** | Turn a strategy into a workable editorial queue and calendar; assign and brief; coordinate subject experts, brand/legal/product reviewers, editors, design, SEO/accessibility and CMS handoffs; track performance and updates. | Strategy lead owns goals and audience; SME owns technical sign-off; legal/compliance owns formal clearance; product owner or publisher may own release. | Claims, legal/regulatory review, brand, metadata, accessibility, localization, analytics, search, stale-content maintenance. |
| **Nonprofit / association / volunteer team** | Coordinate member or volunteer contributions, publication cycles, limited capacity, board/editorial committee reviews, and member communications. Often one person carries both editorial and operational duties. | Board sets organizational policy and delegated approvals; program experts own subject review; publisher/ED may approve public positions. | Mission fit, consent, representation, volunteer availability, sensitive member information, funding/advertising separation. |

## Name the decision rights

For each recurring decision, write one person or role for each needed action:

- **Propose:** supplies an option or draft.
- **Own:** coordinates the work and resolves routine workflow issues.
- **Decide:** chooses editorial direction or disposition.
- **Approve:** provides required subject, legal, brand, policy, or release clearance.
- **Execute:** makes the approved change or publishes it.

Some people hold more than one role. Do not infer that a managing editor can override an editor-in-chief, author agreement, research-integrity process, legal counsel, or publication policy just because they manage the schedule. Likewise, do not let a decision owner make a hidden scope change without revising the brief, schedule, and workload.

## Editorial independence and service

The managing editor is often the operational point of contact for contributors and the person who makes process understandable. In scholarly publishing, this may mean advocating for clear status and fair process while remaining impartial about editorial outcomes. In organizational publishing, clarify how commercial, executive, advocacy, or member interests interact with editorial policy. Record conflicts and the governing rule; never silently change a conclusion to satisfy a stakeholder.
