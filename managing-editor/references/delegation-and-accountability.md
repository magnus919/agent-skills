# Delegation and editorial accountability

Use this guide when several contributors or subagents will touch a piece. Parallel work is useful for independent tasks; it creates risk when roles overlap, sources are shared without context, or reviewers mistake agreement for verification.

Check the publication's current AI policy before using generative systems on manuscripts or reviews. In scholarly peer review, confidentiality restrictions may prohibit uploading any part of a submission to an external AI service, even for a bounded critique; disclosure rules for authors, editors, and reviewers also differ by journal. Obtain policy clearance before delegation, and keep author/reviewer identity and confidential material out of task packets unless the system is approved for that use.

## Staged subagent workflow

1. **Editor defines the assignment.** Freeze a short brief with audience, question, format, scope, standards, deadline, decision owner, sensitive-data rules, and publication restrictions.
2. **Research task (if needed).** Ask a research agent for a source log and claim-evidence map, not an unsupported prose answer. Require source URLs, dates, direct evidence excerpts or locations, source type, limitations, conflicts, and open questions. Do not have the researcher draft from inaccessible or unlogged claims.
3. **Draft task.** Give a writer the approved brief and accepted evidence packet. Require claims that are not established to be marked, source links beside relevant claims, and unresolved questions grouped for the editor.
4. **Independent challenge.** Give a separate reviewer the brief and draft plus the evidence packet. Ask it to find unsupported claims, counterevidence, omissions, contradictions, quote drift, scope mismatch, and ambiguity. Do not ask only whether the piece is good. If the reviewer did not inspect the source evidence, label its work a text-only review.
5. **Human editorial resolution.** The accountable editor checks high-impact claims against evidence, resolves reviewer comments, consults specialists where needed, and documents accepted risks or outstanding uncertainty. The drafter cannot self-approve a consequential claim solely by asserting it is checked.
6. **Copy and production tasks.** Route meaning-preserving edits and specific production checks to qualified contributors or agents. Provide the current version and style rules. Require change notes for meaning-affecting edits. A named human checks rendered output and controls final release.
7. **Close the loop.** Send a compact handoff or rejection note: disposition, what changed, what remains unresolved, next owner/date, and where the current approved version lives.

For urgent or short work, collapse stages only when the reduced check still matches the risk. Record which independent check was omitted and who accepted the risk. Never fabricate a second reviewer or represent model-generated agreement as independent human verification.

## Delegation packet

Use `templates/delegation-packet.md`. Every packet should contain one task, one deliverable, explicit boundaries, inspectable acceptance criteria, an owner, a deadline, and an escalation path. Split large tasks by work product and dependency, not by arbitrary word count.

## Handling conflicts and changes

- **Conflicting edits:** compare each proposal to the assignment, evidence, style rule, and intended reader. Ask the authorized decision owner to resolve genuine editorial disagreements; do not merge incompatible suggestions by intuition.
- **New fact or scope change:** update the evidence record and brief, identify downstream work, re-estimate effort, and get the proper approval before treating the old deadline as intact.
- **Missed deadline:** ask for a recovery estimate, identify what's blocked, and offer scope/date/capacity choices. Do not pressure a contributor to claim work is complete when required checks are outstanding.
- **Agent uncertainty or tool failure:** ask for the partial evidence and exact failure state. Resume from the last verified artifact; never treat a timeout, inaccessible source, or empty result as evidence that no issue exists.
- **Outside contact or publication:** treat as a separate external action. The task packet must state whether it is authorized; otherwise stop at a draft or recommendation and route it to a human.

## Audit trail

For significant work retain the approved brief, source/evidence record, meaningful version changes, decision and approval names/roles, open-risk disposition, and final released version. Follow the publication's retention and access rules. Do not copy confidential material into a general-purpose tracker when a restricted record is appropriate.
