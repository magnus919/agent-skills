# Workflow and quality methods

Use the lightest process that protects the publication's promises. The managing editor should make work visible and predictable, not add ceremony for its own sake.

## Portfolio planning

Maintain a calendar or queue that answers: what is being made, for which audience/channel, who owns it, what stage it is in, what is due next, what blocks it, and what decision is pending. Show dates for important stages, not only publication. Include workload/capacity and dependencies; a list of dates without owners or feasibility is not a plan. A useful default is one active next action per item and a visible limit on work in progress.

Plan recurring publication slots first, then reserve a realistic share for timely requests, corrections, and unplanned work. When an urgent item enters, make the displaced work visible and obtain the appropriate date/scope decision. Reforecast from actual review times rather than repeatedly promising the same impossible date.

## Intake and triage

Assess each request against the publication's stated mission and audience, value, timing, evidence and source readiness, effort/capacity, dependencies, sensitivity, and risk. Do not convert a numeric score into an automatic approval. Triage outcomes should be one of: accept, request clarification, defer, decline, or escalate. Tell the requester what happens next and who owns the next step.

## Editing passes

Use distinct passes when they reduce missed issues:

1. **Developmental/substantive:** Does the piece answer its assignment for this audience? Are structure, reasoning, scope, voice, and evidence fit for purpose?
2. **Verification:** Can consequential factual claims be traced to adequate, independent evidence? Are quotes, numbers, names, dates, links, and captions faithful? What remains unverified or contested?
3. **Copyedit/style:** Is the language clear, consistent, and aligned with the applicable style guide without changing meaning? Are inclusive language, terminology, citations, units, and references consistent?
4. **Proof and release QA:** Does the final rendered output match the approved version? Do links, headings, tables, figures, captions, bylines, metadata, navigation, alt text, and formats work as intended?

For high-stakes material, assign verification to a person other than the drafter when feasible. The need for independence depends on risk and resources; record limits when the same person must perform multiple passes. Peer review is a separate scholarly judgment workflow and must follow the journal's policy; copyediting cannot substitute for it.

## Risk-based gates

Set a gate when proceeding without a check could create a material factual, ethical, legal, privacy, safety, rights, accessibility, reputational, or record-integrity problem. For each gate, specify:

- evidence or artifact needed to pass;
- the role that reviews it and the role that decides;
- how to handle a missing or conflicting result;
- whether a documented exception is permitted and who can authorize it.

Accessibility is a content and production concern, not just a final engineering check. For web and digital publishing, consider meaningful link text, heading structure, plain language, image alternatives, captions/transcripts, and the publication's applicable accessibility standard. Alt text should convey an image's information or function in context; decorative images may need empty alternatives. Ask an accessibility specialist for complex or high-impact material. Do not treat automated scans as a complete accessibility review.

Examples: verify quotes and claims before publication; clear reproduced or adapted assets before layout; preserve reviewer blinding; obtain specialist review for regulated claims; compare a live page to the approved master. Do not use a green checklist as proof of review if no one inspected the underlying evidence.

## Accuracy and evidence

Keep a claim ledger for work with material factual claims. Link each claim to the exact supporting passage or data, source owner/date, verification action, caveat, and status. Distinguish primary records, firsthand observation, expert interpretation, secondary reporting, and unattributed assertion. Seek disconfirming evidence and relevant response; do not force consensus when sources conflict. Check quotations against the record and do not silently rewrite a speaker's meaning. Keep an unverified claim labeled, narrowed, attributed, removed, or escalated.

## Correct, update, or withdraw

Use the publication's correction policy. Log the item/version, what was wrong, when discovered, affected outputs, decision owner, correction wording, update time, notification/distribution action, and follow-up. Correct the public record clearly and promptly under the applicable policy; preserve the prior state and history where required. Do not disguise a material correction as a silent edit. Escalate before publication if the evidence no longer supports a central claim.

## Learn from flow data

Track only measures that guide action: time by stage, time waiting for review, work returned for missing brief/evidence, revision cycles, missed handoffs, corrections, and workload by role. Use trends to find bottlenecks and test process changes. Do not use a fast cycle time, output volume, or on-time rate as a proxy for quality. Avoid individual productivity rankings without context.

## Distinguish similar roles

- A **content strategist** determines why, for whom, and what portfolio of content matters; the managing editor makes that strategy executable and maintains its operation.
- An **editor-in-chief / handling editor** often owns direction and scholarly or editorial decisions; the managing editor coordinates the process and makes authority legible.
- A **copyeditor / proofreader** performs defined language or final proof checks; the managing editor assigns, routes, and verifies the required checks.
- A **production editor/coordinator** may own composition, print, CMS release, or platform QA; map the handoff rather than assume it is part of the managing-editor job.

These are common distinctions, not fixed titles. Confirm the local assignment before routing work.
