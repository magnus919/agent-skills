# Sources and further reading

Accessed 2026-11-15. Sources support the role patterns and standards below; they do not establish one universal job description. Publisher pages are primary for that publisher's process, professional codes are primary for their membership or industry principles, and practitioner articles provide situated examples. Recheck living policies before applying them.

## Role and workflow examples

| Source | What it supports | Evidence limits |
|---|---|---|
| Johns Hopkins University Press, [What do journal editors, managing editors, and production coordinators do?](https://ask.up.hcommons.org/what-do-journal-editors-do/) (Jan. 2024) | Shows journal editor, managing editor, and production coordinator as related but distinct roles; describes submission/review coordination, agreements/assets, copyediting, permissions, composition, proofing, and publication. | A university-press explanation; it explicitly says contacts and duties vary by journal and publisher. |
| H-Net, Kurt Milberger, [Working with Your Editor: The Role of a Journals Managing Editor](https://networks.h-net.org/node/1883/discussions/6576783/working-your-editor-role-journals-managing-editor) | Practitioner view of a scholarly managing editor as a communication hub, advocate for process clarity, and day-to-day operator; discusses scope screening, reviewer coordination, style, permissions, and proofing. | A guest contribution from a particular humanities/social-science journal context, not a universal job specification. |
| Taylor & Francis Editor Resources, [Managing peer review](https://editorresources.taylorandfrancis.com/managing-peer-review-process/) | Publisher guidance on initial scope/quality screening, assigning editors/reviewers, checking ethical issues, reviewer delays, and editorial decision authority. | Advice for its journal editors; managing-editor responsibilities depend on the journal's division of labor. |
| Penguin Random House Author News, [Managing editorial: the traffic-control center of book production](https://authornews.penguinrandomhouse.com/qa-with-managing-editorial-the-traffic-control-center-of-the-books-production-process/) | Practitioner account of manuscript-to-book coordination, instructions, design/copyediting handoffs, schedules, costs, advance copies, and interdepartmental communication. | Interview with an executive managing editor at one publishing group; imprints differ. |
| Content Marketing Institute, [Content operations: More method, less madness](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/content-marketing-strategy/want-more-method-and-less-madness-check-your-content-operations/) | Frames content operations as people, process, and technology; discusses translating strategy into a repeatable creation, management, measurement, and optimization operation, with role distinctions. | Industry guidance for content marketing; it is not a journalism or scholarly-publication standard. |
| Journal Editors Resource, [Editorial role descriptions](https://jer.or.id/editorial_call/job_descriptions.php?lang=en) | Offers a practical role matrix distinguishing editor-in-chief, managing editor, associate editor, layout, copyedit, proof, board, and reviewer work in journal operations. | A secondary role summary; confirm individual responsibilities against publisher and journal policies. |

## Standards and quality controls

| Source | What it supports | Evidence limits |
|---|---|---|
| Reuters, [Standards and Values](https://reutersagency.com/about/standards-values/) | Journalism principles: accuracy before speed, fair comment, transparent corrections, balance, conflicts, sourcing, quote fidelity, and source protection. | Reuters newsroom rules; apply the user's publication policy, not Reuters-specific procedures, elsewhere. |
| Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE), [Core practices and code of conduct](https://publicationethics.org/core-practices) | Scholarly-record integrity, editorial independence, transparent business practices, complaints and post-publication corrections, confidentiality, research ethics, and accountability. | COPE membership framework and guidance; follow the journal's adopted policies and jurisdiction. |
| COPE, [Emerging AI dilemmas in scholarly publishing](https://publicationethics.org/topic-discussions/emerging-ai-dilemmas-scholarly-publishing) | Highlights the confidentiality risk of sharing manuscript content with non-proprietary AI tools and the need to follow journal-specific policy. | Topic discussion rather than a universal legal rule; local policies and tool terms control. |
| World Association of Medical Editors (WAME), [Chatbots, Generative AI, and Scholarly Manuscripts](https://wame.org/page3.php?id=106) | Recommends transparent specification of chatbot use in manuscript evaluation, reviews, and editorial correspondence. | Medical-editors' professional recommendations; journal and publisher policies may differ or change. |
| International Committee of Medical Journal Editors, [Recommendations](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/) (updated Jan. 2026) | Current recommendations for conduct, reporting, editing, and publication in medical journals. | Biomedical journal context; not a substitute for an organization's current policy or specialist advice. |
| W3C Web Accessibility Initiative, [Writing for Web Accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tips/writing/) and [Resources on Alternative Text for Images](https://www.w3.org/WAI/alt/) | Practical content guidance for accessible writing and image alternatives. | Apply the current accessibility standard, platform requirements, and specialist review appropriate to the content. |

## Evidence note

The calendar, assignment, handoff, verification, and decision-log templates in this skill are synthesized operational aids. They are not copied from or endorsed by the sources above. AI/subagent-specific controls adapt confidentiality, disclosure, and accountability principles to delegated workflows; the cited editorial sources describe human publishing roles and standards, not validated agent effectiveness. Treat model reviews as assistance, preserve inspectable evidence, and keep accountable human decisions explicit.
