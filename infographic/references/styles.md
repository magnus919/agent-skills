# Visual style recipes

These fourteen treatments are original adaptable design recipes, not a validated
universal taxonomy, executable templates, or promises that any particular tool can
render them. Pair a recipe with an independently chosen semantic layout. None is
restricted to an industry. Select for reader task, tone, medium, and available assets.

For a meaningful choice, show two or three relevant directions with a reason; do
not force a fourteen-option interview. Keep the information architecture constant
when comparing aesthetics so the user can evaluate the actual difference.

Append any recipe prompt to the composition brief: topic, audience, task, layout,
dimensions, verified facts/labels/units, source constraints and intended reading
order. Route exact text and quantitative marks to reliable typesetting/geometry
when needed. No style authorizes invented facts or third-party branding.

## Editorial

**Fit:** Evidence-led explanations and comparisons where the reader should feel oriented quickly.

**Visual grammar:** A dominant headline, aligned evidence columns, thin functional rules and generous margins. Pair a readable serif headline with plain sans labels; charcoal on a light neutral with one deliberate accent. Charts are directly labeled.

**Access and limits:** Keep source notes readable; do not imitate cramped newspaper columns or add ornamental rules. Poor fit for elaborate spatial mechanisms needing a large central image.

**Prompt fragment:** “Use a clear editorial hierarchy, generous whitespace, aligned evidence, and restrained rules; give the main finding the strongest weight.”

## Technical

**Fit:** Explaining components, mechanisms or assembly relationships.

**Visual grammar:** Consistent stroke hierarchy, explicit component boundaries, numbered callouts and labeled connectors. Use a schematic, sectional or exploded view only when it teaches a real relationship. Plain type with monospace reserved for identifiers; quiet backgrounds and a functional accent.

**Access and limits:** Connector distinctions need labels as well as color. Avoid invented dimensions, fake engineering accuracy and decorative blueprint grids. Poor fit when the underlying mechanism is unknown.

**Prompt fragment:** “Draw a disciplined technical plate with consistent line weights and unambiguous labeled connections; distinguish known structure from conceptual simplification.”

## Pictogram

**Fit:** Quick actions, wayfinding and compact reference guides.

**Visual grammar:** Optically consistent icons with plain verb labels, one action per module and generous gaps. Use numbers only for ordered actions. Limit color to functional emphasis on a calm surface.

**Access and limits:** Explain unfamiliar symbols. For repeated-icon data, state the unit and handle fractions accurately. Avoid stretching icon area to represent quantity. Poor fit for nuanced argument without supporting text.

**Prompt fragment:** “Use consistent pictograms and short plain-language labels, with one action per group and explicit ordering only where required.”

## Playful flat

**Fit:** Approachable explanations that benefit from friendly characters or objects.

**Visual grammar:** Simple geometric silhouettes, a limited bright palette, inclusive figures and dark labels on quiet surfaces. Let an explanatory scene anchor the composition; keep supporting text compact and well separated.

**Access and limits:** Provide labels for conceptual colors and keep character detail from competing with facts. Avoid unrelated confetti and trivializing sensitive subjects. Poor fit when playful tone contradicts the audience need.

**Prompt fragment:** “Create an inviting flat illustration whose objects explain the supplied relationships; use clean silhouettes, a coherent bright palette and calm label zones.”

## Paper cut

**Fit:** Tactile conceptual stories, cycles or approachable comparisons.

**Visual grammar:** Layered cut-paper shapes, shallow consistent shadows and occasional edge texture. Use broad silhouettes and clean typeset text in untextured areas. Keep generous space between semantic groups.

**Access and limits:** Keep texture away from labels and quantitative marks. Material thickness must not encode exact values. Poor fit for dense technical charts or micro-labels.

**Prompt fragment:** “Build a cut-paper composition with broad readable shapes and shallow shadows; keep all exact text and data on flat clean surfaces.”

## Field guide

**Fit:** Identifying features, comparing observable objects, or explaining natural/material forms.

**Visual grammar:** Carefully observed isolated drawings with restrained identifying callouts and useful detail insets. Ink/wash or pencil treatment, readable serif headings and plain labels. Compare objects from consistent views with ample margins.

**Access and limits:** Do not invent diagnostic features or measurements. Mark differing scales; pair illustrated features with text descriptions. Poor fit when reference imagery is unreliable or identification could be unsafe.

**Prompt fragment:** “Create an observational field-guide plate using only supplied or verified features, with clear feature callouts and explicit scale differences.”

## Comic

**Fit:** Explaining change, a decision, or an experience through a short sequence.

**Visual grammar:** Ordered panels, one meaningful change per panel, stable subjects and generous gutters. Sparse speech and captions, strong silhouettes and readable plain lettering. Color helps continuity but never carries sequence alone.

**Access and limits:** Provide a panel-by-panel text equivalent. Mark invented dialogue as illustrative; do not fabricate quotes from real people. Poor fit for dense multi-series quantitative comparison.

**Prompt fragment:** “Tell the explanation in clearly ordered panels with one change per panel, consistent subjects and short verified captions; retain the exact stated sequence.”

## Photo annotation

**Fit:** Explaining visible features in authentic supplied photographs.

**Visual grammar:** A large photograph with anchored callouts, purposeful detail crops and solid label surfaces. Quiet sans typography and an accent reserved for pointers. Leave image evidence unobscured.

**Access and limits:** Record provenance and disclose synthetic imagery. Do not substitute stock or generated scenes for event evidence. Before/after camera changes can mislead. Poor fit when authentic visual evidence is absent.

**Prompt fragment:** “Annotate the supplied authentic image with anchored labels and purposeful crops; preserve its evidentiary content and keep text on clear solid surfaces.”

## Geometric poster

**Fit:** One strong takeaway with a small amount of supporting information.

**Visual grammar:** Bold geometric shapes, a dominant short headline, two or three print-like inks and substantial negative space. Use a confident display face with readable plain supporting copy. Texture stays decorative.

**Access and limits:** Qualifications must remain readable and the headline must not exceed the evidence. Avoid long all-caps text. Poor fit for dense exact copy or many equal-priority claims.

**Prompt fragment:** “Compose a bold geometric poster around one evidence-supported takeaway, with large type, limited inks and enough space for readable qualifications.”

## Isometric

**Fit:** Orienting readers within a conceptual system or environment.

**Visual grammar:** Consistent parallel projection across simplified components, clear zones and deliberate connector paths. Plain flat labels, restrained depth and one coherent light source. Use open space to prevent occlusion.

**Access and limits:** Label conceptual placement; keep quantitative comparisons in flat charts. Do not encode quantity by apparent volume or perspective. Poor fit for exact scale diagrams or overlapping complex networks.

**Prompt fragment:** “Show a simplified isometric system with consistent projection, unobscured components and clear labeled flows; place exact data in separate flat marks.”

## Notebook

**Fit:** Informal explanation, brainstorming summaries and approachable learning.

**Visual grammar:** Controlled hand-drawn outlines and marker emphasis, clean typeset body, optional handwritten headings. Spacious groups with a clear reading route; a restrained paper tone and a few purposeful highlights.

**Access and limits:** No cursive body, ambiguous arrow tangles, fake signatures or scribbles over data. Pair highlights with labels. Poor fit where informality undermines required tone or precision.

**Prompt fragment:** “Use a tidy notebook visual language with controlled sketch lines, sparse highlights and clean readable body text; keep the reading path explicit.”

## Learning diagram

**Fit:** Introducing a concept to a reader with limited prior knowledge.

**Visual grammar:** One central concept with progressive explanations, relevant parts and a worked example or misconception when grounded. Large plain type, consistent labeled conceptual colors and clear whitespace between stages of explanation.

**Access and limits:** Respect beginner adults; avoid childish decoration by default. Simplification must not teach a false rule. Poor fit for expert lookup requiring high information density.

**Prompt fragment:** “Build a clear learning diagram around the central concept, with progressive explanations, consistent labels and only grounded examples.”

## Atlas

**Fit:** Geographic relationships, locations, routes or spatial patterns.

**Visual grammar:** Verified geography on a quiet base with a clear thematic overlay hierarchy. Readable direct labels and a nearby legend; scale, orientation, date and source where relevant. Use restrained cartographic color.

**Access and limits:** Provide location/route text equivalents and non-color cues. Do not invent geography or use maps for nonspatial ideas. Poor fit for abstract process without geographic meaning.

**Prompt fragment:** “Use verified geography and restrained cartographic hierarchy; distinguish the thematic information from the base map and include the relevant legend and provenance.”

## Sculpted

**Fit:** Memorable conceptual metaphors using tactile isolated objects.

**Visual grammar:** Matte clay or molded forms, consistent soft lighting and broad silhouettes. Generous space, plain crisp labels on flat planes and a compact palette. The object relationships must carry the explanation.

**Access and limits:** Describe the metaphor in the text equivalent. Object volume cannot encode exact quantity; keep key words off curved surfaces. Poor fit for solemn contexts or precise spatial measurement.

**Prompt fragment:** “Create a tactile matte sculpted metaphor with consistent soft light and clear silhouettes; preserve conceptual relationships and keep all labels flat and crisp.”
