## If there is a power or water outage

1. **Name the problem clearly.** Record whether it is a **power outage**, a
   **water outage**, or both; note the time you noticed it and the affected unit
   or area.
2. **Report it during posted building desk hours.** Give the building desk the
   unit or area, outage type, and time noticed. Keep a copy of what you reported.
3. **If the desk is closed, use only a contact or reporting channel that the
   building has actually posted or given you.** The Maple Court after-hours
   contact is not confirmed in this draft. Do not guess a phone number or assume
   that a repair will be completed within a particular time.
4. **Keep your accessible check-in plan available.** If stairs, elevators, or
   another route are not safe or usable for you, use the check-in method you
   recorded below or ask a trusted person to help you make the report. Do not
   attempt a route that is unsafe for you.

This card does not establish building-specific elevator, evacuation, medical, or
life-safety procedures. Those procedures require confirmation from the building
and, where appropriate, a qualified safety or accessibility contact before this
card is treated as final.

## What an agent can check in this revision

- The resident-facing sections use concrete power/water wording and omit the
  unsafe “use the elevator only when the outage is short” instruction.
- The card names power and water outages, retains English and Spanish coverage,
  and labels the Spanish text as draft pending review.
- No after-hours number, repair guarantee, local service commitment, or building-
  specific safety procedure is presented as verified fact.
- The accessible check-in fields, voluntary-sharing boundary, report record, and
  pilot status are present.
- The change record maps each supplied comment to an accepted, deferred, or
  rejected decision and records the unresolved dependency.

These are document and provenance checks. They do not prove that residents
understand the card, that an after-hours channel works, that the Spanish wording
is locally appropriate, or that the check-in plan is accessible in practice.

## What must be re-tested in a community pilot

Before calling this guide validated, run a small pilot with deliberately varied
participants, including night-shift renters, people who use mobility aids or need
an accessible route, and Spanish readers where relevant. Do not treat the pilot as
a vote by all residents.

Ask participants to complete these tasks without extra explanation:

1. Identify whether the card covers their outage and fill in the minimum report
   record.
2. Describe what they would do if the desk were closed, including where they
   would find a genuinely posted or already-given channel.
3. Complete the accessible check-in plan, or identify the exact missing
   information that prevents completion.
4. Explain which information is private or voluntary to share.

Record the participant group, observed barrier, proposed change, and follow-up
check. Confirm the actual desk hours, after-hours contact/channel, building safety
procedure, and Spanish translation before publishing a final version. Until those
checks occur, this guide is ready for a community pilot but remains a provisional
revision.
