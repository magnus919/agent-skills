# Neighbors Together: discovery draft

## What is known

Neighbors Together wants something short and welcoming for “our neighbors” about
“the problem.” The brief does not identify the place, problem, participating
perspectives, access needs, evidence, owner, delivery channel, timeframe, or
success measure. No local authority or resident consultation has been supplied.

## Safe purpose for now

Do not publish advice yet. First learn what neighbors mean by the problem and
what small, voluntary capability would be useful. This draft makes no claims
about local services, risks, routes, resources, consensus, or willingness to
volunteer.

## Three decisions needed

1. What specific problem or shared concern is in scope, and what place or
   community boundary does “neighbors” mean?
2. Who should the guide serve, including languages, access needs, renters or
   other constraints, and whose perspective is currently missing?
3. Who will own the guide, where will it be shared, when is it needed, and what
   observable result would count as useful?

## Low-burden resident check

Before drafting advice, invite a small, voluntary response through whatever
channel residents already use, with paper and offline options if needed. Ask
only: “What problem should this address?”, “What would make a one-page guide
useful?”, and “What would make it hard to use?” Permit anonymous answers, a
pass, drawing, or spoken response; do not request names, health details, or
private contact information. Report responses as themes, not consensus, unless
broader participation supports that claim.

## Status and next step

Agent check: the supplied brief was reviewed and no unsupported local facts or
unaccepted roles were added. Community pilot: not run. After the decisions
above and a resident check, draft one small activity with a usable output and
an optional individual action; then test it with residents before calling it
ready for a community pilot.
