# Riverside Tenants Council: 45-minute outage-card workshop

## Purpose and boundary

This is one bounded co-design intervention for the fictional Riverside Tenants
Council, with 12 expected participants. The group will produce and test a
one-page building-outage contact and accessibility card that residents can
revise. It is a prototype, not yet an approved emergency procedure: the
workshop does not invent contact details, promise that a route works, or
replace later verification.

The fixture reports that residents previously found a long phone tree
confusing. Therefore, this workshop treats a short primary/backup contact card
as a design hypothesis to test, not as a settled answer.

## Participants, roles, and materials

- One facilitator keeps time, explains the activity, protects the boundaries,
  and makes space for dissent.
- One note-taker records decisions, disagreements, unanswered questions, and
  suggestions from the quiet comment box. The note-taker does not record
  diagnoses or personal safety situations.
- Twelve residents are expected. Participation is seated; nobody needs to
  stand, move around the room, or disclose a medical diagnosis or personal
  safety situation.
- Use large-print paper, markers, sticky notes, the translated phrase card,
  and a quiet comment box. Offer drawing, circling, pointing, or writing as
  alternatives to speaking.

## Take-home artifact

Each participant leaves with a marked-up one-page prototype of this card. If
the group has not verified a field, the field remains visibly marked
“TO VERIFY” rather than being filled with a guess.

```text
RIVERSIDE BUILDING OUTAGE CARD — DRAFT / VERSION: ______

1. FIRST CONTACT
   Primary contact/name: _________________________________
   How to reach them: ____________________________________
   What to say or send: __________________________________

2. IF THERE IS NO RESPONSE
   Backup contact/name: __________________________________
   How to reach them: ____________________________________
   When/how to try the backup: ____________________________

3. ACCESSIBILITY ROUTE
   If the usual route does not work, contact/ask for: ______
   ______________________________________________________

4. UPDATES AND FEEDBACK
   Where residents can get an update: _____________________
   How to report that this card needs correction: __________

OPEN QUESTION / TO VERIFY: ________________________________
LAST TESTED OR REVISED: __________________________________
```

The card deliberately has a short primary/backup path, an accessibility
route, and an update/feedback route. Those fields are the working structure;
the workshop must not fabricate names, numbers, locations, language text, or
guarantees about availability.

## Facilitation and accessibility choices

Keep the room seated and make all paper readable in large print. Put the
quiet comment box where it can be reached without standing. Read instructions
aloud and also write them in short phrases. Use the translated phrase card as
available support, without claiming that it covers every participant's
language or translating content that has not been verified. Accept marks,
drawings, pointing, and written notes as full participation. A participant
working a night shift can contribute through the same activity without being
asked to explain their schedule or private situation.

The facilitator should use neutral prompts such as “What would make this
unclear?” and “What would a resident do next?” rather than asking who has
experienced an outage or who needs help. Do not ask for diagnoses, disability
details, immigration information, or personal safety stories. Keep the
note-taker's record focused on card requirements, decisions, dissent, and
unknowns.

## Handling dissent and missing voices

Use two visible areas on the paper: **agreed for this draft** and **dissent /
to verify**. A suggestion does not disappear because it lacks a majority. The
facilitator should summarize a disagreement in neutral terms, ask each side
whether the summary is accurate, and preserve both the concern and the
decision rule used. If the group cannot resolve it in the allotted time, mark
the field “UNRESOLVED” and move on; do not force consensus.

For participants who speak less, have limited English, cannot stand, work
night shifts, or prefer privacy, the silent pass, nonverbal options, seated
pairing, translated phrase card, and quiet comment box provide routes into the
same decision. The absent-resident route is intentionally a follow-up
placeholder: the council must choose and confirm a return channel after this
workshop. Comments received later should be appended to the dissent/unknowns
record and considered in the next revision, without pretending they were
present in this session.

## Checks an agent can perform before the workshop

Using the supplied fixture and the draft above, an agent can check that:

- the run-of-show totals exactly 45 minutes;
- the plan assumes one facilitator and one note-taker;
- the expected group size is 12;
- the listed materials are sufficient for the described activities;
- the plan includes seated participation, large print, the translated phrase
  card, nonverbal participation, and a quiet comment box;
- the plan does not request diagnoses or personal safety disclosures;
- the short primary/backup path is presented as a hypothesis grounded in the
  council note, not as a verified fact;
- the artifact has an explicit accessibility route, next-action test,
  dissent/unknowns area, and later-comment placeholder; and
- no invented contact, language, building, accessibility, or emergency details
  have been inserted.

An agent can also inspect the card for legibility and completeness of fields,
simulate whether the instructions lead to a first action, and flag every
“TO VERIFY” item for human follow-up. Those are document and consistency
checks, not evidence that the real contacts or routes work.

## Questions requiring a community pilot or follow-up

The workshop cannot answer these from the fixture alone:

- Are the eventual primary and backup contacts correct, reachable, and
  authorized to be listed?
- What exact accessibility route works in the building, at what times, and for
  which access needs?
- Do the card's words and any later translation make sense to residents with
  limited English? Is the translated phrase card adequate for this use?
- Can residents on night shifts obtain an update and use the route when they
  need it?
- Can participants consistently explain the next action and identify the
  accessibility route when using the completed card, rather than only the
  blank prototype?
- What return channel should an absent resident use, and who will review those
  comments?
- Is a primary/backup structure actually clearer for this council than another
  design, and what dissent remains after residents try it in context?
- Where should the finished card be placed or distributed, and how will a
  later correction be announced?

Treat these as pilot or follow-up questions, not assumptions to fill during
the 45-minute session. The bounded outcome of this workshop is a tested,
marked-up prototype and an honest record of decisions, dissent, and unknowns.
