# Riverside Tenants Council: 45-minute outage card workshop

**Format:** one bounded, participatory workshop for up to 12 residents  
**Facilitation team:** one facilitator and one note-taker  
**Purpose:** co-design and test a one-page building outage contact and accessibility card

This is a working draft for a fictional Riverside Tenants Council. It does not claim
that any contact, service, accessibility arrangement, language translation, or council
role has been confirmed. The workshop creates a testable card prototype and a short
list of decisions that still need resident or council confirmation.

## Community brief and outcome map

The supplied fixture describes 12 expected participants, including two people with
limited English, one person who cannot stand for long, and residents who work night
shifts. The session must not ask anyone to disclose a medical diagnosis or a personal
safety situation. Large-print paper, markers, sticky notes, a translated phrase card,
and a quiet comment box are available. A resident who misses the workshop needs a way
to comment later.

The council note says that residents previously found a long phone tree confusing and
that a short primary/backup contact card is preferred. This is a supplied design
hypothesis, not evidence of consensus.

| Need | Capability participants can demonstrate | Activity | Usable output | Next action | Check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Know what to do first during a building outage without a long phone tree | Explain the card's first action and backup action | Draft the card, then use it in a fictional outage scenario | Annotated one-page card prototype | Fill unresolved primary and backup routes only after an owner accepts them | Each participant or pair can point to the first and backup actions |
| Make an accessibility request without exposing sensitive information | Identify a safe, non-diagnostic accessibility route | Add route options to the card and test them against the scenario | Card section with plain-language route prompts and blanks for local confirmation | Council confirms which routes are real and usable | Participants can identify at least one route or mark the gap |
| Include people who speak less English, work nights, or cannot attend | Offer paper, writing, passing, paired support, and later comment | Quiet written input, optional pair work, and a take-home/comment copy | Comment log and catch-up copy of the draft | Collect absent-member comments through the council's agreed channel | Dissent, unanswered questions, and absent-member input are recorded |

## What participants leave with

Each participant may leave with a large-print or standard-print copy of the marked-up
card prototype, plus a private note of any action they want to take. The council also
keeps one de-identified working prototype and an open-question log. The prototype does
not contain invented phone numbers, names, diagnoses, household details, or private
safety information.

The card is intentionally a draft. Blank fields are visible so that an unconfirmed
contact or route cannot be mistaken for an available service.

### One-page card prototype

```text
RIVERSIDE TENANTS COUNCIL
BUILDING OUTAGE + ACCESSIBILITY CARD — WORKING DRAFT

1. FIRST ACTION
   When I notice or learn about a building outage, I should:
   ________________________________________________________________
   (Use the council-confirmed wording; do not add personal safety details.)

2. PRIMARY UPDATE / CONTACT ROUTE
   Role or channel: _______________________________________________
   How to reach it: _______________________________________________
   ACCEPTED BY: ____________________  DATE/REVIEW: _______________

3. BACKUP ROUTE IF PRIMARY IS UNAVAILABLE
   Role or channel: _______________________________________________
   How to reach it: _______________________________________________
   ACCEPTED BY: ____________________  DATE/REVIEW: _______________

4. ACCESSIBILITY ROUTE
   I can request or receive an update by:
   [ ] paper update   [ ] language help   [ ] seated/door assistance
   [ ] quiet/private request   [ ] other: _________________________
   Local route still to confirm: __________________________________

5. KEEP PRIVATE
   Do not put diagnoses, private safety situations, or unnecessary personal
   contact details on a shared card.

QUESTION OR CONCERN TO CHECK BEFORE PRINTING:
   ________________________________________________________________
```

The bracketed options are prompts for discussion, not promises that those supports
exist. Participants may cross out, replace, or add wording. A participant may leave
any response blank or use the quiet comment box instead.

## Preparation and materials

### Facilitator prepares

1. Print the card prototype in large print and standard print, with several blank
   copies. Print the participant worksheet below in the same formats.
2. Put markers and sticky notes at seated tables. Put the translated phrase card and
   the quiet comment box where people can reach them without standing.
3. Arrange chairs so every activity can be completed seated. Do not require a move to
   a wall, board, or gallery walk.
4. Reserve a clearly marked place for **open question**, **concern/dissent**, and
   **accepted next action** notes. The note-taker records themes without names unless
   a participant explicitly asks for a named follow-up.
5. Prepare the fictional scenario below. Do not fill in local contact routes before
   the council has confirmed them.
6. Tell the note-taker to watch for people who are quiet, interrupted, unable to
   follow the language, or unable to use the materials, and to record the barrier,
   not a diagnosis or personal story.

### Materials supplied by the fixture

- large-print paper;
- markers;
- sticky notes;
- a translated phrase card;
- a quiet comment box.

No purchase, internet access, phone number, roster, or accepted volunteer role is
assumed.

## Run-of-show: exactly 45 minutes

The minute totals include transitions and the time needed for writing or optional
language support.

| Time | Minutes | Activity | Facilitator instructions and completion check |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | 4 | Arrival, access check, and private prompt | Welcome people at seated tables. Explain that passing, writing, drawing, paired work, and private follow-up are all valid. State the no-diagnosis/no-personal-safety-disclosure boundary. Give each person a sticky note prompt: “What must this card help a resident do first?” People may hand it to the note-taker or use the quiet box. **Check:** notes are collected without requiring names or speech. |
| 0:04–0:08 | 4 | Purpose and fictional example | Show the blank card and the worked fictional row. Explain that the long phone tree is a supplied design hypothesis, not a decision already made. Explain primary, backup, accessibility route, and open-question fields. Invite the translated phrase card or a trusted pair, without treating it as complete interpretation. **Check:** ask for a thumbs-up, written mark, or private signal showing whether the task is clear; re-explain with the example if needed. |
| 0:08–0:15 | 7 | Quiet inventory: essential actions and barriers | Participants write or draw up to three things a resident needs from the card. They may work alone or with a partner. Prompt: “What would make the card too long, confusing, hard to read, hard to use at night, or unsafe to share?” The note-taker groups sticky notes under **first action**, **backup**, **access**, and **privacy**. **Check:** every person has a route to contribute, including a blank/pass. |
| 0:15–0:25 | 10 | Seated co-design of the card | Form pairs or groups of three or four, keeping people seated. Give each group a blank card and markers. Ask them to choose the shortest wording that preserves a first action, one primary route, one backup route, an accessibility route, and a privacy boundary. No group fills in an unconfirmed contact. Each group marks one **must keep**, one **change**, and one **unresolved** item. **Check:** each group returns a card with those three marks. |
| 0:25–0:33 | 8 | Fictional outage rehearsal | Read: “A building outage has been reported. The old long phone tree is not being used for this test. You have the draft card, but its local routes are not yet confirmed. What would you do first? What would you do if the primary route did not answer? How would you request an accessible update without sharing private information?” Groups silently trace the sequence on their card, then optionally explain it. **Check:** each group can point to a first action, a backup or a clearly marked gap, and an accessibility route or missing dependency. |
| 0:33–0:38 | 5 | Dissent and missing-voice pass | Read each proposed must-keep item without attributing it. Participants use sticky notes or the quiet box to mark **agree**, **concern**, **different need**, or **cannot test**. Ask specifically: “Who might this card leave out?” Do not vote a concern away. The note-taker records competing needs and unanswered questions as separate entries. **Check:** every concern is either linked to a proposed change or remains visibly open. |
| 0:38–0:42 | 4 | Minimum viable prototype and accepted actions | The facilitator summarizes the shortest common draft and the differences that remain. The group may accept wording, but no one is assigned a role by attendance. Ask for explicit acceptance from any person or council role willing to confirm a primary/backup route or review language/accessibility. Record unfilled roles as unfilled. **Check:** prototype fields, open questions, accepted actions, and unfilled dependencies are all visible. |
| 0:42–0:45 | 3 | Close, absent-member route, and exit check | Each person may complete the three exit checks on the worksheet or place a note in the box. State what was decided, what was not decided, who accepted what, and the review trigger. Provide a copy of the draft and a short comment slip for anyone absent or leaving early. Do not claim consensus. **Check:** participants can explain the first action or identify why it remains untestable; the note-taker confirms dissent and unanswered questions are logged. |

**Total: 4 + 4 + 7 + 10 + 8 + 5 + 4 + 3 = 45 minutes.**

### Optional cut if the session is running late

There is no optional activity in this schedule. If time pressure occurs, shorten the
spoken report-outs during the 0:33–0:38 pass and have groups submit written notes. Do
not cut the rehearsal, open-question record, accepted-action check, or closing route
for people who were absent.

## Facilitation choices and safeguards

### Participation and accessibility

- Keep all work seated; the participant who cannot stand for long never needs to
  move, post a note, or join a gallery walk.
- Offer large-print paper and read instructions aloud, while allowing people not to
  speak. Markers, drawing, passing, and partner work are valid alternatives to
  extended writing.
- Use the translated phrase card as an available aid, not as proof that the session
  has adequate interpretation. Ask participants what wording is unclear and mark
  language review as an open dependency.
- Treat night-shift residents' time as a design constraint. A person may leave early,
  work quietly, or use the comment slip later; no attendance-based role is assigned.
- Keep the quiet comment box reachable and explain whether the note-taker will read
  it during the session or after it. The facilitator should read it privately where
  possible and summarize themes without exposing personal details.
- Do not ask for medical diagnoses, personal safety situations, household
  vulnerabilities, or unnecessary personal contact information. The card's
  accessibility section asks for a route, not a reason or diagnosis.

### Language and understanding

Use short sentences and the fictional example before asking for group discussion.
Invite a participant to request a slower explanation or a pair conversation. Do not
appoint a participant as an interpreter without consent. Competent language review is
needed before a final translated card is printed; the fixture does not establish who
can provide it.

### Dissent and disagreement

The facilitator should use neutral phrases such as:

- “I hear two needs; I will record both rather than merge them.”
- “Is this a wording problem, a missing route, or a disagreement about the design?”
- “Who accepts this next action? If nobody accepts it, we will leave it unfilled.”
- “What would make this card unusable for someone who is not here tonight?”

If people disagree about a primary or backup route, keep both proposals in the open-
question log and leave the field blank until the responsible council role and affected
residents can review it. Do not use a majority vote to turn an untested accessibility
need into consensus.

### Missing voices and catch-up route

At the close, provide each attendee with a copy of the draft and a short comment slip:

```text
Riverside card catch-up comment

The part I would keep: ____________________________________________
The part that could exclude or confuse someone: ____________________
The first or backup action I could understand: _____________________
Accessibility or language route still missing: _____________________
I want a response / no response needed: ____________________________
```

The council must choose and announce the actual return channel; none is supplied in
the fixture. Until that is confirmed, the facilitator should say “return this through
the council's agreed channel” rather than inventing an email, phone number, office,
or deadline. If a resident cannot attend, the same blank card and comment slip should
be made available through that confirmed channel. Their comments should be added to
the open-question log without appointing them to a role or treating a single comment
as group consensus.

## Follow-through and maintenance

### Accepted actions and fallback

The note-taker uses this record during the closing:

| Action or dependency | Person/role explicitly accepting it | Status | Review trigger | Fallback if unfilled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Confirm primary route |  | Unfilled until accepted | Before any card is printed | Keep the field blank; do not publish a guessed route |
| Confirm backup route |  | Unfilled until accepted | Before any card is printed | Keep the field blank and record the gap |
| Review accessibility wording |  | Unfilled until accepted | After resident review | Keep route as a prompt, not a promised service |
| Review language wording |  | Unfilled until accepted | Before translated printing | Use plain-language source draft and mark translation pending |
| Collect absent-member comments |  | Unfilled until accepted | At the council's agreed follow-up point | Preserve the draft and open-question log; do not claim full input |

An accepted action must have a named willing actor or role, a feasible review trigger,
and a fallback. If no one accepts it, it remains **unfilled**. Attendance alone is
not acceptance.

### Agent checks possible before the workshop

An agent can perform these read-only or document checks before anyone uses the guide:

1. Verify the run-of-show arithmetic totals exactly 45 minutes, including transitions.
2. Trace the fixture's success signals to a specific activity and completion check:
   first/backup action, accessibility route, and recorded dissent/unanswered questions.
3. Cold-walk the guide from a new facilitator's perspective: materials, seating,
   instructions, fictional scenario, worksheet, close, and absent-member catch-up.
4. Fill the worksheet with the fictional example and confirm that a blank usable copy
   also exists.
5. Check that the card contains no invented contact details, no required diagnoses,
   no required personal safety disclosure, and no unaccepted volunteer assignment.
6. Check that each supplied constraint has an operational response: seated work,
   large print, paper/quiet contribution, language caveat, night-shift flexibility,
   and a later-comment placeholder.
7. Check that unresolved dependencies are visibly labeled and that the guide does not
   present the supplied phone-tree preference as consensus.

These checks establish structural readiness only. They do not establish that the card
works for Riverside residents.

### Questions requiring a community pilot or follow-up

The workshop or a later council follow-up must answer these questions; the fixture does
not answer them and this guide does not fabricate answers:

- Which real role or channel can accept the primary route, and which can accept the
  backup route? Will both actually be available during an outage?
- What is the correct first action for Riverside residents, and does it apply to all
  relevant building-outage situations?
- Which accessibility routes exist in practice, can be requested privately, and are
  acceptable to residents who need them?
- Is the translated phrase card sufficient for the two participants with limited
  English? What competent language review or interpretation is needed before printing?
- Can residents on night shifts, people absent from the workshop, and residents who
  prefer not to speak understand and use the card without extra help?
- Does the primary/backup design reduce confusion compared with the old long phone
  tree, or does it create a new ambiguity?
- Which concerns represent a minority or missing perspective that must remain visible
  even if most participants prefer another wording?
- Who owns the draft, what channel returns absent-member comments, and when will the
  council revisit the open-question log?

A bounded follow-up should test the draft with residents who attended and at least one
available route for people who did not. Ask whether people can complete the card test
without extra explanation, what prevented participation, and whether the next action
fits their circumstances. Record each observed barrier, proposed change, and later
check. A single workshop pilot is evidence about this workshop and draft, not proof of
effectiveness for every resident or future outage.
