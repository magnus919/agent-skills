# Willow County offline-first household communication guide

**Edition:** 1.0 — 2026-09-15  
**Audience:** Households in a fictional unincorporated Willow County valley of 40 households  
**Format:** One printable participant guide with matching host notes  
**Status:** Draft for local verification and a community pilot  

## Purpose and limits

This package helps households practice a small, voluntary communication action for a
multi-day power or communications outage. It is designed to remain usable when
internet and cellular service are unavailable for up to 48 hours and when a
household has no smartphone, car, generator, reliable indoor heating, or constant
electricity.

It is a preparedness-learning package, not a live incident-response plan, a welfare
check system, a medical service, or a promise that any household will be reached.
It does not establish emergency coverage, professional care, an evacuation route, a
shelter, or a functioning communications network. The fixture does not supply those
facts. A missed paper mark, radio announcement, phone call, or in-person contact does
not prove that a household is safe, unsafe, checked, or in need.

Use fictional households during the activity unless a person freely chooses to use a
real preference. Do not put names, addresses, medical details, diagnoses, access
needs that a person considers private, or phone numbers on a shared worksheet. A
household may pass, write privately, use drawing or a proxy writer, or leave the
activity without explaining why.

## Community brief

### Known design inputs

These are supplied by the fictional fixture, not findings from a Willow County
consultation:

- The setting is an unincorporated valley of 40 households.
- Internet and cellular service may be unavailable for 48 hours.
- Some households have no smartphone, car, generator, or reliable indoor heating.
- A few residents use mobility aids and cannot reach the ridge on foot.
- Private medical details must not be published.
- Volunteers must not be described as providing professional emergency care.
- People should use two independent channels when possible.
- A paper status mark at a household's agreed location requires that household's
  consent.

### Desired capability

After the workshop, a participant can use paper to state a neutral, non-sensitive
message, identify one channel that does not depend on internet or cellular service,
name that channel's limit, identify a second possible channel, and mark which part is
still unconfirmed or requires another person's acceptance.

The workshop demonstrates an individual skill. It does not create a roster or prove
that the valley has coverage.

### Participation constraints translated into design choices

| Constraint | Design choice |
|---|---|
| No internet, cellular service, smartphone, or constant electricity | Every instruction and worksheet is printable. A device is optional. The practice uses paper and pencils. |
| No car and some people cannot walk to the ridge | No step requires a car, ridge travel, or travel to the grange hall. Any in-person option must be locally accessible and separately confirmed. |
| No generator or reliable heat | No step assumes charging, refrigeration, fuel, or a powered meeting place. Keep the session short and offer private catch-up. |
| Mobility aids and unknown access barriers | Ask privately about seating, writing surface, large print, language, and arrival/exit needs. Do not ask for a diagnosis. |
| Limited paper supply | Use a one-page worksheet where possible. The fictional library supply is a claim to verify, not a guaranteed stockpile. |
| Uneven attendance | Provide the catch-up route below. Absence never assigns a job. |
| No accepted volunteer roster | Mark roles as proposed or unfilled until a person accepts. Keep an individual fallback that does not depend on a volunteer. |

## Channel card: what is supplied, what it can do, and its limit

These are fictional local channels from the supplied fixture. They are **supplied**
inputs, not independently verified operating facts.

| Channel | Possible use in the exercise | Limit that must stay visible |
|---|---|---|
| **Printed card — Willow Library porch box** | A household can read a printed status or instruction without internet, a phone, or electricity. A host can give paper copies to people who miss the session. | Stock is checked weekly, not continuously. The fixture says the library can print 60 large-print cards before its backup battery is exhausted, but both that capacity and current stock need a local check. |
| **FM radio — WLV-88.4** | A household with a battery radio may listen for the supplied 08:00 and 18:00 local announcements. This can be a second channel when reception and a radio are available. | It requires a battery radio and may not reach the valley floor. The schedule, reception, and current power/battery situation need a local check. Hearing an announcement is not confirmed by silence. |
| **Phone tree — volunteer coordinator** | A possible channel after phone service is operating. It may be useful when the network returns. | It works only when the phone network is operating. It is not an offline fallback, and no coordinator's acceptance is established by this guide. |
| **In-person check — grange hall** | A possible place to exchange information if a person freely chooses to go and the location is usable. | The grange hall is not accessible to everyone. No resident is required to travel there. An accessible nearby option, opening/availability, and any person willing to help are unconfirmed. |

### Offline fallback ladder

Use the first option that is actually available and consented to; do not treat the
order as a guarantee or as a command to travel.

1. **Paper in hand:** Keep the printed guide and a blank paper slip. Write only a
   neutral message and the next requested action. This is the default option because
   it does not require power, a phone, a car, or a network.
2. **Consented paper location:** If a household has chosen and consented to a visible
   location, a paper status mark may be left there. If there is no consented location,
   mark this option unavailable; do not invent one.
3. **Battery radio:** Listen at the two supplied times only if a battery radio is
   available and reception reaches the household. If either condition is unknown,
   record it as unconfirmed rather than assuming the radio works.
4. **Accessible in-person exchange:** Use only a nearby location or doorstep method
   that the household has chosen, that does not require ridge travel, and for which a
   person has accepted the action. If no person has accepted, the fallback is
   unfilled. The grange hall is not a required destination.
5. **Phone tree:** Treat as a later, network-dependent option, not as an offline
   method. Its use requires the phone network and a coordinator who has accepted the
   role.

## Workshop module: Paper-first relay rehearsal

- **Stable module ID:** WC-OFF-01
- **Capability and observable completion:** A participant completes Worksheet W1,
  writes a neutral practice message on paper, passes it through a paper relay without
  a device, identifies a second channel and one channel limit, and records at least
  one unresolved dependency.
- **Prerequisites and prior outputs used:** None. Participants may use a fictional
  household. No address, phone number, radio, vehicle, internet, or electricity is
  required.
- **Duration:** 75 minutes, including access check, optional interpretation,
  transitions, and closing.
- **Materials and preparation:** One printed guide per participant or shared copies;
  one W1 per participant; pencils or pens; blank paper slips; a visible clock or a
  paper timekeeper; large-print copies; a quiet alternative writing place; and a
  host copy of the fictional scenario. A battery radio may be shown but is optional.

### Participant instructions

#### Before starting

You may use the fictional household **Birch** for every answer. If you use your own
household, keep the worksheet private unless you choose to share a general preference.
Do not write a medical detail or full address. A response such as “paper at an agreed
location” is enough; do not identify the location on a shared copy.

The practice is not a real check. Nobody will visit a household, send a real message,
publish a list, or treat a missing response as evidence about anyone's safety.

#### Scenario

It is 18:00 during a fictional outage. Internet and cellular service are unavailable.
Electricity may be unavailable. Birch has no car and no smartphone. Birch cannot
reach the ridge on foot. The supplied possibilities are a printed card at the Willow
Library porch box, an FM announcement on WLV-88.4 if a battery radio receives it, a
phone tree that works only if the phone network is operating, and an in-person
exchange at the grange hall, which is not accessible to everyone. No helper has yet
accepted a role for Birch.

Your task is to plan and rehearse a **non-sensitive paper message**, not to solve the
outage or promise a check.

#### Practice steps

1. **Choose a household.** Use Birch or another fictional label. Complete the private
   parts of W1: a preferred channel, a limit, a possible second channel, and what
   requires consent or another person's acceptance.
2. **Write a neutral paper message.** On the practice slip, write:

   ```text
   PRACTICE ONLY — household Birch prefers a paper update at its agreed location.
   Please return this slip to the host. No private details are included.
   ```

   Change the fictional label or preference if you wish. Do not use a real address or
   phone number.
3. **Relay without a device.** Hand the slip to a willing partner or the host. The
   receiver reads it silently, states the next action in their own words, and returns
   it. If there is no partner, the host acts as the fictional receiver. This is a
   paper relay, not a household visit.
4. **Remove one channel.** The host announces one of these conditions: “no radio
   reception,” “no paper stock at the porch box,” or “no accepted in-person helper.”
   In W1, mark what still works, what cannot be claimed, and which step would need
   local verification.
5. **Debrief.** Share only a general lesson if you choose: what was clear, what
   depended on consent, and what you could not establish from the fixture. You may
   pass.
6. **Choose one follow-through action.** It may be a private action, such as keeping
   this paper copy with a pencil, or a question for local verification. A proposed
   action is not an accepted volunteer role.

## Facilitator and host notes

These notes match module `WC-OFF-01` and worksheet `W1` above. A host may be a
resident, librarian, or other person who accepts the hosting role; this document does
not establish that anyone has accepted it.

### Prepare before the session

1. Print the complete guide, one W1 per participant, the fictional scenario, and
   several blank paper slips. Offer large-print copies and a quiet place for private
   writing. Do not require a phone, internet account, car, battery radio, or payment.
2. Confirm, before advertising the session, whether the proposed room is physically
   accessible, whether people can arrive and leave without a car or ridge walk, and
   whether interpretation or language review is needed. The fixture does not confirm
   any of these. If they are not confirmed, describe the session as a draft or move it
   to a confirmed accessible arrangement; do not call the grange hall universally
   usable.
3. Prepare a paper-only timing method. If there is no clock or timekeeper, write the
   blocks on the host copy and end each block by agreement. Do not assume electricity.
4. Place the fictional channel card where participants can see it. Mark the radio
   schedule, library capacity, and all unconfirmed facts as **supplied/unverified**.
5. Decide who keeps the host's copy. If nobody accepts that record role, the host
   keeps only their own copy and participants keep their worksheets. Do not collect
   household records by default.
6. Prepare the sentence: “Attendance does not assign a role. We will ask before
   sharing or recording anything. You may pass or use Birch.”

### Run the 75-minute session

| Activity | Minutes | Instructions and completion check |
|---|---:|---|
| Welcome, access check, and boundaries | 10 | Offer paper, large print, private writing, drawing, proxy writing, interpretation, and passing. Say that this is a practice, not a live check. Check that nobody is expected to travel to the ridge or grange hall. **Complete when** participants can name the fictional scenario and one way to pass. |
| Why paper-first and channel limits | 10 | Read the purpose, channel card, and limits aloud. Explain that phone tree is network-dependent, radio may not reach the valley floor, library stock is not continuous, and the grange hall is not required. **Complete when** participants can point to one supplied fact and one unresolved fact. |
| Private W1 inventory | 12 | Invite participants to use Birch. Read each W1 prompt, including the private/share boundary. Do not ask anyone to explain a personal limit. Offer the worked example if someone is stuck. **Complete when** each willing participant has a first channel, a limit, and a second possible channel or “none confirmed.” |
| Paper relay rehearsal | 20 | Participants write a neutral practice slip and hand it to a willing partner or host. The receiver repeats the next action without adding private information. If no partner is available, host acts as receiver. No real household is visited or contacted. **Complete when** the slip returns and the next action is stated. |
| Remove-one-channel test and debrief | 13 | Announce “no radio reception,” “no paper stock,” or “no accepted in-person helper.” Participants revise W1. Ask what still works, what requires consent, and what cannot be claimed. **Complete when** each willing participant records one unresolved dependency. |
| Follow-through choice | 5 | Ask each person to choose one private action or verification question. Ask separately whether anyone accepts a shared role. Record “none accepted” when applicable; never infer acceptance from attendance. **Complete when** each role is accepted, proposed, or unfilled. |
| Closing and catch-up | 5 | Repeat the limits. Explain the catch-up route and how to return non-sensitive corrections. State the proposed review trigger. **Complete when** participants leave with W1 and know who, if anyone, keeps a shared copy. |

### Suggested host words

Use plain language and pause for interpretation:

> “We are practicing a paper action for a fictional outage. We are not checking real
> households today. A blank or missing response does not tell us whether anyone is
> safe. You may use Birch, pass, write privately, or leave.”

> “A channel written on a worksheet is not necessarily available. Mark whether it is
> accepted, proposed, or unconfirmed. We will not appoint a helper who is absent or
> assume that the grange hall works for everyone.”

> “The paper slip is deliberately neutral. Do not put a name, address, phone number,
> diagnosis, or other private detail on it.”

### If the session gets stuck

- **Confusion about the worksheet:** Read the Birch example and ask which line is a
  fact, which is a proposal, and which is unresolved. Do not ask for a personal story.
- **No partner for the relay:** Host receives the slip, repeats the next action, and
  returns it. Mark “host only” and do not call this a community test.
- **No paper slips or pencils:** Use the back of a supplied draft or verbal rehearsal
  with the host, then mark the paper action as untested. Do not claim the offline
  practice was completed.
- **Time pressure:** Cut the optional sharing of personal examples, not the paper
  relay, debrief, or closing decisions. Preserve the unresolved-facts record.
- **Interpretation or access support is missing:** Offer the fictional example and a
  private catch-up route; do not label an unsupported session participant-tested.
- **Disagreement about a location or channel:** Record competing needs and the local
  verification question. Do not write consensus or select a site for people.
- **Someone asks for professional care or an urgent response:** State that this guide
  does not provide professional care or emergency coverage and contains no verified
  local emergency instructions. Do not invent one.

### Follow-through at closing

Use this record verbally or on a host worksheet without collecting private household
details:

| Follow-through item | Record |
|---|---|
| Individual action and feasible timing | Participant keeps a paper copy and pencil; timing is chosen privately. |
| Shared action proposed | Ask willing households, including people without cars or smartphones and people using mobility aids, which paper/radio/in-person options are usable. No outreach is authorized by this guide. |
| Shared action accepted | ______________________________ / none accepted |
| Unfilled role or dependency | Accessible-location verifier, card-stock checker, radio-schedule/reception checker, and any in-person helper are unfilled unless a person explicitly accepts. |
| Fallback if no role is accepted | Individuals retain their own private paper plan; no group coverage is promised. |
| Catch-up route | Give the complete printed guide and W1. A person may complete the Birch rehearsal alone or with a host who has accepted a support role. No travel is required. |
| Evidence for next check | A dated note saying whether the paper relay was completed, what channel was unavailable, and what remains unverified. No personal details. |

### Catch-up sheet for an absent participant

Give the person these steps on paper:

1. Read “Purpose and limits,” the channel card, and the scenario.
2. Complete W1 using Birch if preferred.
3. Write the practice-only paper message and read it aloud to yourself or a willing
   host. If nobody is available, mark the relay “not yet practiced.”
4. Remove one channel from the scenario and record the resulting limit.
5. Choose one private action and one verification question. Do not accept a shared
   role unless you explicitly choose it and understand what it requires.
6. Return only non-sensitive corrections if you want the guide changed. The host may
   offer a supported catch-up only if that support role has been accepted.

## What Willow County still needs to verify

The following are deliberate open items. This package must not be presented as a
fully localized operating plan until the relevant items have been checked by people
with appropriate local knowledge and the results are dated.

| Open item | Why it changes the guide | Evidence needed before relying on it |
|---|---|---|
| WLV-88.4 schedule and whether announcements actually occur at 08:00 and 18:00 | A radio fallback cannot be timed or described as available without this check. | A current local confirmation and a dated reception test at representative valley-floor locations. |
| WLV-88.4 reach in the valley floor | A household may own a battery radio and still not hear the announcement. | A locally conducted reception check, including households that cannot reach the ridge. |
| Willow Library porch-box stock, weekly check practice, and backup-battery capacity | “Printed card available” is not continuous coverage; large-print supply may run out. | Current confirmation of stock, printing limit, replenishment owner, and large-print availability. |
| Which households want a check-in, and by which method | Attendance or a need does not equal consent. | Voluntary, private preference collection with a way to decline, correct, or withdraw. |
| Consent for each household's paper status location | A paper mark must not expose a household or be placed without permission. | Explicit consent recorded by the household, with the location kept private. |
| Accessible locations other than the grange hall | Some residents cannot use the grange hall or reach the ridge. | A local access check covering route, seating, writing surface, timing, and no-car arrival. |
| Any in-person helper or coordinator | No volunteer role is established by this fixture or by workshop attendance. | Explicit acceptance, scope, limits, and fallback if the person is unavailable. |
| Language, large-print, hearing, vision, and proxy-writing needs | A paper guide is not usable if the format or interpretation excludes participants. | Competent local language/access review and a supported pilot observation. |
| Whether a phone tree should be listed after service returns | It is not an offline path and the named owner may not accept. | Current owner acceptance and a separate, clearly labeled network-restored procedure. |

Until these checks occur, use words such as **supplied**, **proposed**, and
**unconfirmed**. Do not publish phone numbers, medical information, private addresses,
or a volunteer list in a shared guide.

## Maintenance and handoff

- **Edition and date:** 1.0 — 2026-09-15.
- **Maintainer:** Unconfirmed. A host may accept maintenance at the end of a session;
  otherwise no shared maintenance role exists.
- **Acceptance status:** Draft for local verification and a community pilot. Not
  accepted as a live response plan.
- **Review trigger:** Before the first community pilot; after any channel, access,
  stock, schedule, or role change; after a failed paper rehearsal; or after a person
  withdraws consent.
- **Feedback route:** Offer a paper correction slip to a host who has accepted to
  receive it. If no host accepts, participants keep their copies and may raise a
  question at the next separately arranged session. Do not require internet or a
  phone.
- **Change control:** Date each revision. Show which open item changed, what activity
  or worksheet line changed, and how replacement paper copies can be obtained. Keep
  private household records with their owners.
- **What works without a group owner:** An individual can keep the printed guide,
  pencil, blank paper, and their own W1. No shared channel, roster, or coverage is
  implied.

claiming participant-tested usability, run the workshop with willing households that
include, where possible, people without cars or smartphones, people using mobility
aids, people who may not have constant electricity, and people needing large print or
