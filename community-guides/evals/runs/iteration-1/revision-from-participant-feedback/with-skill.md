# Maple Court power or water outage card

**Revision:** 0.2 — resident-ready draft for a community pilot  
**Source:** Synthetic local fixture `participant-feedback.json`  
**Status:** Ready for an agent walkthrough and a bounded pilot; not participant-validated  
**Scope:** Fictional Maple Court building. No internet or external source was used.

## Resident-facing card

### If the power or water is out

1. **Note the basics privately:** your unit, whether the problem is power, water,
   or both, and when you noticed it. Do not put medical information or private
   contact details on a shared copy of this card.
2. **During building-desk hours:** notify the building desk. Say: “There is a
   power or water outage in unit ____. I noticed it at ____.” Add an access need
   only if it is necessary for a safe response.
3. **When the desk is closed:** use the verified after-hours method printed below.
   Do not guess a number, invent a contact, or assume that a message was received.
   If the field is blank or marked **not yet confirmed**, the building has not yet
   supplied a dependable after-hours route; report it as soon as the desk opens
   and ask the building owner or manager to complete this card before relying on
   it overnight.

**Verified after-hours reporting method:** ________________________________  
**Contact or location:** _________________________________________________  
**Verified by / date:** __________________________________________________  
**Fallback if that method is unavailable:** ________________________________

### Elevator and accessibility safety

- Do **not** use the elevator during a power or water outage unless building staff
  or emergency responders have confirmed that it is operating safely.
- If you are in an elevator that stops, use its alarm or emergency communication
  method and wait for help. If you are trapped, injured, or in immediate danger,
  use your area's emergency service number.
- If you use a mobility aid or cannot safely use stairs, do not attempt to solve
  the problem by taking an unconfirmed elevator. Use the verified reporting route
  and request an accessible check-in. A check-in may be by phone or at the door,
  according to your choice; no medical explanation is required.
- A neighbor may help only if that person has agreed. Attendance, proximity, or
  a note on a shared list is not an accepted volunteer assignment.

**Optional private check-in plan**  
Preferred method (phone / door / other): _________________________________  
Person or service already agreed: ________________________________________  
Safe time or constraint to note: _________________________________________

Leave this section blank if you do not want a check-in. Keep the completed
section private unless you choose to share it with the person or service that
has accepted the check-in.

### What this card does not promise

This card does not promise a repair time. The outage duration and response time
are unknown until the building confirms them. A neon-green design is not required
for this revision; any final print treatment must remain legible in ordinary light,
in grayscale, and for residents with color-vision differences.

### Language note

The card is intended to remain available in English and Spanish. In both versions,
replace the old phrase **“utility interruption”** with the plain-language equivalent
of **“power or water outage.”** The Spanish wording must receive competent language
review before distribution; this fixture does not provide evidence that a Spanish
translation has been reviewed.

## Facilitator and building-owner notes

Before distributing revision 0.2:

1. Fill in and verify the after-hours method, contact/location, verifier, date, and
   fallback. The contact remains **unresolved** until a responsible building owner
   checks it through the intended channel.
2. Confirm that the desk-hours and after-hours routes accept a report from a renter
   working nights. Do not treat a daytime desk schedule as an after-hours plan.
3. Confirm the accessible check-in process without requiring residents to disclose
   diagnoses or other private information. Record only the minimum information a
   resident elects to share.
4. Print the English copy and obtain competent review of the Spanish copy. Keep the
   essential steps on paper so the card does not depend on internet access.
5. If no person accepts ownership of the after-hours route, leave that role unfilled
   and keep the gap visible. The fallback is next-business-day reporting, not an
   implied emergency service.

During a pilot, ask residents to try the card with a fictional or de-identified
scenario. Do not ask them to disclose a real medical need or personal contact
information. Record the barrier, the proposed change, and the next check; do not
record a consensus that was not obtained.

## Provenance and preserved initial version

This revision is based only on the supplied fictional fixture. The initial guide
is retained here as an unchanged provenance record:

**Version 0.1**

- If service is interrupted, document the affected unit and notify the building
  desk during desk hours.
- Use the elevator only when the outage is short.
- The after-hours contact is not yet confirmed.
- The card uses the phrase “utility interruption” and has English and Spanish text.

The fixture says the comments came from three residents and are **not a
representative vote**. The comments are preserved as distinct observations:

- **R1:** “Utility interruption” was hard to understand; say “power or water outage.”
- **R2:** Night-shift renters need a way to report an outage when the desk is closed.
- **R3:** The elevator sentence feels unsafe; a mobility-aid user needs an accessible
  check-in plan.
- **R1:** Make the card neon green and guarantee a 30-minute repair.

## What an agent can check now

- The old phrase “utility interruption” and the unsafe “use the elevator when the
  outage is short” instruction are not used as current instructions.
- The revised card names power and water outages, includes a closed-desk workflow,
  and visibly marks the after-hours contact as unresolved until filled and verified.
- No 30-minute repair guarantee, invented phone number, accepted volunteer, or local
  service commitment is presented as fact.
- The accessibility plan does not require a resident to disclose medical details and
  does not rely on an unconfirmed elevator or an unaccepted neighbor.
- The original version, source status, participant identities in the fixture, dissent,
  and limits of the three comments remain visible.
- The document has an observable next action for each major need: report the outage,
  fill and verify the after-hours route, choose an optional private check-in, or leave
  the role unfilled with the gap recorded.

These are document and cold-walkthrough checks. They do not prove that a contact
works, that the building can provide an accessible response, that both languages
are understood, or that the card is usable in real conditions.

## What must be re-tested in a community pilot

1. A night-shift renter can find the card, identify whether the issue is power or
   water, and successfully send a report while the desk is closed.
2. The verified after-hours method reaches the intended recipient, and the fallback
   is understandable when the primary method is unavailable.
3. A resident who uses a mobility aid or cannot use stairs can choose and receive a
   safe check-in without using an unsafe elevator or disclosing more than they wish.
4. Residents understand the revised wording in the English and reviewed Spanish
   versions, including what “verified” means and what to do when the field is blank.
5. The final visual treatment is readable in ordinary light and grayscale and does
   not make the single neon-green suggestion look like a community-wide decision.
6. Pilot participants identify any missing dependency, confusing step, or unwanted
   privacy exposure. Recruit beyond the three original commenters before claiming
   broader resident preference.
