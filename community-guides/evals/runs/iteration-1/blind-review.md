# Blind comparison review

Scope: only the two permitted manifest entries, their raw fixtures, and the four
blind outputs in `/private/tmp/community-guides-blind/` were used. No community
skill, reference, or evidence material was consulted.

Scoring is binary against the manifest assertions. “Pass” means the assertion is
met by the output as written; the evidence cites output line ranges. Minor
editorial defects are called out separately and are not upgraded to failures
unless they break the asserted capability.

### Feasibility, usability, and tradeoffs

Both outputs are feasible and safe to run from the fixture. Neither invents contacts,
routes, translation coverage, or consensus. Both preserve the seated, privacy-safe
design and the absent-resident pathway.

Output B is the slight preference for a new host: it supplies a participant worksheet
with a worked example, completion checks, preparation instructions, an accepted-action
record, and a maintenance section. Output A is the lower-burden version: it is about
half as long and keeps the core run-of-show and artifact easy to scan. The extra length
of B is useful only where it adds operational detail; it should not be treated as a
quality score by itself.

Assumptions are handled well in both. B is more explicit about unfilled ownership and
the return channel remaining unknown; A is more concise and equally clear about not
fabricating local facts.

### Actual issue found

- B says “the three exit checks” while its worksheet presents four checkboxes (`B.md:136-145`, `B.md:161`). This is a minor wording/count defect, not a critical failure.

No assertion failed and no critical contradiction was found in this case.

### Feasibility, usability, and tradeoffs

Both outputs are runnable and preserve the routine-care boundary. Both use the fixture’s
water, pest, tomato, accessible-bed, and compost constraints without turning them into
guarantees. Both offer a new gardener a valid observation-only route and make ownership
conditional on actual acceptance.

Output A is the slight preference for ordinary use: it is shorter, has a flexible
30–45-minute activity, and keeps the operating loop and seasonal map close together.
Its pilot is also bounded without imposing a recurring program. Output B is stronger for
auditability and handoff training: it includes a fact-to-design source table, a precise
55-minute host run sheet, a clearer catch-up route, and explicit pilot evidence. That
precision creates more host burden, especially when the optional work period is added;
the document does clearly keep that period outside the huddle (`B.md:235-250`).

Neither output should win merely for being longer. A is easier to carry into a real care
window; B is easier to inspect and teach from when provenance and repeatability matter.

### Actual issue found

- B’s host run sheet says participants should complete “Sections 1–10 of the worksheet,”
  but the worksheet is presented as one table and does not contain those numbered
  sections (`B.md:149-188`, `B.md:235-246`). This is a minor cross-reference/usability
  defect. It does not overturn the substantive assertion result because the surrounding
  instructions clearly identify the needed fields, but a new host should be told to
  complete the worksheet rather than nonexistent sections.

No critical contradiction was found in this case.

## Overall preference

- Workshop: prefer B narrowly for a new host; prefer A when minimum reading and meeting burden are the primary constraint.
- Garden: prefer A narrowly for live use; prefer B when source traceability, exact host timing, and pilot evidence are the primary constraint.

All manifest assertions pass for both outputs. The meaningful differences are
operational: B is more explicit and auditable, while A is more compact and lower burden.
