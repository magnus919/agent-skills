# South Fork Community Garden: Seasonal Care Guide

**Package type:** runnable participant, facilitator, and host guide  
**Garden:** fictional South Fork Community Garden (22 plots)  
**Operating window:** March through November  
**Version:** baseline

## 1. Purpose and boundary

This guide helps gardeners notice ordinary seasonal conditions, choose a small care action, record the choice, and hand off anything that cannot be completed immediately. It is a routine horticulture and stewardship activity for a fictional community garden. It is **not disaster response**.

This package does not provide medical, emergency, or pesticide instructions. No gardener is expected to lift compost, use a tool, work alone, or continue a task that does not fit their time, mobility, energy, or experience. The garden avoids synthetic pesticides: an unknown pest is photographed and discussed before any action is chosen. This guide does not recommend a pesticide or treatment.

The guide uses these local operating facts:

- The west beds dry first.
- The rain barrel is shared and is usually refilled after rain; water availability must be checked, never promised.
- The accessible raised bed is reserved for shared crops and offers a 30-minute watering task option.
- Compost is turned on the first Saturday of each month only when two people can work safely.
- Last year's log observed tomato staking by late May. That is an observation, not a guaranteed date for this year.
- The local water card says to water deeply when soil is dry below the surface.
- The local access card asks hosts to offer seated, paired, short, and observation-only tasks.

This year's rainfall, pest pressure, and volunteer availability are unknown. Every seasonal decision below is therefore conditional on what participants observe on the day.

## 2. The operating loop

Use the same short loop whenever a care window is opened. The loop can be completed in person, by a seated observer, or through a handoff.

> **Observe → Decide → Do, assign, or monitor → Record → Handoff → Revisit**

The output of each loop is one completed worksheet, even when the decision is “monitor” or “needs a partner.” A worksheet is a care record, not proof that water, volunteers, or a particular result will be available.

### Decision rules

| Observation | Decision | Record and handoff |
|---|---|---|
| Soil is dry below the surface and water is available | Water deeply, using the shared barrel and a task size the participant chooses | Record bed, approximate task, barrel status, and who will confirm completion if unfinished |
| Soil is not dry below the surface | Monitor; do not water just to fill a slot | Note what was checked and the next observation trigger |
| The west bed is dry but the barrel cannot support the task now | Do not promise or improvise water; record “blocked by availability” | Ask the host to place it on the shared handoff list and check at the next agreed window |
| A tomato plant is observed to need support | Offer a paired or tool-optional staking task; use the late-May log only as context, not as a schedule | Record the observation and the person or pair taking the support task |
| An unknown pest is observed | Take a photo and discuss before action | Record the photo location, visible observation, and discussion owner; do not select a pesticide |
| It is the first Saturday of the month and two people can work safely on compost | The pair may turn compost if both choose the task | Record the pair and completion; if the condition is not met, defer and hand off without assigning lifting |
| Any task does not fit a participant's capacity or experience | Choose seated, paired, short, or observation-only work | Record the task requested, not a diagnosis or personal explanation |

No rule requires a fixed weekly rhythm or a seven-session program. Hosts open a care window when a seasonal check, a participant request, or a new observation makes one useful.

## 3. Seasonal care map: March–November

This is a set of prompts, not a calendar of guaranteed conditions. A host may use one prompt, several prompts, or none during a care window.

### March: establish the baseline

- Walk or observe each shared area, including the west beds and accessible raised bed.
- Record what is visibly present, what needs a closer below-surface soil check, and what is unknown.
- Confirm how the host will show rain-barrel availability and where unfinished tasks will be handed off.
- Offer a seated route, paired observation, short task, and observation-only role before assigning work.

### April: compare moisture and access

- Compare west-bed observations with another bed rather than assuming all beds have the same need.
- If soil is dry below the surface, apply the decision rule for deep watering only after checking the shared barrel.
- Use the accessible raised bed's 30-minute watering option when it matches the observation and the participant's preference.
- Record a “monitor” decision when the soil check does not support watering.

### May: watch supports and shared capacity

- Look for plant-support needs, including tomatoes. The prior late-May observation is a prompt to look, not a date to act automatically.
- Offer paired staking or a short observation task; participation does not require tool use.
- Check whether two volunteers are available for the first-Saturday compost condition before putting compost on the task list.

### June–August: adjust to observations

- Recheck below-surface dryness when a care window is opened, with extra attention to the west beds.
- Coordinate any watering with the shared barrel; record “not available now” when that is the truth.
- Photograph and discuss unknown pests before action. Keep the record descriptive: location, visible signs, and date.
- Split work into short handoffs when a full task does not fit one person's time, mobility, or experience.

### September–October: maintain the record and share the load

- Revisit open worksheets and ask whether the recorded observation still holds.
- Close completed watering, support, and observation tasks; reassign or defer only with an explicit handoff.
- Continue to use paired, seated, short, and observation-only options. Do not infer that more volunteer availability exists than has been observed.

### November: close the season's learning loop

- Review which observations led to “do,” “monitor,” or “blocked” decisions.
- Record recurring local patterns without presenting them as guarantees for next year.
- If the first-Saturday compost condition is met, a pair may turn compost; otherwise record the condition as unmet and leave the task unassigned.
- Keep the worksheets as the next season's starting evidence, clearly labeled as observations from this year.

## 4. Participant activity: Observe–Decide–Handoff

**Purpose:** turn a real observation into one care decision that another person can understand.  
**Typical time:** 30–45 minutes, with shorter or observation-only participation welcome.  
**Group size:** one person, a pair, or a small group.  
**Output:** one worksheet per observation and a named next step or monitor trigger.

### Materials

- One blank worksheet per participant or pair (Section 5).
- Pencil or another way to record.
- A camera only if an unknown pest is observed.
- Access to the host's handoff list. No special tool is required for the observation role.

### Run the activity

1. **Choose a role (2 minutes).** Pick one: seated observer, paired observer, short task participant, or host recorder. A participant may change roles or stop at any time.
2. **Choose a focus (3 minutes).** Select one shared area or prompt: west-bed moisture, accessible-bed watering option, plant support, unknown pest, or monthly compost condition.
3. **Observe (10–15 minutes).** Write only what is seen or checked. Compare beds where useful. Check below-surface dryness before deciding about water. Do not turn an unknown into a diagnosis.
4. **Decide (5 minutes).** Choose exactly one: **do now**, **assign to a pair/partner**, **monitor**, or **blocked—handoff needed**. Use the decision rules in Section 2.
5. **Size the participation (3 minutes).** State the chosen task as seated, paired, short, or observation-only. A person who wants to water may use the accessible bed's 30-minute option. No one is assigned lifting or tool work by default.
6. **Record and hand off (5–10 minutes).** Complete the worksheet. If unfinished, identify the next role or trigger, not a vague request such as “someone should handle it.”
7. **Close the loop (2 minutes).** The host or recorder confirms where the worksheet lives and when the next check will occur. “Next check” can be a date, the next care window, or a condition such as “when the barrel is confirmed available.”

### Good decisions are small and testable

- “Monitor the west bed and recheck below-surface dryness at the next care window” is a complete decision.
- “Water the accessible shared bed for up to 30 minutes if the barrel is confirmed available; host records completion” is a complete conditional decision.
- “Photograph the unknown pest and discuss before action” is a complete decision.
- “Turn compost” is incomplete unless it is the first Saturday of the month and two people have chosen and can safely perform the task.

## 6. Completed fictional worked example

This is a fictional example for practice. It does not predict this year's weather, water supply, or plant needs.

**Worksheet ID:** SF-EX-01  **Date:** May 28 (worked example only)  **Time/window:** short morning care window

**Observer(s):** Seated observer with a paired garden check

**Area or shared crop:** Accessible raised bed, shared crops; compare west bed nearby

**Chosen role:** seated observer + paired observer

**Focus:** accessible-bed watering and west-bed moisture

**What did we directly observe or check?** The west bed was dry below the surface during the check. The accessible raised bed was also dry below the surface. The shared barrel was checked and was available for this one task at the time of observation. A nearby tomato was leaning and appeared to need support; this is a current observation, not a guaranteed seasonal date.

**Below-surface soil check:** dry  
**How checked / what remains unknown:** checked below the surface in both areas; future rainfall and later barrel availability are unknown.

**Shared barrel status:** confirmed available for this task now  
**Notes:** availability is not promised for a later window.

**Pest observation:** none

**Compost condition:** not relevant

**Decision:** **Assign to a pair/partner.** One participant will take the accessible raised bed's 30-minute watering option, if still available when the pair begins; the paired partner will confirm completion. The tomato support need is recorded as a separate short paired task so watering is not made dependent on staking.

**Participation size:** paired; up to 30 minutes for watering; short paired option for support

**Handoff owner or role:** host recorder adds “tomato support—paired, short” to the handoff list; the watering pair records whether the barrel remained available when they began.

**What must be confirmed before action?** Barrel availability at task start and the participant's chosen role. No lifting or tool use is required of the observer.

**Follow-through check:** next agreed care window or earlier if the host receives a completion note  
**Status:** open at worksheet creation

**Follow-through note:** Close the watering item only after the pair reports completion. Leave the tomato item open until a pair accepts it; if no pair is available, retain it as an explicit handoff rather than assigning it to an unavailable person.

## 7. Facilitator instructions

The facilitator runs the activity; the host owns the shared task list and follow-through. One person may hold both roles for a small care window.

### Before the care window: 10–15 minutes

1. Select one or two observation prompts from the seasonal map.
2. Print or share worksheets and identify the handoff location.
3. State the boundary: routine care only, not disaster response; no medical, emergency, or pesticide guidance is being supplied.
4. Prepare role choices: seated, paired, short, and observation-only.
5. Check whether the window is the first Saturday and whether two people have chosen compost work. If not, do not put compost turning on the active task list.

### During the care window: 30–45 minutes

- Start with observation, not an assumed task.
- Ask “What did we check?” before asking “What should we do?”
- Require a below-surface check before a watering decision.
- Treat water availability as a current condition, not a promise.
- If a pest is unknown, pause action at the photo-and-discuss step.
- Separate tasks so a participant can complete an observation without accepting lifting, tools, or a long shift.
- Read back each handoff: area, task, condition, owner or role, and next check.

### After the care window: 10 minutes

- Collect or photograph the worksheet into the garden's agreed local record.
- Put unfinished work on the handoff list with a named role or trigger.
- Mark blocked items as blocked; do not silently convert them into promises.
- Tell participants how to report completion or a changed observation.

## 8. Host instructions and follow-through

The host maintains continuity between care windows without requiring a fixed program.

### Handoff board fields

For each open item, record:

`area | observation | decision | task size | condition before action | owner/role | next check | status`

Example:

`West beds | dry below surface | deep watering | short or paired | confirm shared barrel | watering pair | next care window | open`

### Follow-through timing

- **At task start:** the accepting participant or pair confirms the condition that matters, especially barrel availability.
- **By the end of the same care window:** the recorder marks completed, open, deferred, or blocked.
- **At the next agreed check:** the host asks whether the observation changed and either closes the item, revises the decision, or creates a new worksheet.
- **For an unknown pest:** keep the item in “discussion needed” until the discussion occurs; do not substitute a pesticide recommendation.
- **For compost:** only close as completed when the monthly first-Saturday condition and two-person condition were both true.

Follow-through may be a short message, a paper-board update, or a verbal report captured by the host. The package does not require a particular app or communication channel.

### Inclusive task menu

Hosts should offer choices rather than infer limitations or experience:

- seated observation and worksheet completion;
- paired below-surface check or paired watering;
- an up-to-30-minute accessible raised-bed watering option when the observation supports it;
- short support or recordkeeping task;
- photo and discussion handoff for an unknown pest;
- observation-only confirmation of a blocked task.

If a participant has only a few minutes, accept a partial observation and hand it off. If someone is new, pair them with a willing participant and explain the decision rule. If someone cannot or does not want to use a tool or lift, keep the worksheet and observation role fully valid.

## 9. Small community pilot

Run a bounded pilot with **4–6 willing participants**, **2–3 shared areas**, and **two observation windows within 14 days**. This is a trial of the worksheet and handoff loop, not a fixed seven-session program. A participant may join one window, both, or only contribute an observation.

### Pilot setup

- Choose one west-bed moisture observation, one accessible raised-bed option, and one additional prompt such as plant support or an unknown-pest photo if it naturally occurs.
- Name one host recorder for each window.
- Make seated, paired, short, and observation-only roles visible before starting.
- Do not schedule compost turning unless the first-Saturday and two-person conditions actually occur.

### Pilot review after the second window

Ask participants and the host:

1. Did an observation lead to a clear do, assign, monitor, or blocked decision?
2. Could a person with less time, mobility, or experience take a valid role?
3. Did any handoff state its owner or trigger clearly enough for another person to act?
4. Did the guide avoid assuming water, volunteers, pest identity, or a date-based plant need?
5. Which worksheet field was missing, confusing, or unnecessary?

Keep the worksheet if it supports clear decisions. Revise only the confusing field or handoff rule, then run another small trial when a useful seasonal observation occurs.

## 10. Agent checks

An assisting agent may help prepare or review a worksheet, but it must not replace the gardener's observation or silently turn uncertainty into an action. Before presenting a decision, run these checks:

1. **Scope check:** Is this routine seasonal care from March through November, rather than disaster response?
2. **Source check:** Is each local fact traceable to the supplied garden facts, local sources, or an explicitly labeled current observation?
3. **Unknowns check:** Are rainfall, pest pressure, volunteer availability, and water availability treated as unknown or current conditions rather than promises?
4. **Decision check:** Does the worksheet contain one of do now, assign, monitor, or blocked—handoff needed?
5. **Access check:** Is there a seated, paired, short, or observation-only route, with no required lifting or tool use?
6. **Handoff check:** Does unfinished work have an owner or role, a condition or trigger, and a next check?
7. **Pest boundary check:** For an unknown pest, does the output stop at photograph and discussion before action, with no pesticide recommendation?
8. **Compost check:** Is turning limited to the first Saturday of the month when two people can work safely?
9. **Water check:** Does watering depend on a below-surface dry observation and a confirmed shared-barrel status?
10. **No overclaim check:** Are last year's tomato-staking observation and every worked example clearly labeled as context or fiction, not a forecast?

If a check fails, the agent should return the worksheet for clarification or mark it blocked. It should not invent a date, diagnosis, water supply, volunteer, or treatment.

## 11. Completion condition

This package is in use when a facilitator can open a care window from Section 4, a participant can complete Section 5, a host can maintain the handoff fields in Section 8, and the agent checks in Section 10 can be applied without adding unsupported advice. The seasonal cycle is complete when each open worksheet is closed, deliberately deferred with a visible condition, or retained as an explicit unresolved handoff for the next care window.
