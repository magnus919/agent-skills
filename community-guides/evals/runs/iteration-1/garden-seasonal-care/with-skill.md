# South Fork Community Garden: Seasonal Care Guide

**Edition:** 1.0 — prepared 2026-09-15  
**Use:** March through November, repeated when observations or seasonal conditions call for it  
**Community:** Fictional 22-plot South Fork Community Garden  
**Delivery:** One repeatable seasonal-care huddle, an optional work period, and a paper catch-up route  
**Status:** Ready for a community pilot; not participant-tested

## 1. Scope and boundary

This is a routine horticulture and shared-stewardship guide. It helps gardeners
notice ordinary changes, choose a proportionate care action, and hand off a task
with a check-back. It is not disaster response, an emergency plan, medical
guidance, or pesticide-use guidance. It does not tell anyone to diagnose illness,
use a pesticide, lift a load, or respond to an emergency. Unknown pests are
photographed and discussed before any action, consistent with the garden's
supplied practice.

The guide is designed to work without a guaranteed water supply, a guaranteed
volunteer roster, internet access, new purchases, or a fixed seven-session
program. The host chooses when to run a huddle based on the season, a change in
the beds, or a handoff that needs practice.

## 2. What the guide enables

By the end of one huddle, a participant or pair can:

1. Record an observation without turning it into an unsupported diagnosis.
2. Choose one of four care decisions: act now, schedule a recheck, hand off, or
   discuss before acting.
3. Shape the next action to fit available time, mobility, tools, and experience.
4. Record who has actually accepted a shared task, what remains unfilled, and
   when the result will be checked.

The usable output is a completed **Observe → Decide → Handoff worksheet** and,
where appropriate, one line in the shared garden care log. A participant may keep
the worksheet private and share only the task decision and check-back needed for
the garden.

## 4. How to use this package

The host runs the same huddle whenever a seasonal care decision is useful. The
huddle may be used in March for a baseline walk, before a late-May support check,
after a dry spell, after rain when the barrel may refill, at a first-Saturday
compost check, or in November for a final observation and handoff review. These
are examples of triggers, not a required schedule.

Each huddle has three parts:

1. **Observe:** a quiet, accessible walk or observation from a chosen point.
2. **Decide:** complete the worksheet and choose an action based on evidence.
3. **Handoff:** accept, decline, or leave a task unfilled and set a check-back.

An optional 30-minute work period follows only when the task is accepted and its
prerequisites are present. It is not required for the huddle to be complete.

## 5. Participant activity: Observe → Decide → Handoff

### Purpose

This activity converts a real garden observation into a small, checkable care
decision. It is not a gardening quiz. A first-time gardener may record what they
see; an experienced gardener may help interpret the options without overriding
the observer's record.

### Materials

- One blank worksheet per participant or pair (Section 6).
- Pencil or other writing method; a clipboard or firm surface if useful.
- Optional camera for an unknown pest observation. A camera is not required.
- Optional shared garden care log. Do not put private contact details or health
  information in it.
- No purchase or tool is required for the observation portion.

### Steps (about 25 minutes)

1. **Choose a zone and observation position (2 minutes).** Choose the west beds,
   accessible raised bed, compost area, or another agreed garden area. Use a
   seated or paired observation if standing or walking is not a fit. A participant
   may observe from the edge and may pass on a task.
2. **Look before deciding (5 minutes).** Record what is directly observable:
   soil condition below the surface where appropriate, plant support or leaning,
   visible damage or an unfamiliar pest, recent rain, and the state of a shared
   resource such as the barrel. Do not diagnose a pest or promise water.
3. **Name the decision type (3 minutes).** Select one:
   - **Act now:** the observed condition and prerequisites support a small task.
   - **Schedule a recheck:** evidence is incomplete or conditions may change.
   - **Hand off:** the observer has a clear task but needs another person's help,
     time, access, or experience.
   - **Discuss before acting:** an unfamiliar pest or another uncertain issue
     needs the garden's agreed discussion before action.
4. **Fit the action to real capacity (5 minutes).** Select one or more shapes:
   10-minute observation, seated task, paired task, 30-minute watering option at
   the accessible raised bed, short visit, or observation-only contribution. No
   one is required to lift compost or use a tool. Record whether the action is
   accepted, declined, or still unfilled.
5. **Write the handoff (7 minutes).** Name the task in plain language, record the
   prerequisite, the person who actually accepted it if any, the fallback, and a
   check-back date or trigger. Attendance or being nearby is not acceptance.
6. **Share selectively (3 minutes).** Share the garden-relevant decision, not
   private circumstances. A participant may share verbally, hand over the
   worksheet, use a pair partner, or ask the host to record only the task line.

### Decision rule

Use the observation to select the smallest useful next step:

| Observation | Proportionate decision |
|---|---|
| Soil is dry below the surface and water is available in the shared barrel | Offer or accept a watering task; record the barrel state and a recheck. If water is unavailable, do not promise it; record a later check or handoff. |
| Soil is not dry below the surface | Do not assign watering from this observation; schedule a recheck if weather or bed conditions make that useful. |
| A plant is leaning or support appears insufficient | Record the visible support need. Offer a short or paired support task; use the late-May log only as a prompt, not a guarantee. |
| Unknown pest or unfamiliar damage | Photograph if useful and discuss before action. Do not select a pesticide or treatment from this guide. |
| It is the first Saturday and two people have accepted compost turning | Record the accepted two-person task and its check-back. If two people have not accepted it, leave it unfilled and record when availability will be checked. |
| The observer cannot or does not want to do the physical task | Keep the observation contribution, choose observation-only or handoff, and do not treat the limitation as a failure. |

## 8. Facilitator / host instructions

### Preparation (before the huddle)

1. Choose the trigger and a meeting point that permits an edge-of-bed or seated
   observation. Do not require a full garden walk.
2. Print one worksheet per participant or pair, plus two extras. Put the key
   decision rule and the boundary statement on the first page if worksheets are
   distributed separately.
3. Identify the observation zones available that day: west beds, accessible
   raised bed, compost area, or another agreed area. Do not assume a zone is
   reachable for everyone.
4. Check which shared facts can be observed that day: barrel state, recent rain,
   visible support needs, and whether it is the first Saturday. Do not fill in
   rainfall, pest pressure, or volunteer availability from assumption.
5. Bring pencils and a firm writing surface. A camera is optional. No tool,
   lifting, treatment product, or purchase is needed to run the huddle.
6. Decide who will hold the optional shared-log line. Ownership is unconfirmed
   unless a person accepts it. If no one accepts, keep worksheets with
   participants and announce the next check-back verbally or on paper.
7. Offer language or access support if the garden has it, but do not claim that
   an interpreter or helper is available unless that person has accepted.

### 55-minute run sheet

| Activity | Minutes | Host instructions and completion check |
|---|---:|---|
| Arrival and access choice | 5 | Offer seated, paired, short, or observation-only participation. Ask each person to choose a zone or edge position. Do not ask for private health information. Completion: everyone has a participation option or may pass. |
| Boundary and purpose | 4 | Say: “Today we are doing routine garden observation and handoff. This is not emergency response, medical guidance, or pesticide instruction. We will record what we see, choose a small decision, and leave uncertain issues for discussion.” Completion: participants can state that observation is enough to participate. |
| Brief demonstration | 6 | Use the fictional worked example from Section 7. Point out observation versus diagnosis, acceptance versus attendance, and a fallback. Completion: participants can name one unresolved dependency in the example. |
| Quiet observation | 8 | Participants observe from their selected position, alone or paired. Invite notes on below-surface dryness where appropriate, support, visible unfamiliar damage, and shared resource state. Offer host or pair assistance with writing. Completion: each participant has at least one direct observation or chooses observation-only. |
| Worksheet decision | 12 | Participants complete Sections 1–10 of the worksheet. Prompt: “What is the smallest useful next decision: act, recheck, hand off, or discuss first?” Circulate without taking over. Completion: one decision type and one check-back are recorded. |
| Handoff circle or paired share | 10 | Invite each person to share only the garden-relevant line. Ask: “Has anyone actually accepted this task?” Record accepted, declined, or unfilled. Match by time and task shape, not by assumed capacity. Completion: every shared task has an owner or explicit fallback. |
| Optional-work decision | 3 | Ask whether an accepted task has its prerequisite and whether the participant wants the optional work period. If not, stop at the worksheet; the huddle is complete. Completion: no task begins on an unconfirmed prerequisite. |
| Closing and catch-up route | 7 | Read back decisions, unresolved dependencies, unfilled roles, and the check-back trigger. Tell absent participants they may use the worksheet at a later observation and share only the task line. Completion: host identifies who holds any shared record and when it will be revisited. |

**Total: 55 minutes.** The optional work period is outside the huddle and lasts
up to 30 minutes for the bounded watering option or another accepted short task.
It is not silently added to the run time.

### Host prompts

- “What did you see directly?”
- “What is still unknown?”
- “Which decision fits: act now, recheck, hand off, or discuss first?”
- “What task shape fits today: seated, paired, short, 30-minute watering, or observation-only?”
- “Who has actually accepted this task? If nobody has, what is the fallback?”
- “What will tell us to check again?”

### If the session gets stuck

- **Confusion:** reread the fictional example and ask the participant to change
  only its zone, observation, or decision. Do not add a technical diagnosis.
- **Time pressure:** omit the optional work-period discussion and preserve the
  worksheet decision, acceptance status, fallback, and closing check-back.
- **Disagreement:** record the different observations or needs and the unresolved
  question. Do not write consensus that was not reached.
- **No helper, tool, or water:** keep the observation, mark the dependency, and
  use a recheck, discussion, or observation-only fallback.
- **Participant missed the huddle:** provide the blank worksheet and invite a
  10-minute observation from an accessible position. They can hand the host only
  the shared-log line, or keep the worksheet private.

## 9. Task menu and handoff rules

Use the menu to make effort and access visible without treating any option as a
lesser contribution.

| Contribution | Typical time | Access / experience fit | Output |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Observation-only note | 5–10 min | Seated, edge-of-bed, remote-from-zone if the host can relay the observation; beginner-friendly | One direct observation and a proposed decision |
| Paired observation | 10–15 min | Useful when writing, walking, or interpretation is easier with a partner | Confirmed observation and an open question |
| Short support recheck | Up to 15 min | Pair or experienced/beginner combination; no one is required to use a tool | Report whether support is needed and what remains unknown |
| Accessible raised-bed watering option | Up to 30 min | Bounded shared-crop task; confirm water availability first | Watering task completed or marked unavailable, plus recheck |
| Shared-barrel availability check | 5–10 min | Observation-only; no promise of supply | Barrel state recorded for the decision |
| Compost-turning readiness check | 5–10 min | Observation/coordination only unless two accepted people can work safely | First-Saturday status: two accepted / not accepted / recheck |

For every handoff, the host or participant records:

1. the exact small task;
2. the prerequisite, such as water availability or two accepted people;
3. an acceptance status of accepted, declined, or unfilled;
4. the fallback if the task does not happen; and
5. the date or observation trigger for checking the result.

No one is appointed because they are absent, nearby, experienced, or attending.
No gardener is required to lift compost or use a tool. If a task needs a tool or
physical action, it remains optional and must have a participant-accepted shape
or an observation-only fallback.

## 10. Observation-based seasonal adjustments

This table is a routing aid, not a calendar of guaranteed conditions.

| When or signal | Host adjustment | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| March baseline or first huddle | Use one quiet observation in each available zone; establish the shared-log format and recheck triggers. | This year's rainfall, availability, and plant condition. |
| West beds look dry | Ask for below-surface observation before selecting watering. Check the shared barrel, then offer the 30-minute accessible-bed option or another accepted bounded task. | Whether water is available and whether another check is needed. |
| Recent rain | Do not assume watering is needed. Recheck below-surface condition before assigning it; record barrel status only if observed. | Actual moisture in each selected bed. |
| Late May prompt | Use last year's tomato-staking note to look for visible support needs. Record this year's observation; do not predict staking or assign it automatically. | Current plant condition, materials, and accepted pair. |
| Unknown pest or unfamiliar damage | Take an optional photo and bring the observation to the garden's discussion. Keep the decision at “discuss before acting.” | Identification and any later action are outside this guide. |
| First Saturday of a month | Check whether two people have accepted compost turning. If not, mark the task unfilled and schedule a check; do not treat the calendar date as sufficient. | Volunteer acceptance and ability to work safely that day. |
| November closeout | Use observation-only or short tasks to record what needs checking before the garden's next cycle. Do not invent winter procedures. | Next cycle's conditions and any future plan. |

## 11. Follow-through and record ownership

At the close of each huddle, the host makes a short action record:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Individual action | One participant-chosen observation, recheck, or accepted task and an achievable time/trigger. |
| Shared action | Exact task and accepted owner(s), or **unfilled**. |
| Dependency | Water, second person, existing material, discussion, or another fact that must be confirmed. |
| Fallback | Observation-only, later recheck, pair request at the next huddle, or leave unfilled. |
| Check-back | A date or trigger such as “after the next rain” or “before the next huddle.” |
| Record holder | Named only if the person accepts. Otherwise participants retain their copies and the host announces the check-back. |

The shared log should contain only garden-task information. Do not collect names,
health information, household vulnerabilities, or private contact details in this
guide or its example. If a participant later wants a private follow-up, the host
should arrange it separately and only with that participant's permission.

## 12. Proposed small community pilot

This is a proposal for local review, not an invitation sent by this package.

Run one 55-minute huddle with 2–4 gardeners representing different mobility,
time, and experience preferences, plus the optional 30-minute work period only if
participants accept it. The pilot is one bounded test of this activity, not a
seven-session program and not evidence for every future huddle.

### Pilot setup

- Use one real observation zone chosen on the day.
- Provide printed worksheets and a paper shared-log line.
- Offer seated, paired, short, 30-minute watering, and observation-only routes.
- Ask for volunteers to accept or decline the shared-log role and any task; do
  not assign absent or silent people.
- Keep unknown pest observations at “photograph and discuss before action.”

### Pilot evidence to collect

With permission, record only the activity-level evidence below; do not retain
private circumstances in the shared guide record.

| Check | Evidence of a usable pilot |
|---|---|
| Worksheet completion | Each participant or pair can record an observation, a decision type, and a check-back without extra technical explanation. |
| Decision quality | The group distinguishes direct observation from diagnosis and chooses a proportionate action or recheck. |
| Access and time fit | Participants can select a task shape that fits their available time and mobility, including observation-only. |
| Handoff clarity | Each shared task is accepted or explicitly unfilled, with a prerequisite and fallback. |
| Follow-through | At the agreed check-back, the host can say whether the task occurred, remained unfilled, or needs a new observation. |

Ask participants:

1. “Could you complete the worksheet without extra explanation?”
2. “What part of the observation or decision was unclear?”
3. “Did the task shapes include a meaningful option for your time and access needs?”
4. “Was it clear who accepted the task and what would happen if it did not occur?”
5. “What should change before the next huddle?”

Obtain permission before recording or retaining feedback. Log each proposed
change as `observed barrier → proposed revision → check at next use`. A pilot
cannot establish participant-tested usability for the whole March–November
period; it only tests this huddle and the selected follow-through.

## 14. Maintenance

- **Maintainer:** Unconfirmed; the garden should name a person only after they
  accept the role.
- **Review trigger:** After the pilot, after any material change in garden
  practice, or when repeated huddles reveal a barrier or unfilled dependency.
- **Review focus:** This year's observed access barriers, task acceptance,
  worksheet completion, follow-through, seasonal assumptions, and any change to
  the shared barrel, accessible bed, pest-discussion practice, or compost rule.
- **Feedback route:** Use the pilot questions in Section 12 or write a proposed
  change on the worksheet's back. Keep private details out of shared records.
- **Revision method:** Record `observed barrier → proposed revision → check at
  next use`; issue a new edition date and give participants the revised Markdown
  or printed copy.
- **Unresolved facts:** Rainfall, pest pressure, current plant condition, water
  availability, and volunteer availability remain unresolved until observed.
