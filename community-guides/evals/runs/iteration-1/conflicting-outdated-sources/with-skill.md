# Harbor View evacuation-alarm guide — verification-pending draft

**Audience:** Residents of the fictional 60-unit Harbor View apartment building  
**Purpose:** Help residents understand what is and is not currently established about where to go after a building evacuation alarm.  
**Draft status:** **Do not publish as a final location-specific instruction yet.**  
**Prepared:** 2026-09-15  
**Evidence boundary:** This draft uses only the supplied fictional source packet. It is not emergency authority and does not replace instructions given during an actual event.

## The decision in one minute

The older printed notice says to use the basement laundry room, but the newer building update says construction closed the basement and directs residents to the north parking-lot gate. That newer direction is the better current planning source, but it is not yet confirmed as usable today: two resident observations dated 2026-03-02 report that the north-gate sign is missing and a temporary fence sometimes blocks the route.

**Decision:** Pause publication of a final assembly-point instruction until all three checks below are completed:

1. Inspect the north-gate route and sign.
2. Confirm the current route and assembly point with the building's responsible person.
3. Ask residents with mobility needs to test or describe an accessible alternative.

Until those checks are complete, Harbor View should not present either the basement laundry room or the north parking-lot gate as a confirmed current assembly point.

## What residents should be told now

The following is proposed interim wording for approval by the building's responsible person. It is intentionally not a final assembly-point instruction.

> **Harbor View evacuation-alarm update — location being verified**
>
> The old printed notice naming the basement laundry room is out of date because the basement was closed for construction according to the newer building update dated 2026-02-14. That update names the north parking-lot gate, but residents reported on 2026-03-02 that the sign was missing and a temporary fence sometimes blocked the route.
>
> Harbor View is verifying the route, sign, and an accessible alternative before publishing a final assembly-point instruction. Do not rely on the old basement notice. Do not assume that the north-gate route is open until the verification is announced. During an alarm, follow current directions provided at the scene by the building's responsible person or emergency responders.
>
> A verified location and accessibility update will be issued after the checks are complete.

### What this wording does and does not claim

- It **does** preserve the dates and provenance of the conflicting sources.
- It **does** identify the basement notice as outdated for current planning because the later update reports that construction closed the basement.
- It **does not** claim that the north-gate route is always open or safe.
- It **does not** claim that an accessible alternative has been found.
- It **does not** claim that residents have reached consensus or that a responsible person has accepted the verification work.

If the responsible person cannot approve this interim wording, publication should pause rather than silently converting the unresolved north-gate route into a rule.

### Reconciliation rule

The sources can be reconciled only to this point:

1. The basement instruction is not safe to republish as current because it conflicts with the newer notice that construction closed the basement.
2. The north parking-lot gate is the latest stated destination in the packet.
3. The north-gate direction remains **unconfirmed in current conditions** because the latest resident observation reports a missing sign and intermittent obstruction, and the packet explicitly says no source confirms that the route is currently open and safe at all times.

The second and third points must remain visible together. The resident observation must not be overwritten by the newer management email, and the observation must not be exaggerated into a claim that the route is permanently blocked.

## Community pilot: two separate tests

The pilot must not be described as one general usability test. It has two distinct purposes, with different evidence.

### Pilot A — comprehension of the draft

**Purpose:** Determine whether residents understand the source status and the publication hold without extra explanation.

**Participants:** Invite a small, varied group of Harbor View residents, including people who did not see the original notices and people who prefer the available offline or accessible format. The packet does not identify languages, access needs, or attendance constraints; these must be checked before inviting.

**Give participants:** The proposed interim wording and the source-check card. Do not give the facilitator's reconciliation explanation first.

**Ask each participant privately or in pairs:**

1. What location, if any, does this draft say is confirmed right now?
2. Which instruction should not be reused, and why?
3. What is the status of the north-gate route?
4. What must happen before a final location-specific notice is published?
5. What part of the wording is difficult to find, interpret, or access?

**Comprehension success criteria:** A participant can say, in their own words, that no final location is confirmed; the basement notice is outdated for current planning; the north gate is provisional because access is unresolved; and the three verification checks are still required. Record misunderstandings and proposed wording changes, not residents' personal details.

**Limit:** This test shows whether the draft is understandable to the people who took part. It does not prove that the route is open or that the draft is suitable for every resident.

### Pilot B — actual route and accessibility conditions

**Purpose:** Test the physical implementation gap identified by the March observation: route access, visible direction, and an accessible alternative.

**Precondition:** Obtain responsible-person approval for the inspection and establish a safe observation method. Do not ask residents to enter a blocked or unsafe route. If the responsible person or safe access is unavailable, record the physical check as not completed and keep publication paused.

**Observe and record:**

- Whether the north parking-lot route is open at the inspection time.
- Whether a resident can identify the route without relying on the old basement notice.
- Whether the sign is present, readable, and located where the route decision is needed.
- Whether the temporary fence changes the route or blocks it at the inspection time.
- Whether a resident with mobility needs can use the route, or can describe a workable alternative, without being pressured to disclose private health information.
- Whether conditions vary by time or access arrangement; one successful observation is not proof that the route is always open.

**Physical-test success criteria:** The responsible person has a dated record of route and sign condition; the assembly point is described precisely enough to communicate; an accessible alternative is accepted by the people who need it or remains explicitly unresolved; and any time-dependent fence condition is stated rather than hidden.

**Limit:** This is evidence about the observed conditions and participants. It does not establish permanent safety, emergency authority, or universal accessibility.

## Facilitator notes for the verification-focused pilot

### Prepare

- Print the interim wording and source-check card, or provide an equivalent accessible offline copy.
- Keep the three source IDs and dates available for the facilitator, but do not overload the resident-facing page with process notes.
- Arrange competent language/access support if needed; no such arrangement is supplied by the packet.
- Obtain permission from the responsible person before any physical route observation.
- Prepare a private way to record barriers without collecting names or sensitive details unless separately authorized.

### Run the comprehension session

| Activity | Minutes | Completion check |
|---|---:|---|
| Welcome and consent to anonymous feedback | 5 | Participants know this is a draft comprehension check, not an emergency drill. |
| Silent read or supported read | 5 | Each participant has the same interim wording. |
| Private source-check card | 8 | Participants identify the old, new, and unresolved claims. |
| Paired or small-group questions | 10 | Facilitator records misunderstandings and access barriers. |
| Debrief and next action | 7 | Group knows the physical check is separate and publication remains paused. |

If time is short, cut discussion before cutting the private comprehension check and closing explanation.

### Run the physical check

Use a separate observation record. Do not treat a resident's report as permission to inspect, and do not appoint a resident to a role they have not accepted. If no accepted inspector or mobility participant is available, leave that field unresolved and keep the gate closed.

## Decisions and open questions

### Decisions already supported by the packet

- Do not republish the 2023 basement laundry-room instruction as current.
- Treat the 2026 north-gate direction as the leading but provisional planning direction.
- Pause the final location-specific guide until the packet's minimum verification checks are complete.
- Keep the source dates and the resident observation visible in the guide and verification record.

### Questions that must be answered before release

1. Who is the building's responsible person for confirming the route, and have they accepted that role?
2. When was the north-gate route last inspected after the 2026-03-02 observation?
3. Is the sign now present and readable? If not, what exact replacement direction will residents see?
4. Does the temporary fence block or redirect the route at particular times?
5. What exact place beyond or at the north parking-lot gate is the assembly point?
6. What accessible alternative works for residents with mobility needs, and who has confirmed it?
7. How will the old basement notice be controlled so residents do not receive two competing instructions?
8. Which communication formats and language/access arrangements are required for all 60 units?
9. What event will trigger the next review: construction change, route obstruction, missing sign, resident report, or a scheduled date?
