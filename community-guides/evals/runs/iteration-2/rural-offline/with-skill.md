# Willow County Offline-First Household Guide

**A runnable paper-and-radio communication rehearsal for the fictional Willow County valley**  
**Edition:** draft 1 · 2026-09-15  
**Audience:** 40 households in a fictional unincorporated valley  
**Use case:** a multi-day power or communications outage, including up to 48 hours without internet or cellular service

## What this package is

This is a household activity and host package. It helps each household make, write down, and practice a communication plan that still has a chance of working when phones, internet, cars, or steady electricity do not.

It is not an emergency response plan, medical-care plan, shelter listing, evacuation plan, dispatch service, or promise that any channel reaches every home. The supplied fixture does not establish local emergency coverage, a staffed check-in service, or a guaranteed accessible meeting place. Leave those items for local verification rather than filling them with assumptions.

The package is designed to be usable on paper. A household may participate by writing, drawing, dictating to a partner, or completing a table-top rehearsal. No one must disclose medical details, accept a volunteer role, travel to the grange hall, own a car or smartphone, or rely on constant electricity.

## The local facts this draft uses

These are **supplied fictional facts**, not independently verified facts:

| Channel or constraint | What the fixture says | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|

| Large print | The library can print 60 large-print cards before its backup battery is exhausted. | The capacity and timing must be checked before publication; do not promise that every household can obtain a new copy during an outage. |

| Phone tree | A volunteer coordinator owns a phone tree. | It works only while the phone network operates, so it is a pre-outage or service-return fallback, not the outage core. No coordinator role is assumed to be accepted for this guide. |

## What is not yet verified

Before this guide is published as a local operating agreement, the community still needs to verify:

1. Whether the WLV-88.4 schedule is current and whether the signal can actually be heard at representative valley-floor homes.
2. Whether the library porch box is reachable and whether the stated large-print capacity is still available when cards are needed.
3. Which households want a check-in, what paper status-mark location each household has consented to use, and which information they do **not** want shared.
4. Which accessible locations are currently usable, including whether the grange hall can be used by anyone who chooses it. No one is assigned to travel there by this guide.
5. Which people, if any, have explicitly accepted a relay or host role, and what their backup is. Attendance or goodwill is not acceptance.
6. Whether households have a battery radio, paper, a pencil, and a way to keep the paper dry; these are inventory questions, not requirements to purchase.
7. Any local emergency route or professional support. The fixture establishes none. A blank field for a household's independently verified route is included below; this guide does not supply one.

## The household plan in one page

Each household completes Worksheet **W-01** and keeps its copy where it can be found without internet. The plan has four parts:

1. **Paper first:** keep the quick card and a blank status slip at home. If the household consents, use its agreed status-mark location. A mark says only what the household chooses to say; it is not a medical report.
2. **Second channel:** choose one independent option that the household can realistically receive: a battery-radio announcement if reception and schedule are verified, a consented in-person exchange that does not require the household to reach the grange hall, or another locally verified paper arrangement. Do not count the phone tree during a cellular outage.
3. **Access choice:** record “we can travel,” “we cannot travel,” or “we will decide case by case.” A mobility aid, no car, weather, heating, or safety concern is enough reason to choose a home-based plan. No explanation is required on a shared copy.
4. **Check and revise:** after a rehearsal or real outage, record what was actually possible. “No contact received” is not proof that a household is safe or unsafe; it is a reason to use only a verified next step.

## Participant activity: Paper status and fallback rehearsal

**Module ID:** M-01  
**Capability and completion:** A participant can create a consented paper status action, name a second channel with its limitation, and rehearse receiving or relaying a short message without internet, cellular service, a car, a smartphone, or continuous electricity. Completion is a filled W-01 plus one observed table-top or paper rehearsal.

**Duration:** 60 minutes for the activity; use the 90-minute host agenda below for a complete session.  
**Materials:** one printed quick card and W-01 per household, blank paper slips, pencils, large-print copies, tape or envelopes if available, a battery radio only if one is already available, and a visible timer. Nothing requires charging.

### Scenario

The power and cellular network have been unavailable for 48 hours. Internet is unavailable. A household has no car and may not be able to reach the ridge. The radio may or may not reach the valley floor. The household needs to communicate one short status without publishing a medical detail. Decide what you can actually do from your home, then name what remains unknown.

### Steps for participants

1. **Choose privately.** On W-01, choose a paper status location only if the household agrees to use it. It may be a place at or near home; do not write a private detail on the shared table. Choose “can travel,” “cannot travel,” or “case by case.” Passing is allowed.
2. **Choose the second channel.** Select radio, a consented in-person exchange, or another locally verified paper route. Write its limitation beside it. If you choose the phone tree, mark it **service-return only** because the scenario has no cellular service.
3. **Write a short status slip.** Use no more than one sentence. Examples: “Please make contact through our second channel” or “We cannot travel to the optional location.” Do not write a diagnosis, medication list, or private telephone number.
4. **Rehearse without walking.** Fold the slip and place it at the chosen location, or simulate placement on the table. If the household cannot travel, keep the entire rehearsal at home. A partner or host may read the fictional slip only with permission. Do not make an actual public mark during the session unless the household explicitly wants to and the location is safe.
5. **Test the fallback.** The partner asks: “What would you do if the paper channel is missed?” The participant answers using only the written plan. If radio is selected and a battery radio is present, tune or simulate the two stated announcement times; do not claim reception from a demonstration in another location. If the radio is absent, mark that fact and use the paper alternative.
6. **Check the plan.** Circle one result: **works on paper**, **blocked by a local unknown**, or **needs a different plan**. Write one next action and the date or event that will trigger a review.

### Debrief questions

- Which part could you do with the lights, internet, cellular network, and car unavailable?
- Which step depended on a fact we have not verified?
- Could a person who cannot travel to the grange hall still complete the plan?
- Did the plan accidentally ask anyone to provide professional emergency care or reveal private information?
- Who accepted a follow-through action, and what is the fallback if that person is unavailable?

## Completed fictional worked example

This is a fictional example for learning, not a roster or a claim about a real Willow County household.

| Prompt | Example response |
|---|---|
| Household name or private label | **Pine Bend household** |
| Paper status location, with consent | A covered sleeve beside our front door; only our chosen neighbor may look |
| Who may read or carry a status slip? | Jo has agreed to read it if already nearby; no one is assigned to travel to us |
| Access choice | Cannot travel to the ridge; case by case for a nearby exchange |
| Second channel | Battery radio, if our valley-floor reception is confirmed; paper exchange is the backup |
| Limitation | Radio requires batteries and may not reach our home; reception is not checked yet |
| Radio at home | Available; batteries to be checked before the session ends |
| Schedule/reception check | Unfilled — confirm WLV-88.4 at 08:00 and 18:00 from this home |
| Phone tree use | Service-return only; not used in the 48-hour no-cellular scenario |

**Status slip:** “Please make contact through our second channel.”  
**Placement rule:** Place it in the covered sleeve only if the household has first consented and the message contains no private detail.  
**Rehearsal result:** The paper action worked at the table. Radio reception and the identity of any relay person remain unverified.  
**Accepted next action:** Pine Bend household will check batteries and record whether WLV-88.4 is heard at both stated times.  
**Fallback:** If no radio is heard, the household keeps the paper plan and does not infer that no announcement occurred. An accessible, consented nearby exchange must be verified separately.  
**Review trigger:** After the reception check and after the next outage rehearsal.

## Host and facilitator instructions

### Prepare before participants arrive

- Print one quick card and one W-01 per household, plus spare blank paper. Provide large-print copies if available; do not promise that the library can produce them until its battery capacity is checked.
- Bring pencils, tape or envelopes if available, a timer, and one battery radio only if one is already on hand. Do not design the session around a working outlet, Wi-Fi, a smartphone, or a projector.
- Choose a room or porch arrangement that does not require travel to the grange hall. If the grange hall is proposed, describe it as optional and accessibility-dependent. Offer a table-top/home-based route for every activity.
- Prepare a private way to ask whether a household wants a check-in. Do not create a public medical or vulnerability list. Participation does not create consent to share a location or contact detail.
- Bring the fictional scenario and the completed Pine Bend example. Do not bring a presumed volunteer roster or present the fictional channels as verified.
- Before local publication, obtain the two targeted checks: radio schedule/reception and library battery/card capacity. Record the result and date in the maintenance section.

**If a helper or material is unavailable:** The host reads the scenario aloud, participants work alone or with a chosen partner, and the host marks the missing item as untested. The activity still works with paper and pencil alone.

### 90-minute run-of-show

| Time | Activity | Host instructions and completion check |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Arrival, access, and consent | Offer writing, drawing, dictation, partner help, and passing. State that no one must disclose medical details, accept a role, or travel. Ask privately who wants a check-in; do not publish the answer. |
| 10–20 min | Scope and local facts | Read the “What this package is” warning and the channel table. Say clearly that the phone tree does not work during a cellular outage and that the grange hall is optional. Ask participants to mark any fact they think needs checking. |
| 20–32 min | Private household planning | Participants complete W-01 Parts A–B. Walk around only to clarify fields; do not read private answers without permission. Completion check: each household has either a paper action or a marked reason it cannot yet choose one. |
| 32–47 min | Paper status rehearsal | Read the scenario. Each household writes a short slip and simulates placement or receipt without walking. A mobility or heating constraint is handled at the table. Completion check: a slip exists, its sharing condition is explicit, and no private medical detail is present. |
| 47–60 min | Fallback test | Participants select a second channel and write its limitation. If a battery radio is available, use it only as a demonstration; do not claim valley-floor reception. Completion check: W-01 names a fallback or records the unknown blocking it. |
| 60–70 min | Paired debrief | With permission, pairs ask the five debrief questions. A participant may pass or debrief privately. Record barriers, not names or diagnoses. |
| 70–82 min | Shared decisions and acceptance | Ask for only actions people volunteer to accept: for example, checking a radio from a specific home or asking the library about capacity. Write accepted, asking, and unfilled separately. Do not appoint absent people. |
| 82–88 min | Catch-up and follow-through | Explain the catch-up route below. Each household completes W-01 Part C with one actor, one action, and one review trigger. |
| 88–90 min | Close | Repeat unresolved facts, privacy rules, and the next review trigger. Return every household's worksheet to its owner. Say whether the group is ready for a pilot, not that it has proven coverage. |

### Host language for the unfamiliar exercise

“We are not trying to predict who is safe. We are practicing one short, consented paper action and naming the limits of the next channel. If a step requires a car, a smartphone, a working phone network, constant electricity, or travel you cannot make, mark that limitation and choose a different plan. A missing message is an unknown, not reassurance.”

### If the session gets stuck

- **Confusion:** Read the Pine Bend example and ask participants to change one field for their own circumstances.
- **Time pressure:** Cut the optional radio demonstration first. Keep the private worksheet, paper rehearsal, acceptance check, and closing review trigger.
- **Disagreement:** Record both needs and the unresolved question. Do not write a consensus or assign a shared location until consent and accessibility are confirmed.
- **No accessible room or helper:** Run the entire session by paper at home or in small pairs. No one needs to attend the grange hall.
- **No paper copies:** Hand-copy the field labels onto any available paper. The essential action is the sequence, not the printed design.
- **A participant asks for emergency coverage:** State that this fixture establishes no local emergency route or professional care. Leave the household's independently verified route blank rather than inventing one.

### Close and follow-through

The host keeps only an aggregate action log unless a household separately consents to a named record. The household keeps W-01. For every shared action, record:

| Action | Actor | Acceptance | Dependency | Fallback | Review trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Check WLV-88.4 schedule and valley-floor reception | __________________ | accepted / asking / unfilled | Battery radio and two observation times | Mark radio unknown; keep paper plan | After both time checks |
| Confirm library porch-box access and large-print capacity | __________________ | accepted / asking / unfilled | Library confirmation and battery status | Use hand-copied cards; do not promise stock | Before publication or next card run |
| Gather consented household paper locations | __________________ | accepted / asking / unfilled | Household choice and privacy | No shared roster; households use private plans | Before any real check-in |
| Check accessible in-person options | __________________ | accepted / asking / unfilled | Current access information | No required travel; use home-based rehearsal | Before naming a meeting location |

**Catch-up route for absent households:** Give the household a quick card, W-01, and the Pine Bend example through a consented paper handoff or another locally verified route. A host explains the six participant steps in person or leaves them as printed instructions. The absent household returns only the fields it wants shared. No one is assigned by default because they missed the meeting.

**Follow-through check:** At the stated review trigger, ask: “Did the paper action work as written? Which channel was actually available? What barrier prevented participation? What should change?” Preserve “not tested” and “unknown” as valid results.
