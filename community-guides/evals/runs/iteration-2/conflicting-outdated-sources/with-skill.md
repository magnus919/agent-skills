# Harbor View evacuation-alarm guide

**Status: verification draft — pause publication as final resident guidance.**

This draft is for the fictional 60-unit Harbor View apartment building. It was
prepared only from the supplied fictional source packet. The packet says that
its notices, dates, and observations are fictional and are not emergency
authority.

## The decision residents need

The available sources do **not** yet confirm a currently open and safe route to
an assembly point. The building should verify the north parking-lot route and
an accessible alternative before distributing a final guide.

Do not publish the old basement instruction as current guidance. A newer
building update says that construction closed the basement. The proposed
replacement—the north parking-lot gate—has a current implementation concern:
two residents observed that its sign is missing and that a temporary fence
sometimes blocks the route.

### Reconciliation

The old and new destination claims cannot both be current. The February 14,
2026 management update is newer and specifically explains why the basement
instruction no longer applies. The north gate is therefore the building's
proposed replacement in the supplied sources.

The March 2 observations do not silently overturn that update, and they do not
prove a permanent closure. They do show that the replacement instruction may
not be implementable as written at all times. The unresolved question is whether
the north parking-lot route is currently open and safe, and what accessible
alternative is available.

## Publication gate

Publication should remain paused if any of these conditions is true:

1. The route and sign have not been inspected.
2. The inspection finds the north-gate route blocked, unsafe, or not reliably
   identifiable, and no replacement instruction has been verified.
3. The building's responsible person has not confirmed the current route.
4. Residents with mobility needs have not been asked to test or describe an
   accessible alternative, or that alternative remains unresolved.
5. The final wording would claim that the route is open or safe at all times
   when the evidence supports only a date- or condition-specific observation.

Publication may proceed only after the minimum verification is complete and the
resident wording accurately reflects the findings. If the north gate is not a
reliable destination, the building needs a verified replacement before this
guide can become final.

## Separate community pilot plan

The pilot should happen only after the building has decided that a supervised
route check is appropriate and has a responsible person for the verification.
It is separate from the agent source checks above and cannot replace the
publication gate.

### Pilot questions

Test two things separately:

1. **Comprehension:** Can residents understand which notice is outdated, what
   location the newer update proposes, and why this draft is still on hold?
2. **Actual conditions:** At the time of the supervised check, can residents
   identify the north-gate route and sign, and does the temporary fence block
   it? Can residents with mobility needs test or describe an accessible
   alternative?

### Pilot method

- Invite a small, representative group of residents; do not send the
  invitation or collect personal feedback as part of this draft.
- Give participants the resident-facing draft without adding an oral answer
  that the document does not contain.
- Ask each participant to state the proposed location, identify why the
  basement instruction is not current, and explain what remains unverified.
- In a separately authorized and supervised route check, record the date and
  observed sign, route, fence, and accessibility conditions. Do not turn one
  observation into a claim about all times.
- Record only the activity, observed barrier, proposed change, and subsequent
  check. Obtain permission before recording or retaining personal feedback.

### Pilot decision rule

If residents cannot answer the comprehension questions without extra
explanation, revise the resident wording and repeat that comprehension check.
If the route check finds a missing sign, blocking fence, or unresolved
accessible alternative, keep publication paused and return the finding to the
building's responsible person. A successful comprehension check shows that the
draft is understandable; it does not prove that the route is continuously open
or safe.

## Maintenance and ownership

The packet does not name the building's responsible person, a guide owner, or a
review date. Those ownership details must be assigned before publication. The
guide should be reviewed whenever construction changes, the route or sign
changes, the temporary fence changes, residents report a new access problem, or
the building changes its assembly-point instruction.
