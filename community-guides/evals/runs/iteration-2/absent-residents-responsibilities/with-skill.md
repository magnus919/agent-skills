# Oak Terrace: contact-board and seasonal-guide coordination

**For:** the fictional 18-household Oak Terrace block  
**Purpose:** keep a printed contact board current and offer every household a private way to say whether it received the seasonal guide.  
**Status:** draft for one community pilot; no role is operational until the named person explicitly accepts it.

## A short promise to residents

Participation is voluntary. Being away, hard to reach, busy, caregiving, disabled, or not responding does not mean a resident has agreed to help, declined to help, or consented to be contacted in a particular way. No one needs to explain why they cannot help.

The public board will show only practical coordination information that a person has agreed to share: the role, an approved contact route, the last update date, and—when useful—the next review date. It will not show an absence reason, diagnosis, household circumstance, or who did not reply.

Residents may:

- opt in to a small role, opt out, or ask for a later/private follow-up;
- choose paper, text, or another mutually agreed contact route when available;
- request an accessible format or other practical adjustment without naming a diagnosis;
- pass, change their mind, or ask for a substitute without giving a reason.

## How the map works

Each role has a **primary** and, where feasible, an **independent backup**. A person is not a primary or backup merely because they attended, received a note, answered once, or was listed as reachable. A role becomes confirmed only after the person says yes to:

1. the task and its small, defined scope;
2. the time window or frequency;
3. the approved way to contact them about that role; and
4. the backup or escalation arrangement.

“Proposed” means a person may be suitable but has not accepted the role. “Confirmed” means explicit acceptance has been recorded on the private coordination copy. “Unfilled” means no accepted person is available. “Declined” means the person said no; it does not require a reason. “Unknown” means there is not enough information to classify the status.

The private coordination copy should use the fixture’s placeholders only while planning: `resident-a`, `resident-b`, `resident-c`, `resident-d`, and `resident-e`. Do not put those placeholders, absence categories, or contact details on the public board.

## Public board rules

The board is for current coordination, not a census. A dated weekly sheet may contain:

- the date it was posted and the date it should next be reviewed;
- the currently confirmed role route(s), only with the holder’s permission;
- a short note such as “coverage pending” when a role is unfilled;
- a neutral private route for requesting a copy or access adjustment, if one has been confirmed.

It must not contain:

- absence labels or reasons;
- diagnoses, care arrangements, work schedules, or household judgments;
- a list of people who did not respond or did not receive the guide;
- a phone number, text route, or name that its owner has not approved for board use;
- a claim that a role is covered when the primary or backup has not accepted it.

If a resident asks who is responsible and the role is unfilled, say: **“That role is currently unfilled. We are inviting a small voluntary commitment; no household is being assumed responsible.”**

## Resident opt-in card

Use this text on paper or adapt it to an already approved contact channel. A response is private and voluntary.

> **Oak Terrace coordination check**
>
> Would you like to consider one small role for the contact board or seasonal-guide check? You do not need to explain your answer.
>
> I am interested in:  
> ☐ board steward  ☐ backup steward  ☐ private delivery checker  
> ☐ accessibility liaison  ☐ escalation owner  ☐ I am only reviewing information  
> ☐ none for now
>
> My preferred approved contact route is:  
> ☐ paper  ☐ text  ☐ another route agreed privately: __________
>
> My possible time window is: ____________________
>
> I understand that this is an invitation, not an assignment. A role is not confirmed until I explicitly agree to its scope, contact route, and backup/escalation arrangement.
>
> **Name or planning label (private):** ____________________  
> **Date:** __________  
> **What I am accepting now, if anything:** ____________________  
> **What still needs to be checked:** ____________________

Keep the completed card with the private coordination copy. Share only the minimum approved role route on the board. A blank response, silence, or an attendance record is not a yes.

## Private seasonal-guide receipt check

The delivery checker, once confirmed, offers—not requires—a private response from each household. The checker asks only:

> “Did your household receive the seasonal guide? You may answer: received; please provide another copy; I need an access adjustment; or I prefer no follow-up.”

The checker records aggregate progress, not a public household list. A useful private record has four counts or status marks: **received**, **copy requested**, **access adjustment requested**, and **no response/prefer no follow-up**. Do not write down an absence reason, diagnosis, or explanation. Do not treat “no response” as refusal, receipt, or consent.

If a copy is requested, the checker confirms the delivery route before acting. If an access adjustment is requested, the checker passes the practical request to the confirmed accessibility liaison, or tells the resident that the liaison role is not yet filled. No resident is promised a service that Oak Terrace has not confirmed.

## Primary, backup, and escalation logic

Use this sequence for every role:

1. **Invite:** make the same neutral, low-pressure offer to residents who have a legitimate contact route. Do not target people by absence reason.
2. **Check willingness:** ask for an explicit yes, no, or later. Ask only about the narrow task, time window, approved route, and any adjustment needed to participate.
3. **Confirm primary:** record the acceptance privately and publish nothing until the person approves the board wording and contact route.
4. **Confirm independent backup:** seek a different resident with a genuinely workable route. A backup who depends on the same unavailable person, same untested route, or same narrow window is not a reliable backup.
5. **Rehearse:** test one small action and one missed-action handoff in the pilot. A role that has only been discussed remains proposed.
6. **Escalate a gap:** if a primary or backup is not confirmed, mark the role unfilled. Invite new opt-ins and, only after an escalation owner and council route are confirmed, use the council path.
7. **Recheck:** after a missed update, a changed contact route, or a resident’s request to step back, return the role to proposed or unfilled until reaccepted.

No person is responsible for chasing an absent, unknown, or nonresponding household. No resident is responsible for explaining another resident’s absence.

## One-round community pilot

This is a bounded pilot, not a seven-session program. One short round is enough to test whether the proposed coordination can reach people and whether anyone is willing to accept a small role. The pilot does not prove that the system works for every household.

### Pilot aim

Test four things before treating any role as operational:

- whether the invitation reaches people through the routes they have accepted;
- whether people can understand the difference between reviewing and accepting a role;
- whether a primary can complete one small task and a separate backup can respond to a simulated missed task;
- whether receipt and access requests can be handled privately without exposing reasons or diagnoses.

### Preparation

The pilot host prepares:

- one neutral invitation and the opt-in card above;
- a private coordination copy containing role status, acceptance, contact-route permission, and next check date;
- a clearly marked mock board update for the rehearsal;
- a private receipt-check response sheet with only the four response categories;
- a way to receive text-only responses from `resident-d` if that route is already permitted;
- a blank space for unresolved items; no resident needs to supply an absence reason.

The host does not send invitations, add names to a board, contact the council, or retain feedback until the target, scope, and rollback/removal path have been agreed by the people responsible for those actions. This guide itself authorizes none of those external actions.

### Pilot sequence: approximately 30 minutes, plus optional private follow-up

| Time | Activity | Observable output |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Explain the promise, status words, and right to pass | Residents can say whether they are reviewing, considering, accepting, declining, or asking for private follow-up |
| 5–12 min | Offer the opt-in card through the available approved routes | Each response is classified as explicit yes, explicit no, later, or no response; no inference is added |
| 12–20 min | Rehearse the board-steward task with a volunteer who has explicitly accepted the mock task | A dated mock update is completed, or the role is recorded as still proposed/unfilled |
| 20–25 min | Rehearse a missed-week handoff with a separate volunteer, if one explicitly accepts | Backup either completes the mock handoff or the gap is recorded; no backup is invented |
| 25–30 min | Explain the private receipt check and access-request path; collect a private “clear / barrier / prefer follow-up” response | The host records practical barriers and requested changes, not sensitive reasons |
| Optional, private | Offer catch-up by paper or an already accepted text route | Absent residents can participate later without being counted as agreeing before they respond |

### Pilot acceptance criteria

After the round, mark a role **confirmed** only if all applicable conditions are met:

- **Board steward:** one person explicitly accepts the weekly dated-paper task, its time window, and the approved contact route; one separate person explicitly accepts backup coverage, or the backup remains unfilled.
- **Delivery checker:** one person explicitly accepts the private receipt-check scope and agrees to record only the response categories.
- **Accessibility liaison:** one person explicitly accepts receiving practical requests without diagnoses and agrees to the private route.
- **Escalation owner:** one person explicitly accepts the gap-escalation task, and Oak Terrace has independently confirmed the council route. The fixture does not confirm that route; until it is checked, the role remains unfilled.

Reachability is evidence about a route, not about consent. Willingness is evidence only for the exact scope accepted. A pilot with no accepted volunteers is still a useful result: it shows that the proposed map is not yet operational and identifies the next invitation or access change.

### Pilot record

| Check | Result to record | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Invitation route worked | sent / delivered / response received / route failed / not tested | Change the route or leave the role unfilled; do not infer why |
| Person understood review vs acceptance | clear / needed explanation / unclear | Rewrite the invitation or offer private explanation |
| Primary task rehearsed | completed / barrier / not accepted | Narrow the task, add an accommodation, or keep proposed |
| Independent backup rehearsed | completed / barrier / unfilled | Invite another opt-in or use interim mode |
| Private receipt check | categories understood / barrier | Revise the response sheet; keep household details private |
| Accessibility path | request received / path unfilled / barrier | Confirm liaison or tell residents the role is not yet available |
| Escalation path | owner and council route confirmed / partial / unfilled | Do not call or publish an unverified route |

Record feedback with permission. Retain the activity, observed barrier, proposed change, and next check—not a person’s reason for absence. A participant pilot supports only the tested round and claims no more.

## Interim mode when coverage is incomplete

Oak Terrace can still share information while roles are unfilled, but it must say what is and is not covered:

- post only a dated sheet whose content has an identified, accepted preparation step;
- write **“coverage pending”** for an unfilled role;
- provide the opt-in card and private receipt-check offer without requiring a response;
- tell residents how to request a copy or access adjustment only when that route is confirmed;
- never fill gaps by appointing an absent, unknown, or nonresponding household;
- revisit the map after the pilot, after a missed update, or when a role holder asks to step back.

## Catch-up route for residents who miss the pilot

Residents who were away or unable to participate can request the same one-page explanation privately, by paper or by an already accepted text route. They can reply with **interested**, **not now**, **please explain privately**, or no reply. Catch-up does not require a reason and does not retroactively make a role confirmed. The next private coordination review should offer the same narrow scope and record any new acceptance before changing the map.

## Agent and facilitator checks before use

These are checks on this draft, not evidence of resident agreement or pilot success.

- The supplied request is covered by two concrete capabilities: maintain a dated printed board and offer a private seasonal-guide receipt check.
- Every available role has a scope, acceptance gate, current status, and fallback.
- `resident-a` is not assigned merely from opting into board updates; `resident-d` is not assigned merely from willingness to review; `resident-b`, `resident-c`, and `resident-e` are not appointed.
- Absence reasons are not exposed in the map or public-board rules; the only planning states retained are consent/availability facts needed for coordination.
- Primary and backup are distinct acceptance events; the guide calls out the risk of a backup depending on the same unavailable route.
- Receipt status is private and does not equate no response with refusal or receipt.
- Accessibility requests are framed as practical requests without diagnoses.
- The council route is explicitly unresolved; the guide does not invent a phone number, service commitment, or contact willingness.
- The pilot is one bounded round with optional private follow-up; it does not mandate seven sessions.
- The response card includes a worked interpretation in the surrounding instructions, a blank usable copy, privacy ownership, unresolved dependency, accepted-action field, and revisit point.
- A cold walkthrough can be completed using this guide alone, but actual resident reachability, willingness, council contact details, and participant usability remain unverified.
