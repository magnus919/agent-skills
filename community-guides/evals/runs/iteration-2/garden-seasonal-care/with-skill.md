# South Fork Community Garden: Seasonal Care Guide

**Edition:** 1.0 draft for a small community pilot (2026-09-15)  
**Community:** fictional 22-plot South Fork Community Garden  
**Season covered:** March through November  
**Guide owner:** not yet confirmed; the host role must be accepted before the first use

## Scope and boundary

This is a routine horticulture and stewardship guide. It helps gardeners notice
conditions, choose a proportionate care action, and hand off a shared task. It is
not a disaster-response plan, emergency procedure, medical guide, or pesticide
manual. It supplies no emergency, medical, or pesticide instructions. If an issue
falls outside ordinary observation and garden coordination, pause this guide and
use a separately approved process or qualified local advice.

The fixture and all local sources below are fictional and supplied for this guide;
they have not been independently verified. This package does not assume a fixed
number of meetings. A host may run the activity once, repeat it during ordinary
care windows, or use only the worksheet for a brief check-in.

## What this guide enables

By the end of an ordinary care check, a gardener should be able to:

1. record an observation without presenting a guess as a fact;
2. choose one of four bounded decisions: leave as-is, monitor and recheck, request
   a shared task, or photograph/discuss an unknown pest before action;
3. accept, decline, or leave a handoff unfilled without pressure; and
4. leave a next check that another gardener can understand.

The useful output is a completed **SCW-1 Observe–Decide–Handoff Worksheet**.
The blank copy appears below, followed by a fictional worked example.

## South Fork starting brief

| Item | Supplied starting point | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Garden | 22 plots in a shared community garden | Use a zone or shared-bed location, not personal household information. |
| Known pattern | West beds dry first. | Make the west beds an observation priority, not an automatic watering order. |
| Shared resource | Rain barrel is shared and usually refilled after rain. | Check current availability; never promise that water will be available. |
| Access | Accessible raised bed is reserved for shared crops and has a 30-minute watering-task option. | Offer a seated, short, paired, or observation-only role. A gardener is never required to lift compost or use a tool. |
| Pest practice | Garden avoids synthetic pesticides. Unknown pests are photographed and discussed before action. | Do not name a treatment or recommend a pesticide from this guide. |
| Compost | Compost is turned on the first Saturday of each month when two people can work safely. | Treat turning as conditional. If two accepted people are not available, leave the task unfilled and record a follow-up. |
| Prior observation | Tomato staking was needed by late May last year. | Use this as a prompt to look, not as a guaranteed date or instruction to stake. |

### Known unknowns

This year's rainfall, pest pressure, and volunteer availability are unknown. The
guide therefore asks people to observe and record before deciding. It does not
invent a watering amount, pest identification, treatment, medical accommodation,
emergency response, or guaranteed volunteer roster.

## Seasonal care rhythm: March–November

These are care windows, not a seven-session curriculum. The host chooses dates
that match ordinary garden activity and the availability people actually accept.

| Season window | Observe | Possible decision and handoff | Check to leave behind |
|---|---|---|---|
| March: opening check | Walk or review the shared areas. Note which zones are accessible to the people present, current rain-barrel status, and any baseline observations. | Create no-purchase observation roles. Invite a paired or seated check; do not assign absent people. | A zone list, one observation per participating person, and any unfilled role. |
| April: early growth and water | Look especially at west beds and the shared raised bed. Record what is seen about surface and below-surface dryness only where the gardener can do so comfortably and through the garden's ordinary method. | Leave unchanged, schedule a recheck, or request an accepted watering handoff. Confirm the barrel before relying on it. | Worksheet with the next check and water availability marked known, unknown, or unavailable. |
| May: support check | Look for actual support needs in shared crops. Late-May tomato staking is a prior observation, not a calendar promise. | If support is needed, record the observation and ask for an accepted task; do not assume the task or invent a technique. Unknown pests are photographed and discussed before any action. | A factual note, a named-but-accepted or unfilled handoff, and a review trigger. |
| June–August: repeat ordinary checks | Revisit the same zones after weather or at the garden's normal care times. Compare observations rather than relying on last year's dates. | Use short, paired, seated, observation-only, or longer roles. Re-offer only unfilled tasks. A novice may use the worked example; an experienced gardener may explain the garden's existing practice. | One new worksheet or an update that says what changed and what did not. |
| September–October: shared resource and compost check | Note shared-bed condition, water availability, and whether the first-Saturday compost condition is met. | Turn compost only when two people can work safely and both have accepted. Otherwise record “not undertaken” and a fallback observation. | Accepted pair or explicitly unfilled compost role; no claim that turning occurred. |
| November: close and carry forward | Record what patterns were observed this year, what remained uncertain, and which access options worked. | Choose “carry forward,” “monitor next season,” or “needs a separate qualified decision.” Do not convert a gap into a treatment recommendation. | A short year-end note and the next review trigger for the guide owner. |

### A lightweight repeatable check

At any window, use the sequence **notice → separate fact from guess → choose →
handoff or recheck → record**. A check can be fifteen minutes of observation,
thirty minutes of an accepted task, or a longer paired visit. No one must do all
parts. One person can observe, another can carry out an already accepted garden
task, and a third can receive the handoff.

## Participant activity: Observe, Decide, Handoff

**Capability practiced:** turning a real observation into a bounded care decision
that another gardener can follow.  
**Normal duration:** 35–45 minutes, including access choices and closing.  
**Short version:** 20–25 minutes; use one zone, one observation, and one next
check.  
**Worksheet:** SCW-1.

### Participation choices

- **Observation-only:** review a zone from an agreed accessible position, write or
  dictate an observation, and choose the next check.
- **Seated or short task:** use the accessible raised bed's 30-minute watering
  option when it is actually available and accepted, or take another garden-
  approved short task.
- **Paired task:** one person observes or reads the worksheet while another does
  the already agreed garden task.
- **Longer task:** take a task only if its time and conditions fit your own
  availability. No gardener is required to lift compost or use a tool.

Writing, drawing, speaking to a partner, or dictating to a host are all valid
ways to complete the sheet. Do not put medical details, private contact details,
or a household vulnerability on a shared copy. A participant may pass on any
question or role.

### Steps

1. **Choose a zone and a way to participate.** Select the west beds, accessible
   raised bed, compost area, or another shared area. Mark observation-only,
   seated, paired, short, or another feasible mode. This is a planning choice,
   not a disclosure of a personal condition.
2. **Notice before acting.** Record only what was seen, heard, or confirmed. For
   water, record whether the soil seemed dry below the surface only if that is
   something the gardener can check through the garden's ordinary method. Record
   the rain-barrel status separately. Do not turn last year's date or another
   person's guess into today's fact.
3. **Name the uncertainty.** Examples: “I cannot tell whether the mark is a pest,”
   “barrel availability was not checked,” or “no second compost person has
   accepted.”
4. **Choose one decision.** Use the decision ladder:

   | If the observation shows… | Choose… | Record… |
   |---|---|---|
   | No care change is indicated or there is not enough information | **Leave as-is** or **monitor and recheck** | What would cause the next check. |
   | A routine shared task appears useful and the task is within the stated option | **Request a handoff** | Task, place, time option, and whether someone explicitly accepted. |
   | An unknown pest or mark is present | **Photograph and discuss before action** | Photo reference if used, observation, and discussion needed. No treatment. |
   | Compost is due by calendar but two people have not accepted or cannot work safely | **Leave unfilled** | The missing condition and fallback observation. |

5. **Make the handoff usable.** Say what is known, what has not happened, the
   smallest feasible task, and the next check. Ask the receiving person for a
   clear yes, no, or later answer. Attendance is not acceptance. If nobody
   accepts, write “unfilled” and keep the fallback visible.
6. **Close the loop.** The person taking the task records what they actually did
   or did not do. The next observer checks the agreed condition and brings the
   worksheet back or updates the shared garden log through the garden's existing
   process.

### Fictional worked example (not a real commitment)

**Date:** 2026-04-18  
**Zone:** west beds, shared edge  
**Participation mode:** observation-only, seated  
**Observation:** “The soil is dry below the surface at the checked spot. The
rain barrel status was not checked.”  
**Uncertainty:** “I do not know whether the whole bed needs water.”  
**Decision:** “Request a short recheck and possible watering handoff; confirm
barrel availability first.”  
**Task:** “A gardener who accepts it checks the remaining shared edge and, if the
garden's ordinary practice supports it, waters the shared raised bed using the
available 30-minute option.”  
**Acceptance:** “Unfilled in this example; no person is appointed.”  
**Fallback:** “Leave the task open, record barrel status when someone is next at
the garden, and recheck the observed spot.”  
**Next check:** “At the next ordinary care window, or sooner if the garden's
existing routine calls for it.”

This example demonstrates a decision without promising water, assigning an
absent gardener, prescribing a quantity, or treating one dry spot as proof about
the whole bed.

## Facilitator / host instructions

### Prepare

Before the first use, the host should:

1. print one blank SCW-1 per participant plus two spare copies;
2. provide pencils or another low-barrier way to record, with a way to dictate or
   draw if writing is not preferred;
3. choose a meeting point and an observation route that can support seated,
   paired, short, and observation-only participation;
4. identify the actual current status of the rain barrel only when someone checks
   it; do not advertise water availability in advance;
5. confirm that any offered shared task is an existing garden practice, not a new
   technique invented for this guide; and
6. have a paper catch-up route for people who miss the activity.

If no host has accepted the role, print the worksheet but do not present an
unconfirmed roster, partner, or task assignment as settled.

### Run the normal 45-minute activity

| Minutes | Activity | Host instructions and completion check |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–5 | Welcome and boundary | Say: “This is a routine garden observation and handoff activity, not disaster response. We will not diagnose pests, give pesticide or medical advice, or invent emergency steps.” Explain that passing and observation-only participation are valid. |
| 5–10 | Access choices | Offer seated, paired, short, longer, and observation-only options. Say that nobody must lift compost or use a tool. Let people choose privately or with a partner. |
| 10–15 | Demonstrate the method | Read the fictional worked example. Point out the separation of fact, uncertainty, decision, acceptance, and fallback. Ask participants to identify the one field that prevents an assumed volunteer: explicit acceptance. |
| 15–28 | Observe | Participants use SCW-1 in one zone. The host answers process questions but does not fill in a participant's conclusion. A participant may dictate or draw. Completion check: each person has a location, factual observation, and uncertainty or “none noted.” |
| 28–36 | Decide and hand off | Each person chooses one decision. For a shared task, ask for acceptance one person at a time and record yes, no, later, or unfilled. Completion check: no task relies on an absent or silent person. |
| 36–41 | Debrief | Ask: What did we observe? What did we assume? Which participation mode worked? What resource or person was not confirmed? What would make the next check easier? Record unresolved questions rather than forcing agreement. |
| 41–45 | Close | Read back accepted actions, unfilled roles, fallbacks, and the next check. Explain who keeps the sheet and how a participant can correct or withdraw shared information. Give absent participants the catch-up route. |

### Short 25-minute version

Use 0–3 minutes for the boundary and access choices, 3–6 for the worked example,
6–15 for one-zone observation, 15–21 for the decision and handoff, and 21–25
for the debrief and close. Cut the walk between zones and any optional group
discussion; preserve the worksheet completion and explicit acceptance check.

### If the activity gets stuck

- **People cannot distinguish fact from guess:** return to the example and ask,
  “What could another gardener verify without trusting our interpretation?”
- **A pest is named confidently:** record the visible observation, mark the pest
  unknown, and use the garden's required photograph-and-discuss-before-action
  practice. Do not supply a treatment.
- **Water is assumed to be available:** mark barrel status unknown until checked;
  keep the task as a conditional request or use a non-water observation fallback.
- **A person is volunteered without accepting:** replace the name with
  “unfilled” and ask whether anyone wants to accept the smallest feasible task.
- **Two people are not available for compost:** do not turn it. Record the
  condition as unmet and leave a recheck or other observation-only fallback.
- **The group runs long:** stop collecting new observations, finish decisions and
  accepted/unfilled statuses, and preserve the closing next check.
- **A participant cannot attend:** provide the blank sheet, the short boundary,
  the worked example, the selected zone, and any unfilled handoff on paper or
  through the garden's existing communication method.

### Follow-through record

Immediately after closing, the host keeps a brief record containing:

- the date and activity duration;
- zones observed;
- decisions made, including “no action” and “monitor”;
- accepted shared tasks and the person who explicitly accepted them;
- unfilled roles and their fallbacks;
- the next check or review trigger; and
- access barriers or worksheet changes proposed.

Do not turn this into a contact directory. If a participant wants to keep a
private reminder, that is their copy. If a shared record is used, people can
correct or withdraw their own shared information through the host.

## Shared-task handoff card

The host may read or copy this short form when a worksheet is too much for a
spoken handoff:

> **Zone:** ______  **Observation:** ______  **Unknown:** ______  
> **Small task:** ______  **Time option:** ______  
> **Can you accept?** yes / no / later  **Accepted by:** ______  
> **If unfilled:** ______  **Next check:** ______

The handoff is complete only when the receiving person has explicitly accepted,
or the task is marked unfilled with a fallback. A host may not assign an absent
gardener. A task that exceeds a person's available time or access choice should
be reduced, paired, deferred, or left unfilled.

## Small community pilot

**Pilot shape:** one ordinary care window using the normal 25- or 45-minute
activity, followed by one agreed next check. This is a small pilot, not a fixed
seven-session program. A suggested cohort is three to six willing gardeners, or
fewer if that is who is available; the guide also works with one gardener and a
host.

**Pilot roles:** one accepted host; participants choose their own modes; one
person may hold the shared record if participants agree. No one is recruited into
a role by attendance, and no personal needs are collected for the pilot.

**Observe during the pilot:**

1. Could a participant complete the factual-observation and decision fields after
   seeing the worked example?
2. Could a participant choose an access mode without having to disclose a medical
   reason?
3. Did every shared task end as explicitly accepted, declined/later, or unfilled?
4. Did the next person have enough information to check the decision without
   relying on an unstated assumption?
5. Which step, if any, required host explanation that should be added to the
   worksheet or host notes?

Record only the activity, barrier, proposed revision, and later check. Ask before
retaining personal feedback. A successful pilot supports readiness for another
community care window; it does not prove seasonal effectiveness or participant-
tested usability for every gardener.

## Agent checks and evidence status

The following are agent checks for this package, not participant evidence:

- **Source-boundary check:** all local claims are limited to the supplied fictional
  fixture and are labeled supplied/fictional rather than independently verified.
- **Capability trace:** observation → SCW-1 → bounded decision → accepted or
  unfilled handoff → next check is present in both participant and host sections.
- **Worksheet walkthrough:** the fictional west-bed example fills the same fields
  provided in the blank worksheet, including uncertainty, resource status,
  acceptance, fallback, and next check.
- **Access walkthrough:** observation-only, seated, paired, short, longer, and
  catch-up routes are provided; lifting compost and tool use are not required.
- **Safety-boundary check:** no unsupported pesticide, medical, emergency, water
  quantity, pest diagnosis, or guaranteed volunteer instruction was added.
- **Cold-run check:** a new host can prepare, run either timing option, record
  accepted/unfilled roles, and close with a follow-up using this file alone.

**Pilot status:** not yet participant-tested. This guide is **ready for a small
community pilot**, pending an accepted host and whatever ordinary garden access
arrangements the participants confirm.
