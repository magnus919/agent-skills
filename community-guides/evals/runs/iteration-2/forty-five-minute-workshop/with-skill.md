# Riverside Tenants Council: the 45-minute outage card workshop

## Scope and intended outcome

This is one bounded, participatory intervention for the fictional Riverside Tenants Council and its 12 expected participants. It is not a seven-session program.

The workshop goal is to co-design and lightly test a one-page building-outage contact and accessibility card. The council's supplied note says that residents previously found a long phone tree confusing; a short primary/backup card is therefore a design hypothesis, not an established fact.

By the end, participants should have:

- a marked-up draft card with a short primary route, a backup route, and an accessibility route;
- a recorded list of dissent, unanswered questions, and missing input; and
- a clear distinction between actions someone has accepted and roles that remain unfilled.

The card must not ask for or display medical diagnoses or personal safety situations. A name or contact detail belongs on a shared copy only if the person concerned voluntarily agrees; a role or channel placeholder is sufficient for this workshop.

## Materials and preparation

The facilitator and note-taker prepare:

- large-print copies of the blank card and the fictional example below;
- markers and sticky notes;
- the supplied translated phrase card, with plain-language spoken instructions and pauses for interpretation;
- a quiet comment box and blank feedback slips for people who prefer writing or who cannot comment aloud; and
- a visible timer.

Arrange the room so everyone can remain seated, reach the writing surface, and participate without standing or moving between stations. Offer a quiet seat or a lower-stimulation edge of the room. The facilitator should say at the start that speaking, writing, drawing, pointing, working with a partner, or passing are all valid forms of participation. The note-taker records decisions and concerns without recording diagnoses, personal safety details, or unnecessary names.

Prepare the card with neutral placeholders rather than invented local contacts. For example: “Primary: [accepted role or channel]” and “Backup: [accepted role or channel].”

## Run of show — exactly 45 minutes

All blocks include their own transitions and interpretation pauses. The facilitator protects the final five minutes.

| Time | Activity | Facilitation and completion check |
|---|---:|---|
| 0:00–0:04 (4 min) | Arrival and participation choices | Welcome people, offer seated and written options, point out the translated phrase card and comment box, and explain that no one needs to disclose a diagnosis or personal safety situation. Completion: each person knows how to participate or pass. |
| 0:04–0:08 (4 min) | Purpose, boundaries, and the card hypothesis | Explain the outage-card goal and the council's “short primary/backup route” hypothesis. Say that the group is testing clarity, not declaring a final contact system. Completion: participants can state what the group is making. |
| 0:08–0:13 (5 min) | Private first pass | Each person marks or writes: “What would I need to know first during a building outage?” and “What could make this hard to use?” They may use a sticky note, draw, dictate to the note-taker, or pass. Completion: every participant has an opportunity to contribute before group discussion. |
| 0:13–0:20 (7 min) | Route and barrier mapping | In pairs or at individual seats, compare contributions and place them under three headings: primary route, backup route, accessibility route. The facilitator uses the fictional example if people are stuck. Completion: at least one candidate item or question is visible under each heading; unknowns are marked “needs confirmation.” |
| 0:20–0:29 (9 min) | Build the one-page card | Participants edit the large-print card. Keep only the shortest usable wording. The note-taker captures alternatives on a separate “dissent/open questions” area rather than erasing them. Completion: the draft has fields for primary, backup, accessibility route, what to do if neither works, last-checked/review trigger, and an explicit blank for unresolved items. |
| 0:29–0:36 (7 min) | Outage scenario test | Read: “The building has lost power. The usual route has not answered. You need the next update, and a resident needs an accessible way to receive or request it. What do you do first, and what do you do if there is no response?” Participants trace the card silently, with a partner, or aloud. Completion: at least one participant can explain the next action and at least one can identify the accessibility route; failures are recorded as card changes. |
| 0:36–0:40 (4 min) | Dissent, missing voices, and unresolved items | Ask, “What would make this card unsafe, unusable, or unfair for someone not represented here?” Record competing needs and unanswered questions verbatim where possible. Do not force a vote or manufacture consensus. Completion: dissent and missing-input questions are visible on the card or decision log. |
| 0:40–0:43 (3 min) | Next actions and absent-resident route | Ask for volunteers to accept only specific follow-up actions: confirm a role/channel, review wording, or receive later comments. Mark each as accepted or unfilled. Give absent-resident feedback slips and explain that a copy may be returned through the quiet comment box by a council-agreed review point; if no person accepts responsibility for checking it, record that role as unfilled. Completion: the owner and timing of each accepted action are written down, or the dependency is explicitly open. |
| 0:43–0:45 (2 min) | Close and take-home copy | Read back the current draft, open questions, and accepted actions. Ask for the two success checks: “Can you explain the next action?” and “Can you point to the accessibility route?” Hand out or photograph/duplicate the marked draft only for participants who want a copy. Completion: participants know what is decided, what is not, and how a resident who missed the meeting can comment later. |

## The participant artifact: one-page outage contact and accessibility card

The group leaves with the current marked draft. It is labelled **DRAFT — workshop-tested only; confirm contacts and channels before relying on it** until the open dependencies below are resolved.

### Worked fictional example

| Card field | Example wording | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Primary route | “Building support contact — [role/channel to be confirmed]” | A participant can identify the first action. |
| Backup route | “If no response, use [backup role/channel to be confirmed]” | The backup is distinct from the primary. |
| Accessibility route | “For a seated, written, interpreted, or otherwise accessible update/request, use [route to be confirmed]” | The route describes access, not a diagnosis. |
| If neither works | “Write the time tried and use [next route to be confirmed]” | The next step is not implied or invented. |
| Last checked / review trigger | “To be completed by the council” | A later check is visible. |
| Open question | “Who accepts responsibility for checking later comments?” | Unfilled responsibility is not hidden. |

Do not put diagnoses, personal safety situations, or other sensitive personal details on the shared card. A participant may keep a private note or ask for a one-to-one follow-up instead. The card records a route, not a resident's private circumstances.

## Facilitation choices and recovery

Use short sentences, read every field aloud, pause after instructions, and confirm meaning without asking limited-English participants to perform for the group. The translated phrase card supports the activity, but its wording should not be treated as professionally reviewed translation. Participants may work in pairs, use a marker or sticky note, dictate to the note-taker, or pass.

For the resident who cannot stand for long, keep all materials within seated reach and have the facilitator or note-taker move the large card rather than asking the resident to move. For night-shift residents, avoid framing attendance or speaking as a test of commitment; the written card and later-comment route are equally legitimate contributions. Keep the comment box available throughout.

If discussion stalls, offer the fictional example and ask what should change. If time runs short, cut elaboration from the scenario but preserve the scenario's completion check, the dissent/open-question record, and the closing route for absent residents. If a helper, translation aid, or expected contact is unavailable, continue with placeholders and label the missing dependency; do not invent an accepted role.

## Handling dissent and missing voices

Dissent is a usable output. The note-taker keeps three separate labels:

1. **Proposed wording** — what the group is considering.
2. **Concern or competing need** — why someone thinks it may fail or exclude people.
3. **Decision status** — accepted, deferred, or unresolved, with the next check.

If two needs conflict, retain both statements and record the smallest question that would resolve or test the conflict. Do not resolve it by majority vote unless the council already has a decision rule; no such rule is supplied here. A card with no agreed primary route or no plausible accessibility route remains a draft, not a successful consensus artifact.

For a resident who cannot attend, provide a duplicate of the blank card or feedback slip. The resident can write a preferred wording, barrier, or question without identifying a diagnosis or safety situation and return it through the quiet comment box by the review point the council names. The facilitator must record who will check those comments and when, or mark that responsibility **unfilled**. Do not speak on behalf of absent residents or appoint them to roles.

## What an agent can check before the workshop

These are mechanical and evidence-bound checks an agent can perform before anyone runs the session:

- add the agenda blocks and verify that they total exactly 45 minutes;
- verify that every supplied constraint appears in the plan: 12 expected participants, one facilitator, one note-taker, limited-English access, seated access, night-shift flexibility, privacy boundaries, and a later-comment route;
- cold-walk the instructions to ensure a new facilitator can prepare and run the activity using only the stated materials;
- check that the card has primary, backup, accessibility, failure, review, and open-question fields;
- check that no worksheet field requests a diagnosis, personal safety situation, or invented contact information;
- verify that the fictional example is clearly fictional and that the primary/backup preference remains a hypothesis;
- verify that private, voluntary, shared, unresolved, accepted, and unfilled information are distinguished; and
- run a fictional, non-human scenario trace to check that the closing success signals are observable.

These checks do not establish that the card is understandable, translated well, locally legitimate, or workable during a real outage.

## Questions requiring a community pilot or follow-up

The workshop itself should leave these questions visible rather than pretending to answer them:

- Do residents actually find the short primary/backup card clearer than the long phone tree?
- Which roles and communication channels are real, reachable, and voluntarily accepted by the people named or represented?
- Does the proposed accessibility route work for different access needs, including seated participation and limited-English communication, during an outage?
- Is the translated wording understood and accepted after competent language review?
- Can night-shift residents use the card at the times they are likely to need it?
- Does the “if neither works” step work without assuming money, transport, home ownership, or a particular device?
- Who will review later comments, update the card, and withdraw or correct shared contact information?
- When will the council re-check the card: a council-chosen date or a specific change in the building's contact arrangements?

No participant-testing or local-contact validation is claimed until those questions have evidence. The guide is ready for a community pilot of this single workshop, with ownership and review status still to be confirmed by the council.

- **Maintainer:** Riverside Tenants Council owner unconfirmed.
- **Review trigger:** A council-agreed review point or a change to the building's outage contact/accessibility arrangements.
- **Feedback route:** The quiet comment box and the later-comment route named by the council; exact owner and deadline remain open until accepted.
- **Agent checks:** Timing, coverage, privacy, worksheet completeness, facilitation fallback, and fictional scenario walkthrough described above.
- **Participant pilot:** Not yet performed.
- **Unresolved facts:** Actual primary/backup contacts, actual accessibility channel, reviewed translation, later-comment owner/deadline, and the card's performance in a real or community-run test.
