# Maple Court outage card — resident-ready revision

**Revision:** 0.2 (bounded draft for community re-test)  
**Status:** Ready for a community pilot; not participant-validated.  
**Audience:** Maple Court residents, including renters, night-shift residents, and residents who need an accessible check-in.  
**Scope:** Reporting a suspected power or water outage. This card does not promise a repair time or define emergency, elevator, or accessibility procedures that have not been confirmed for Maple Court.

## Resident card

### Power or water outage?

1. Note your unit, which service is affected, and when you noticed it. Share only the information you choose to share.
2. During building desk hours, report the outage to the building desk.
3. Ask how you will receive an update, if you want one.

### If the desk is closed

The Maple Court after-hours reporting contact is **not confirmed for this revision**. Check the building’s current posted notice if one is available, but do not use an unverified phone number or assume that a contact listed elsewhere is current. Record the outage and report it when the desk reopens unless Maple Court provides a confirmed after-hours route.

If there is an immediate safety issue, use the building’s established emergency process. This card does not replace or define that process.

### Accessible check-in

If you use a mobility aid or need an accessible way to report or receive updates, tell the desk what access you need and ask for an accessible check-in method. The building-specific accessible route and after-hours option still need confirmation. **Do not infer from this card that an elevator is operating or safe to use during an outage.**

### Language note

The original fixture records English and Spanish coverage. This revision supplies the updated English wording above; the corresponding Spanish copy must be updated and reviewed for meaning, parity, and readability before a bilingual release. Spanish wording is not invented here because it was not included in the supplied fixture.

## Checks an agent can perform now

- Confirm that every supplied comment has an explicit accepted, deferred, or rejected decision.
- Confirm that the resident copy says “power or water outage,” does not promise a 30-minute repair, and does not direct residents to use an elevator.
- Confirm that no phone number, after-hours contact, accessibility route, emergency procedure, repair commitment, or resident consensus has been invented.
- Confirm that the accessibility limitation, Spanish-copy limitation, version number, source fixture, and pilot status are visible.
- Cold-read the card for the desk-open and desk-closed paths and check that the interim instructions do not contradict the unresolved-contact status.

These are document and reasoning checks only. They cannot establish that a contact works, that an elevator is safe, that the accessibility plan is feasible, that the Spanish copy is accurate, or that residents can use the card under outage conditions.

## What must be re-tested in a community pilot

Before treating v0.2 as validated:

- Ask participants with different schedules, including at least one night-shift renter if available, to explain what they would do when the desk is closed. Record the barrier and circumstance; do not turn a few comments into a universal preference.
- Ask a resident who uses a mobility aid, or another participant with a relevant access need, to walk through the check-in and update path without being directed to use the elevator. Confirm the actual building-specific procedure with the responsible owner.
- Test the card with a new reader who has not seen this revision. Check whether the outage wording, required report details, and desk-hours path are understood without extra explanation.
- Test the confirmed after-hours and accessibility information once supplied. Until then, mark those paths unresolved rather than scoring the interim wording as complete.
- Have a competent reviewer compare the English and Spanish versions for meaning, parity, and readability; test the final physical or digital presentation, including grayscale and contrast if color is reconsidered.
