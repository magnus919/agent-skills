## Active resident copy

### English — Maple Court outage card v0.2

If you have a **power or water outage**, write down the affected unit and notify the building desk during desk hours.

The after-hours reporting contact is **not yet confirmed**.

If you use a mobility aid or need an accessible check-in, the accessible check-in plan is **not yet confirmed**.

This card does not provide elevator instructions for an outage.

## Remaining uncertainty

- The verified after-hours reporting contact is still missing. Until it is confirmed, this card cannot provide a complete night-shift reporting workflow.
- The accessible check-in plan and any safe outage-related elevator guidance are still missing. The card deliberately does not substitute general advice for that missing procedure.
- The fixture does not show whether “power or water outage” is clearer for most residents, including Spanish-speaking residents.
- The Spanish text has not received competent language review. Its wording must be checked before it is treated as final resident copy.
- No evidence establishes a preferred color, a 30-minute repair time, or participant-tested usability.

## What an agent can check now

An agent can verify that:

1. v0.1 is identified as the source version and is not overwritten by this revision.
2. The old “use the elevator only when the outage is short” instruction is absent.
3. The revision contains no invented after-hours contact, repair guarantee, or replacement safety procedure.
4. The only active resident action is traceable to the supplied v0.1 instruction to document the affected unit and notify the building desk during desk hours.
5. The Spanish copy expresses the same supported action and unresolved statuses, contains no extra operational action, and is labeled as awaiting competent review.
6. R1’s two comments, R2’s comment, and R3’s comment are preserved without converting them into a vote or consensus claim.
7. The English and Spanish copies agree on the absence of confirmed after-hours, accessibility, and elevator guidance.

These are document and provenance checks. They do not establish that residents can use the card successfully.

## What must be re-tested in a community pilot

Before treating v0.2 as validated, run a bounded pilot with participants who include night-shift renters, residents who use mobility aids, and residents who read the Spanish copy when that copy has passed competent language review. Test:

- whether residents understand “power or water outage” without extra explanation;
- whether the desk-hours instruction matches what residents can actually do and whether the unresolved after-hours notice makes the missing route visible;
- whether mobility-aid users can identify what is missing from the accessible check-in notice and whether the venue/card format creates an access barrier;
- whether both language copies communicate the same meaning;
- whether the card is readable in its intended print/display format, without assuming neon green is preferred; and
- whether removing the unsafe elevator sentence leaves a critical information gap that can be filled only by verified accessibility and safety guidance.

Do not use the pilot to imply or test a 30-minute repair guarantee. Record each observed barrier, the participant population and language, the proposed change, and the follow-up check. A successful agent walkthrough or document inspection is not participant-tested validation.
