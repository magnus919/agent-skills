# Author task exercise: repair service

Input: `tasks.json`, case `repair-service`. Producer and reviewer: the authoring
assistant, 2026-11-06. This is an actual writing exercise using fictional supplied
inputs, not an independent model comparison or customer study. The author has seen
the guidance and rubric. No performance improvement is measured.

## Message brief

Draft R1. Audience: independent Westport cafe owners seeking espresso-machine help.
Alternative: replace the machine or call separate general repairers. Capability:
bench repair with written fault diagnosis and a repair-or-replace recommendation.
Customer activity: review what is wrong and decide whether to approve paid repair.
Outcome supported: an informed repair-or-replace decision, not guaranteed savings
or uptime. Inputs are supplied fiction; preferences and prevalence are untested.

Proof: sample written diagnosis demonstrates the output's shape, not repair success.
Commitment: request an inspection slot, with availability confirmed afterward.
Inspection fee applies (amount unknown); customer drop-off; no transport. Voice:
candid, neighborly, practical. Owner and approval scope not supplied; draft only.

## Candidate openings

| Approach | Headline | Supporting line | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offer-led | Espresso-machine bench repair for Westport cafes | Get a written fault diagnosis and repair-or-replace recommendation before approving paid repairs. | Strong service identification; less focus on the replacement alternative |
| Outcome-led | Decide whether to repair or replace your espresso machine | Bench inspection and a written recommendation for independent Westport cafes. | Leads with decision value; repair service needs the second line |
| Alternative-led | Before replacing your cafe's espresso machine, get a diagnosis | Our Westport bench service provides a written fault diagnosis and repair-or-replace recommendation. | Makes the alternative explicit; may sound more relevant to replacement-ready owners than repair seekers |

Select offer-led for the supplied service-seeking visit intent. The written output
and recommendation remain visible. This is a reasoned drafting choice, not a tested
winner. Retain the outcome-led option as a comprehension-test alternative.

## Initial homepage draft R1

**Espresso-machine bench repair for Westport cafes**

Get a written fault diagnosis and repair-or-replace recommendation before approving
paid repairs.

**Request an inspection slot**

We confirm availability after your request. Bring your machine to our Westport bench;
transport is not offered.

**See what a diagnosis looks like**

View a sample diagnosis with the fault findings and recommendation. This shows the
deliverable, not a promise that every machine can be repaired.

Objection: “Do I have to approve a repair before I know what's wrong?” No. The
written diagnosis and recommendation come before approval of paid repair work.

## Desk review and revision R2

Finding: R1 records the inspection fee in the brief but omits it from the action
block. “Before approving paid repairs” could be read as meaning inspection is free.
This is an author-observed copy issue, not a customer observation.

Replace the action block with:

**Request an inspection slot**

Inspection carries a fee; the amount needs confirmation. We confirm availability
after your request. You drop off the machine at our Westport bench; transport is not
offered. The diagnosis and recommendation come before approval of paid repair work.

Revise the objection answer too: “You approve paid repair work after the written
diagnosis and recommendation. The initial inspection has a separate fee; its amount
needs confirmation.” Publication remains blocked on that price information and any
required service terms, not on perfecting the headline.

## Brand adaptations after R2

Service card: **Westport espresso-machine bench repair. A written diagnosis and a
repair-or-replace recommendation before you approve paid repair work.**

Action caption beside the card: **Request an inspection slot. An inspection fee
applies; confirm its amount before proceeding. Availability is confirmed after your
request. Customer drop-off required.**

Button: **Request an inspection slot**. This is clearer than “Bring back the buzz,”
which could fit the friendly voice but obscures the action. Practical language is
visible in “written diagnosis” and “before you approve paid repair work.”

## Original-to-revised record

- “For 30 years our passionate team has empowered hospitality” → selected opening.
  Reason: the supplied task needs service relevance; the history claim is not given
  as verified evidence. The new line names service, audience and location.
- “Get back to greatness” → written diagnosis and repair-or-replace recommendation.
  Reason: concrete deliverable and decision value replace an unbounded aspiration.
- Three equal actions → primary inspection request with sample diagnosis as a
  secondary evaluation path. Reason: the supplied page goal is inspection requests.
- R1 action block → R2 fee-explicit block. Reason: preserve the difference between
  inspection and approval of later repairs.

## Unresolved and validation

Confirm inspection fee, scope of inspection, customer approval mechanics and sample
viewing behavior. No fabricated urgency or turnaround. Ask intended cafe owners to
explain what the inspection commits them to, what they pay for, what they receive
and what happens next. No participants, outcomes or conversion test exist here.
