# Author task exercise: supplier reviews

Input: `tasks.json`, case `supplier-review`. Producer and reviewer: authoring
assistant, 2026-11-06. Fictional supplied inputs; actual authored copy and desk
review, not an isolated baseline/candidate run or independent adjudication.

## Message brief

Draft S1, owner/approval not supplied. Analysts currently manage separate email
threads and copied spreadsheets. Vendor Thread links one supplier questionnaire to
multiple internal review requests, with a dated decision record for each request.
Supported changed activity: reuse a questionnaire rather than copy it into each
review. Security value: inspect the decision record. Procurement next decision:
confirm rollout scope. No AI capability, certification or measured saving is given.

Proof: demonstrate links and per-request dated decisions in the sample sandbox.
Security review and migration required. Four-to-eight-week onboarding estimate,
subject to scope. Integration coverage, price and scheduling terms are unknown.
The main action requests a workflow demonstration, not production onboarding.
Voice: exact, restrained, professional.

## Candidate openings and selection

| Approach | Opening and support | Reason and tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Offer-led | Supplier-questionnaire reuse for internal reviews. Link one questionnaire to multiple requests, with dated decisions for each. | Clear product task; less immediate explanation of the spreadsheet alternative |
| Outcome-led | Reuse a supplier questionnaire across review requests. Vendor Thread links the questionnaire to each internal review and retains its dated decision record. | Choose for analysts evaluating repeated review work; name/category depends on the support line |
| Alternative-led | Move supplier reviews beyond copied spreadsheets and email threads. Link a questionnaire to multiple internal review requests and inspect dated decisions. | Recognizable workaround; longer and less focused opening |

Choose outcome-led because the supplied analyst task concerns repeated review
requests. The tradeoff is that the product category is not explicit until the
support line. Test category comprehension rather than claim it is already clear.

## Homepage opening

**Reuse a supplier questionnaire across review requests**

Vendor Thread links the questionnaire to each internal review and retains its dated
decision record, so analysts can work from the shared questionnaire instead of
copying it into separate spreadsheets and email threads.

**Request a workflow demonstration**

Discuss your review workflow and inspect questionnaire links and dated decision
records in a sandbox. Scheduling terms need confirmation. A demonstration request
does not begin a production rollout.

**Inspect the product's actual mechanism**

Follow one questionnaire into two review requests and inspect each request's dated
decision record. The demonstration supports the capability description; it does
not establish a productivity percentage or compliance outcome.

## Security pitch and procurement objection

Security opener: “Inspect dated decisions for each supplier review request while
analysts reuse a linked questionnaire across reviews.” This keeps the shared
mechanism visible while emphasizing the security reviewer's supplied information need.

Procurement objection: “What would rollout involve?” Production rollout requires
security review and data migration. Estimated onboarding is four to eight weeks,
subject to scope. Integration coverage and pricing are open questions for scoping;
we cannot yet promise a particular integration, fee or launch date.

## Compressed brand expression and review

Initial card S1: “One supplier questionnaire. Linked reviews. Dated decisions.”

Desk finding: “linked reviews” compresses the important one-questionnaire-to-many-
requests relationship too far. It could describe links between reviews rather than
reuse of the questionnaire. That is an author comprehension hypothesis, not a user
finding.

Revised card S2: **Link one supplier questionnaire to multiple internal review
requests, with a dated decision record for each.**

Tradeoff: longer, but preserves the actual differentiator. Timing is omitted because
the card makes no deployment-speed claim; full onboarding terms stay in the brief.
If a later card includes the estimate, its scope qualification and prerequisites
must accompany it.

Misleading near miss: “AI-powered instant compliance.” Reject: AI capability was not
supplied, and neither instant deployment nor compliance success follows from the
product features. A restrained visual style cannot repair that meaning change.

## Original-to-revised record

- “Our AI-powered ecosystem transforms vendor risk” → reuse-led opening. Removes
  unsupported AI and replaces abstract transformation with the supplied mechanism.
- “Instantly compliant” → sandbox mechanism explanation and qualified rollout note.
  The revised copy supplies what can be inspected instead of a guaranteed outcome.
- “Start now” → workflow demonstration request with an explicit non-rollout consequence.
- S1 card → S2 card. Restores the questionnaire-to-multiple-requests relationship.

## Handoff and validation

GTM owner must confirm integrations, pricing, scheduling and rollout scope. Brand
owner reviews analyst, security and procurement expressions against the same source
brief; UX defines request inputs and confirmation behavior. Ask intended readers
which object is reused, what decisions can be inspected and what a demo request
commits them to. No study participants, actual observations or measured lift exist.
