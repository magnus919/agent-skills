# Research brief: customer-centered messaging

- Question: What reusable customer-centered messaging method can be extracted from the public sources, and where should it live in this catalog?
- Depth: moderate, bounded primary-source investigation with an independent adjacent-evidence check.
- Commissioned by: Magnus. Date: 2026-11-06.
- Durable destination: this repository research package and the integrated skill references.

## Findings

1. **This is principally messaging methodology.** The public introduction centers customer goals and obstacles, a helpful business, a credible path, action and outcomes (S1 C1–C4). Website guidance applies that model to offer clarity, imagery, navigation and action (S2 C6–C9). Its cross-channel use supports a messaging owner rather than a website-only skill (S4 C12). Confidence: high for the method's description; effectiveness unresolved.
2. **Use neutral operational language and preserve provenance.** The useful abstraction is a customer goal → obstacle → credible help → feasible path → decision → outcome chain. Our proposed workflow adds evidence labels, positioning prerequisites and validation. These additions are authored recommendations, not claims that they originated with StoryBrand. Confidence: high for traceability; practical value needs evaluation.
3. **The best primary fit is `go-to-market`.** Its description and positioning reference already own audience, differentiated value, message hierarchy and objections. Narrative is a way to express positioning; it cannot determine whether the claimed advantage is true. `brand-designer` documents the approved strategy and voice; its templates can consume the result. Confidence: high based on current catalog responsibilities.
4. **Do not import sales promises or rigid rules.** Public materials include anecdotal growth claims and strong assertions about psychology (S1 C5; S2 C7/C10; S3 C11). Independent writing research supports usability benefits in a specific historical setting, not this complete formula or a conversion forecast (S6 C14–C16). Confidence: high for this evidence boundary; no conclusion that the method is ineffective.

## Accepted integration plan

1. Add `go-to-market/references/customer-centered-messaging.md`, adapted from the [draft method](customer-centered-messaging.md), with an explicit dependency on positioning.
2. Add `go-to-market/templates/customer-message-brief.md`, adapted from the [draft template](message-brief-template.md).
3. Route website-message, customer narrative and unclear-offer requests from the `go-to-market` entrypoint and existing positioning reference. Preserve attribution in a source section and link this research package.
4. Route approved briefs into `brand-designer` strategy/voice work. Keep the actual method in one place; a routing link is sufficient initially.
5. Add output-quality eval cases to every modified skill's manifest, preserving existing IDs. Candidate scenarios: missing audience evidence; multi-audience enterprise purchase; unsubstantiated results; invented emotional pain; complex onboarding hidden by a simple plan; brand voice preservation; website draft with honest action expectations.
6. For each new assertion, challenge it with a satisfying answer, a contradictory near miss and missing evidence. Separate trigger probes from portable evals. Run format, quality, eval/coverage, script-discovery and generated-catalog checks. These checks do not demonstrate messaging uplift.

The owner recommendation was accepted. The method and template are now integrated into `go-to-market`; `brand-designer` consumes them through a dedicated handoff and enriched strategy/voice templates. No new skill is created. See `integration-review.md` for review boundaries; no behavioral uplift is claimed.

## Confidence and limitations

Primary documents converge on the method, but they share a publisher and do not independently verify effectiveness. Seven retained sources cover the public model, website applications, current commercial framing and adjacent usability evidence. Search was bounded and result sets truncated; no systematic literature review, paid-course access, full-book review, live customer research or conversion experiment was performed. No independent controlled evaluation of the complete StoryBrand method was retrieved in this pass; that is a search outcome, not proof that none exists.

Open questions: does the method improve comprehension for our intended users? Does it preserve differentiation and voice? Which buyer roles require separate pages? Does higher action uptake produce qualified demand and successful delivery? These require real materials, users and measured outcomes.
