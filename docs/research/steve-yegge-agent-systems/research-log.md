## Scope and decision standard

The question is which of Steve Yegge’s public lessons about coding-agent harnesses and coordination survive contact with his later case reports and can improve the catalog. Included material runs from the 2024 Sourcegraph-era interface essay through the November 24, 2026 Software Engineering Daily episode. Earlier work enters only when it explains the architectural lineage. The inquiry covers session persistence, task graphs, team coordination, flow, maintenance, authority, economics and enterprise entry points. Labor-market forecasts, model consciousness and a full survey of AI coding are outside the decision scope.

This is a deep public-source review of a defined period and subject. Inclusion required first-person writing or attributed speech, original project documentation, or independent original research that directly challenges transferability. Publisher and host summaries provide orientation and only support claims at their level of detail. Use the date of the event or interview recording when known, and say publication when that is the available date. A mirrored essay, a transcript, a recorded talk and a recent interview that retell the same incident count as one underlying observation.

## Weighting and interpretation

- **Latest stated position.** Prefer the November transcript evidence for the current view. The interview’s published date supplies a cutoff, with an earlier recording date possible. Use exact event dates for the two keynotes. Use the October essay for the concrete casework design. Earlier views remain in the chronology, including cases where he was already revising a prediction.
- **Operating observations.** Prefer reports exposing failure, constraints and remedies over predictions. The September cut and drydock leakage are especially valuable because they expose opposite failures from one intervention. The counts in the essay and interview describe different units and possibly different snapshots. The source record preserves that distinction.
- **Implementation evidence.** Project READMEs establish what the maintainers document. The work did not install or exercise these runtimes. The Wheelhouse source, budget ledger, review logs and customer data were unavailable. As a result, performance, recurrence, recovery and dollar claims remain reported or untested.
- **Independent checks.** A controlled topology benchmark, a failure taxonomy and a production research case offer bounded corroboration or counterexamples. Benchmark versions, comparison baselines and workloads matter. DORA supplies organizational context. No benchmark figure sets a catalog threshold.
- **Catalog inference.** Source locators show the observations that motivate each proposal. Ownership and overlap were verified against Git at the named revision. The recommended experiments remain unexecuted. High-consequence effects should be qualified with local acceptance and independent observation.
