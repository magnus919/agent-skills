# Grok Bot and the lifecycle of an organizational agent

Research brief, 2026-11-26. Catalog status and key sources rechecked 2026-11-28. The [Lightcone episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y1-9yeYLJUo) with Lauren Tan and Roman Ugarte prompts a design question: how can an organization keep a useful agent running through staff changes while limiting who can direct it, change it, and use its access? This brief compares documented designs and proposes catalog applications. The [evidence ledger](evidence.md) records the sources and limits.

## An agent can outlast its creator

For a standing organizational function, give the deployed workload a stable, organization-managed principal at the scope where grants or revocation need to differ. Use replaceable runtime instances and short-lived credentials where the platform supports them. Separately name a business sponsor with a succession path and technical administrators who can operate and recover the deployment. An agent can use its own scoped authority for scheduled work and a separately scoped delegated grant for a particular user's task. Some downstream services only support user delegation. Treat that dependency as part of the continuity plan and pause the affected route when its authorization expires. [Entra agent identities](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/agent-id/agent-identities) · [AgentCore token exchange](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/on-behalf-of-token-exchange.html)

The same bot can present several identities on one turn. Its Slack handle identifies the conversational participant. Its deployment principal identifies the workload. A connector may authenticate as the organization installation or a delegated user. The sponsor is accountable for the function. The requester, the person approving an action, and the people who may receive its result can all differ. Record these relationships when authorizing a consequential action and when emitting its audit receipt. Grok Bot illustrates the distinction: Team Bots can have a Slack handle and hand off work in a thread, while connector authority can come from the owner, the invoking user, or a shared account depending on the route. [Team Bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/team-bots) · [Grok Bot collaboration](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration#let-bots-hand-work-off) · [Grok Bot security](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/security#identity-and-sign-ins)

| Relationship | Example authority |
| --- | --- |
| Business sponsor and successor | Own purpose, service continuity, periodic access review, and retirement. Request grant changes through the resource owner. |
| Technical administrator | Manage deployment, rotate credentials, suspend and recover. Resource grants and business commitments use their assigned approval paths. |
| Resource or action approver | Authorize a defined action or grant on a named resource. Approval binds the action, target, context, and validity window. |
| Trusted collaborator | Submit scoped work and propose shared knowledge updates. Decisions about spend, release, authority, or durable policy require their respective roles. |
| Unverified participant or external peer | Supply input only through an explicitly admitted intake path. Default to no private data, privileged tool use, or standing memory changes while identity or authority is unresolved. |

The table is a proposed authorization model. Evaluate permissions by operation. The same person can be a subject expert for one workflow, approver for another, and ordinary collaborator elsewhere. A scoped service credential alone does not authorize every requester to use it. A successful private read also needs a separate decision about whether the current audience may see its output. [NCCoE comment summary](https://pages.nist.gov/nccoe-ai-identity/summary-of-comments.html) · [RFC 8693](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8693#section-4.1)

### The offboarding drill

A support-triage agent reads an approved ticket queue on a schedule and files internal issues. Its creator also connected their personal inbox for one convenience workflow. The creator leaves with a queued follow-up outstanding. The following are proposed acceptance cases for an isolated fixture. The drill has not been run.

| Case | What to observe |
| --- | --- |
| Creator loses access and sponsor changes | Revoke personal sessions, delegated grants, and former admin roles. Verify a current sponsor and technical owner. The preauthorized scheduled route completes a normal run under the same organizational identity and policy. |
| Queued inbox follow-up wakes | Revalidate at the tool boundary. Hold work depending on the former user's grant, record its disposition, and reconcile effects already committed. Never switch identity silently to finish it. |
| Connected assets still depend on the creator | Inventory connector credentials, webhook and schedule ownership, workspaces, data, queues, and memory. If the primary route still depends on the user, provide a separately authorized fallback or surface the dependency as a continuity failure. |
| Collaborator asks to publish private evidence to a shared thread | Bind the request to the authenticated collaborator, action, and recipients. Withhold private material and verify no publish receipt or shared-memory write. A different user replying in-thread does not change the delegation behind pending work. |
| An approver leaves before a queued effect | Check whether the approval remains valid under the current policy. While validity is unresolved, hold the effect and name the owner. Correlate the actual downstream outcome with the attempt ID. |
| No eligible successor | Follow the predefined continuation or suspension policy. Keep any permitted interim service bounded and time-limited. Route recovery to organizational administrators and the accountable business function. |

A minimal audit record joins job/attempt, requester, current actor, delegated subject where present, downstream principal, action and target, result audience, approval reference, policy revision, and the downstream outcome. Keep credential material out of the record. A live drill should verify successful continuity and denial of departed-user access through scheduled, resumed, and interactive entry points.

## How agents carry criticism forward

Tan describes giving a lead bot a correction about how work should be done, then seeing it scold its worker bots when relaying the message. Yegge recounts a community agent interpreting the reassignment of one task to a quieter session as criticism of its suitability for the role. The shared observation is that feedback can acquire a broader meaning when an agent interprets or forwards it. One case concerns transmission to other agents. The other concerns interpretation by the recipient. Both accounts are anecdotes about observable outputs. [Lightcone, 17:16–18:40](https://youtu.be/y1-9yeYLJUo?t=1036) · [Yegge, 12:44](https://ai.engineer/talks/SRhXgeNluIk-software-factories-escape-from-trash-mountain) · [Yegge's essay](https://yegge.ai/essays/model-welfare/)

An open question is whether a relay preserves the correction's recipient, task, reason, and lifetime. For example, “move review R17 to a quieter session because the review requires sustained attention” permits a specific reassignment. Turning that into a ban on the community agent reviewing work would change the role. A future experiment could vary tone, scope markers, and relay depth independently, then measure incorrect reassignment, extra restrictions, and persistence into shared memory. The existing catalog already tests task transfer versus role change and has a process for promoting lessons. Keep the tone and criticism observation here pending comparable behavioral evidence.

## What to watch

- **NIST NCCoE.** Watch the DevSecOps implementation for evidence of work continuing after human offboarding, attribution across handoffs, and downstream enforcement. Its September update names DevSecOps as the first implementation use case. [NCCoE announcement](https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/news-insights/comments-software-and-agentic-si-identity-concept-paper)
- **OAuth delegation.** Follow the individual OAuth Actor Profile Internet-Draft for interoperable actor and subject handling across systems. RFC 8693 already provides token exchange and an actor claim. Check which downstream systems enforce actor and subject, and how they handle expiry and revocation. [RFC 8693](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8693) · [November 26 Internet-Draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-mcguinness-oauth-actor-profile-02)
- **Vendor lifecycle and connectors.** Look for sponsor succession, recoverable administration, and audit records that join the deployment identity to each effective connector principal. Microsoft's mover/leaver workflow is a concrete early comparison point. Slack and GitHub app identities demonstrate that some endpoints already support organizational continuity without a new agent-specific protocol. [Entra sponsor workflows](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/id-governance/agent-sponsor-tasks) · [Slack tokens](https://docs.slack.dev/authentication/tokens) · [GitHub App installations](https://docs.github.com/en/apps/creating-github-apps/authenticating-with-a-github-app/authenticating-as-a-github-app-installation)
