# Supervisory-capacity validation protocol — #762

Pinned catalog: `d284993fc6dc786d53496a3e9a7298de87292715`.
No bundle or skill implementation is authorized by this validation.

Use three public, faithfully reconstructed organization contexts: Klarna customer
service at its February 2024 announcement; GitLab Developer Experience's public AI
review workflow as retrieved 2026-11-01; Anthropic's disclosed August 2026 internal
research/engineering oversight snapshot. Different operating models and periods
must not be treated as a comparable performance sample. First-party statements
are attributed reports, not independent observations or audited efficacy evidence.
No interviews, consented participants, private employee records or contact occur.

First freeze a route-only recommendation using the same source evidence and
existing skill owners. Then fill one common worksheet and compare the proposed
staffing/authority/sampling/escalation/instrumentation/workflow/automation-scope
disposition. Record whether the worksheet changes a consequential recommendation,
merely organizes information, or cannot resolve the question. This is an authored
contrastive exercise, not a blinded experiment or proof of actual decision change.

Recommend a follow-on only if the same decision failure is supported in two
independent cases, existing routing is insufficient, and the worksheet materially
changes the decision. Unknowns cannot count as failures; narrative completeness
cannot count as material improvement. Otherwise recommend no bundle for this evidence.

Separate capacity (available qualified review time/throughput), competence
(task-specific qualification), authority (rights to decide/stop), and attention
(the ability to notice and engage in time). No employee scoring, surveillance,
universal reviewer ratio or inference from headcount-equivalent marketing claims.
At least one case must explicitly recommend constraining automation when evidence
cannot support unreviewed consequential action. Recommendations are hypothetical
case assessments, not interventions in those organizations.
