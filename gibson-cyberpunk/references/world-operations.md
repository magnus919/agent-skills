# World Operations: Make the City Work Before Making It Look Futuristic

## Model a place as overlapping systems

For each setting, answer the smallest useful set of questions in this table. Do not build a dossier if a scene only needs two rows.

| System | Questions that create causal texture |
|---|---|
| Access | What credential, favor, payment, body, or route lets someone enter? Who is excluded? |
| Maintenance | What breaks first? Who repairs it, with what parts, and what does the workaround cost? |
| Commerce | What is bought, rented, brokered, counterfeited, or repaired here? How does a sale change a relationship? |
| Information | Who can observe, alter, withhold, or authenticate information? What remains off-network? |
| Authority | Which actor claims control? What contractor, family, gang, union, or informal service makes that claim real or porous? |
| Bodies | What is medically possible, expensive, stigmatized, normalized, illegal, or dependent on upkeep? |
| Supply | Where do food, power, water, hardware, labor, and waste go? Which shortage becomes a plot lever? |
| Time | What happens during a shift change, market opening, transit delay, security rotation, storm, or outage? |

A “corporation controls the city” is only premise. Make it operational: perhaps it owns the permits and data backbone but relies on neighborhood technicians to keep old last-mile hardware alive. That dependency generates bargaining power, favors, and failure modes.

## Write pressure at three scales

1. **Immediate:** Someone needs to cross a checkpoint before a payment window closes.
2. **Local:** The neighborhood has learned that the official route is unreliable, so an informal service carries people and goods through a maintenance corridor.
3. **Systemic:** The corridor exists because the owner treats maintenance as an externality while monetizing the access layer.

Move from immediate need to system only as much as the user needs. Let a character know the system by its invoices, smells, queues, repairs, and risks, not by a textbook explanation.

## Technology should have a social cost curve

For every advanced tool, identify:

- **Capability:** What becomes newly possible?
- **Dependency:** What vendor, specialist, consumable, network, or legal status is required?
- **Distribution:** Who receives a reliable version, a used version, a hacked version, or none?
- **Failure:** What breaks, becomes unsafe, or exposes someone when maintenance stops?
- **Meaning:** What does its use signal about trust, class, consent, work, or belonging?

This prevents sterile “future gadget” description. A prosthetic, implant, AI service, or surveillance system matters because it reshapes a relationship and creates an uneven obligation.

## Cultural layering without extraction

Use concrete social context rather than a grab bag of borrowed nouns.

- Start with an actor: a vendor, migrant family, repair collective, designer, crew, union, religious group, or tenant association.
- Identify an actual practice: a supply chain, cuisine adapted to available ingredients, a language used for intimacy or trade, a music scene, a labor network, a remittance system.
- Give the practice internal variation and agency. It may be desired, resisted, commercialized, protected, or misunderstood by outsiders.
- Do not invent unreadable “foreign” signage to signal futurity. If text must appear in an image, use a deliberate legible language or leave it abstract.

## Diagnostic failures

| Weak move | Repair |
|---|---|
| “The megacorp owns everything.” | Identify one contract, enforcement mechanism, dependency, and exploit. |
| “The city is chaotic.” | Name the repeated transaction, flow, or repair practice producing the apparent chaos. |
| “Everyone has implants.” | State maintenance, cost, consent, distribution, and social meaning. |
| “It is multicultural.” | Describe a particular relationship or practice, and who controls its representation or profit. |
| “The future is grimy.” | Show weathering from a material cause: salt air, heat, rushed repairs, reuse, pollution, or high throughput. |
