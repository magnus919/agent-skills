# Gibson Cyberpunk — Build Futures That Feel Used, Unequal, and Alive

## Why Install This Skill

“Cyberpunk” often collapses into wet pavement, neon signs, and a cool jacket. William Gibson’s Sprawl fiction is more useful than that. Its force comes from the friction between a person trying to get through the day and the enormous systems shaping access, work, bodies, and culture around them.

This skill helps your agent create or analyze a world with that underlying logic. It can turn an image request into an art direction brief with human action and material history, turn a vague “megacity” into a place with maintenance and markets, or help a story find stakes without copying Gibson’s prose or extending his canon.

## Quick Start

Ask your agent:

```text
Design an original freight-market scene in a dense future city. I need one
human problem, a believable repair economy, and image direction that does not
fall back on generic neon cyberpunk.
```

## Triggers

Load this skill when you need:

- Original settings, scenes, or visuals informed by the material and social logic of Gibson’s Sprawl fiction.
- A useful explanation of how a dense, high-tech, unevenly maintained city might operate.
- Art direction that emphasizes human action, infrastructure, and cultural specificity rather than generic cyberpunk decoration.
- Careful separation of a textual observation, an interpretation, and a new creative adaptation.

## Requirements

No software, model, or API dependency. The skill works best when the agent can consult the relevant primary text for a specific canon claim. It does not authorize imitation of William Gibson’s prose, use of named canon as if licensed, or reproduction of protected scenes or characters.
