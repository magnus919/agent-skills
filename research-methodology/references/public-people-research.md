# Public people research

Use this cross-track protocol for a substantive public biography, career timeline, or background on someone's public work. Choose the journalistic, industry, or comprehensive track for the underlying decision and use its evidence standard. A single straightforward fact needs only a direct verification.

## 1. Bound the question before searching

- Define the useful claims and the as-of date: identity, current role, dated career milestones, work in a specified field, or public professional themes. Limit collection to what the question needs.
- Separate **public lookup inputs** from **private working context**. Build external requests from minimal public identifiers (name, public organization or field, confirmed aliases) and neutral topics. Do not send meeting notes, private motives, unverified affiliations, or personal contact details to an external search, scrape, model, or research service, including through a fallback. Keep any private synthesis in the authorized private workspace.
- Exclude home addresses, personal contact details, nonpublic lives of relatives, details identifying or locating minors, and unrelated personal attributes. Do not use people-search brokers, contact the subject or relatives, or bypass access restrictions. If available public context cannot disambiguate someone, ask for a public identifier or report the ambiguity.

## 2. Always attempt bounded public biography

A substantive profile needs more than a résumé. Make a brief, purpose-bounded life-history pass even if the initial request emphasizes work. Attempt each category below by checking subject-centered public sources; an attempt does not require a dedicated query per category or publishing everything found. Respect explicit scope exclusions, stop at privacy boundaries, and note what was unavailable rather than filling gaps.

| Category | What to establish | Distinctions to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Origins and upbringing | Publicly stated place of origin and where the adult subject grew up; a relevant move, community, or early influence if directly documented | Birthplace, childhood home, places lived later, and a self-described hometown are separate claims. A birthplace does not establish upbringing; present locations are not needed. |
| Education and training | Publicly documented schooling, apprenticeships, training, and academic milestones in dated order | Attendance, admission, completion, earned degree, honorary degree, and institutional appointments are different. The date of a biography is not the date of graduation. |
| Formative experiences | Early projects, mentors, transitions, community or formative episodes that help explain the relevant public work | Attribute the subject’s account and keep it separate from independently established event dates. Do not infer psychological causes or motives. |
| Family and relationships | Consider publicly and intentionally disclosed context about parents, siblings, adult children, or spouses/partners when directly relevant to the subject's story | Use only subject-centered sources and the minimum needed, often a relationship without a name. Never investigate or contact relatives, build relationship graphs, identify minors or expose details about them, or infer family ties or present relationship status from names, pictures, or silence. |

Begin with a substantive public first-person interview, oral history, memoir, or well-sourced profile about the subject. Then use one complementary source path, such as a public institutional record, dated public archive, or independently reported biography, to check material dates and affiliations. A statement about family disclosed by the adult subject can be attributed as such when relevant; do not use relatives’ names as new search inputs. Do not chase an unavailable category with intrusive records. If a material claim conflicts, make one bounded resolution pivot to a more direct, independently originating source. Mark the result **supported**, **attributed self-report**, **unresolved conflict**, or **not established in bounded public sources**. Record an out-of-scope or unsafe category as excluded rather than suggesting that no such experience or relationships exist.

Useful methods: the [National Library of Australia biography guide](https://www.library.gov.au/research/research-guides-0/biography-research-guide) shows how to start from context and cross-reference sources; the [Oral History Association’s core principles](https://oralhistory.org/oha-core-principles/) explain why recollections need context and attribution; the [Oxford Dictionary of National Biography’s description of its coverage](https://academic.oup.com/products-for-institutions/pages/purchase-oxford-dictionary-of-national-biography) illustrates biographical chronology, not permission to excavate private records for living subjects.

## 3. Derive a search plan for an unfamiliar field

Start from how work in this field leaves public traces, not a list of professions or preset websites. Repeat this loop when you learn how the field actually works:

1. **Describe the contribution.** What does meaningful work, participation, leadership, or recognition look like here? What does that activity leave behind: an object, record, credited performance, decision, or first-person account? Name the traces that could support the specific claim, not just where a biography might appear.
2. **Find the record keepers and their vocabulary.** Ask who has a reason to create, preserve, publish, or independently verify each trace. Identify local institutions, archives, regions, languages, and terms for the roles, outputs, credit, and dates. If unclear, make one neutral field-orientation search without the person's name, read an authoritative example, and revise the vocabulary.
3. **Turn the traces into independent searches.** Use minimal public identity hints plus the field's actual words. Try looking for the **artifact** itself (who made what), a **credit or institutional record** (how a contribution was recorded), and a **first-person voice** (what the person said or published). Use different record producers where possible, and test a separate path to corroborate rather than treating three queries as three origins.
4. **Pivot from verified outputs.** Follow public titles, dates, collaborators, projects, issuers, or identifiers from a trustworthy artifact to its original record and adjacent evidence. Check each pivot still refers to the same person. If no employer is relevant, use a public output, collective, venue, or author identifier instead. If the footprint is genuinely sparse, report that limit; do not force a familiar site or infer a career from silence.

### Worked orientations, not a checklist

- **Software:** A release note identifies a change the project published. Follow its linked change and maintainer or reviewer credit to check the person's role; a mention or downstream adoption does not establish authorship.
- **Scholarship:** A publisher's article and contribution statement can separate analysis from supervision. Pivot through a persistent author identifier, then to a related dataset or another institution's dated project record; another copy of the same article adds no independent corroboration.
- **Civic work:** A public body's agenda may establish a proposed item or listed speaker. Follow the item identifier to the clerk's adopted minutes and recorded vote to distinguish proposing, speaking, and deciding. A news recap based solely on those minutes is not a new origin.

For another field, derive analogous traces, record keepers, and pivots. Do not copy these sites or assume that each field has an employer, publication, or public register.

## 4. Search distinct evidence lanes

Choose lanes by the question and the traces you found; use each to get close to the origin rather than as a quota.

| Lane | Useful starting points | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and current role | Employer or institution directory, appointment notice, official registry, representative organization | A bio can be stale; a name match alone is not identity. |
| Career timeline and scope | Dated appointment/departure notices, archived public bios, official reports, filings or project records | An event date is not the same as the page publication or retrieval date. |
| Work artifacts and contributions | Original outputs and their public credit records, including publications, artifacts, releases, or records of decisions | Identify what the credit actually assigns: authorship, maintenance, review, or another contribution. Participation, mention, or adoption does not establish ownership or leadership. |
| Public voice | First-person talks, interviews, transcripts, and professional posts | Documents what the speaker said in context. Views, intentions, and self-reported outcomes are not independent performance evidence. |
| Independent corroboration | Firsthand reporting, another institution's direct record, an independent organizer's observed event | Syndication, scraped profiles, and repeated bios derived from one record are one evidentiary origin. |

Start with an identity query using public name plus a relevant public discriminator, then targeted queries for role/timeline and work. Try public name variants, diacritics, or local terminology as warranted by the field. Search snippets and mirror pages are leads; open the underlying evidence before relying on a material claim. An inaccessible or missing source is not an invitation to substitute an unsourced assertion.

## 5. Resolve identity, freshness, and independence

- **Resolve identity before combining sources.** Check at least one discriminator beyond the name (such as affiliation plus role, authorship identifier, career chronology, or an official cross-link). For a plausible same-name collision, seek an independent discriminator and keep candidate records separate until resolved. Never blend careers.
- **Date time-sensitive claims.** Record the event/effective date, publication or revision date when available, and access date separately. Prefer direct recent evidence for a current role. If the only support is an older event bio, say what it claimed at the time; a page that still loads is not evidence that the role is current.
- **Trace the claim's origin.** Group copied biographies, syndication, press pickup, and restatements under the original source. Mark uncertain independence as uncertain. A primary source can establish what it officially announced or what a person said; corroborate consequential claims separately and keep conflicting evidence visible.
- **Use bounded follow-up.** If a material role, date, identity, or disagreement is unresolved, make a targeted check with a different source lane or public discriminator. Stop when the evidence supports the requested answer or a targeted follow-up no longer closes the remaining gap.

## 6. Report only what the evidence supports

Lead with verified facts relevant to the objective. Include relevant supported public biography and state material gaps; keep the remaining coverage and exclusions in the research log. Label dated/historical claims, attributed self-description, inference, and unresolved gaps separately. In particular, do not convert a public interest, partnership, prior job posting, or professional statement into an inferred hiring need, hidden motive, private availability, or confirmed future plan.

For substantial work, use the skill's existing brief and log. For each material claim, preserve a compact record of **claim, person match/discriminator, source and supporting passage, source origin, relevant dates, and supported/conflicted/unknown status**. Keep exclusions and weak sources visible without copying unnecessary personal data. Link key statements to sources in the synthesis. If a plausible same-name collision, current role, or material date remains unresolved, state what could safely be established and what evidence would resolve the rest.

**Synthetic example:** A 2021 conference bio calls a fictional researcher “Head of Policy, Northbridge Lab.” A dated 2025 announcement appoints them to Wren Institute and links the same public author identifier. Two other sites copy the conference bio. Report the documented former title and dated appointment, use the author identifier to bind the records, and count the copied bios as one origin. Do not infer that either title is current on the requested as-of date without appropriate recent support.
