# One-on-ones depend on the relationship

First identify the relationship, purpose and desired output. Two people deciding
a specific technical or budget question still need a decision contract; a small
headcount alone does not turn that session into a developmental one-on-one.
One-on-ones may combine relationship and work purposes; make a change of purpose
explicit, especially when it changes power, privacy or the record.

The flows below are practice-informed original scaffolds, not validated scripts
or universal cadence rules. Read the evidence reference for GitLab practitioner
practice and CIPD professional guidance. Select the questions that serve this
conversation; do not turn every meeting into a complete checklist.

## Manager and employee

Give the employee meaningful agenda space and ask what would make the time useful.
Agree one or two priorities, using existing updates for routine status. The manager
remains responsible for clear expectations and support; do not make the employee
invent every solution to structural overload or missing resources.

| Current purpose | Useful questions and flow | Proportionate outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Progress and prioritization | “What changed since we last spoke?” “Which result matters most now, and what should move down the list?” Compare expectations and constraints before proposing changes. | Shared understanding; changed priorities only if actually decided |
| Obstacles and support | “Where are you blocked, and what do you need from me?” “Which dependency can I remove or clarify?” Distinguish a request for help from a promise to deliver it. | Specific support offer/commitment or an unresolved dependency |
| Feedback or performance coaching | Ask permission to focus the conversation; describe a concrete observation and impact, then ask “How did you see it?” and “What conditions or expectations need clarifying?” Discuss support and a feasible next step. | Accurate feedback and accepted next steps; disagreement can remain |
| Career development | “What work would you like more opportunity to do?” “What experience or support would help you explore that?” Separate aspirations, possible opportunities and authorized commitments. | A development hypothesis or accepted experiment; no invented promotion/pay promise |
| Check-in and working relationship | “How is the current workload working for you?” “Is there something in how we work together that I should change?” Permit declining personal questions. | Understanding and any agreed adjustment; a personal disclosure is not required |

For a 25-minute developmental session, a possible flow is 3 minutes to choose
priorities, 15 on the employee's main issue, 5 on support/next steps and 2 to check
understanding. Adapt or abandon the allocation if the topic needs something else.
Career discussion may deserve a separate session rather than being perpetually
crowded out by status, but do not impose it on every meeting.

Power asymmetry matters. Invite feedback without making public contradiction or
personal disclosure the price of being heard. Offer a private or later route;
ask what can be shared and with whom, subject to actual policy obligations. Do
not promise absolute confidentiality, anonymity or freedom from consequences.
Do not infer motivation, diagnosis or capability from camera use, speech speed,
quietness, disagreement or a request for support.

A developmental check-in is not a substitute for formal performance, disciplinary,
pay or promotion procedures. If the intended purpose is a formal process, identify
and prepare for it transparently under applicable policy and appropriate support;
do not ambush someone under a casual check-in label or make legal assurances.
Feedback should remain specific and fair; do not conceal a real concern merely
to keep a conversation comfortable. Separate ordinary support notes from formal
records and sensitive disclosures using authorized audiences/retention rules.

## Peer to peer

Do not import managerial authority into an equal relationship. Establish whether
this is mutual learning, coordination or relationship repair. Ask “Where did our
handoff make your work harder?” then reflect the answer before explaining intent.
For repair, distinguish observed events from interpretations, ask what a workable
future interaction would look like, and propose a bounded reciprocal experiment.
For a brief request, start with a usable opener and one proposed next step; add
detail only if it helps this conversation. Neither peer can assign the other's workload without applicable authority or
acceptance. A brief mutual understanding can be enough; do not force personal
confession, consensus or a performance plan. Serious misconduct needs the proper
policy route, not mediation by an unqualified meeting assistant.

## Internal service provider and customer

Treat this as service alignment, not automatically line management. Start with
“What outcome do you need, for whom and by when?” and “What happens if we cannot
meet it?” Compare demand, capacity, agreed service commitments and constraints.
Ask “What would acceptable delivery look like?” and “Which dependency or tradeoff
needs an authorized decision?” Distinguish requested urgency from an accepted
service deadline. A useful output is a scoped request, acceptance criteria and
accepted commitments—or a documented gap and proposed escalation. Do not imply
that a customer can unilaterally reprioritize the provider's whole backlog.

## Internal business stakeholder

Use this for alignment with a sponsor, finance partner, operating owner or affected
business representative. Ask “Which business outcome or risk must this address?”
“What constraint or evidence would change your position?” and “What can you decide,
and whose approval is still required?” Reconcile priorities and dependencies;
record assumptions, authorized calls, required approvals and dissent. Do not treat
sponsorship as every required approval or treat a stakeholder's desired date as a
team commitment. A two-person binding choice still uses the decision contract.

## Notes, access and continuity

Offer the same ordinary access options as other meetings and apply current stated
preferences without diagnosis disclosure. Agree what is worth recording, who can
see it and how to correct it. A peer repair may need only a sentence of mutual
understanding; a service discussion may need a short commitment record. Do not
create a private dossier or automatic performance interpretation. Keep practical
preference memory in its authorized facility, not a shared one-on-one agenda.
Review accepted commitments at a later relevant session without assuming all
prior topics remain current. Choose cadence from the relationship's needs and
changing work; neither practitioner examples nor this skill establish one best
frequency for every relationship.
