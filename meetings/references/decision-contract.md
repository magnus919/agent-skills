# A decision contract, not just a named owner

Use this for a decision session or an agenda item that can bind others. Reuse the
organization's current documented process before proposing a new one. A title,
meeting invitation or majority preference does not override required approval.
For informal choices, a sentence can be enough; do not create governance ceremony.

## Establish before closure

- **Question and scope:** what exactly can be decided, for whom, and by when?
- **Authority:** who makes the call; who must approve; who advises; who implements?
  Check scope, delegation and any quorum. A sponsor, decider and required approver
  can be different people. Record unresolved conflicts in authority explicitly.
- **Readiness:** what evidence, constraints and alternatives must be considered?
  Include known approval gates and affected perspectives. Distinguish missing
  information that blocks a safe call from uncertainty the decider can accept.
- **Closure rule:** how will an actual decision be expressed and recorded? What
  happens to objections? Who can stop or limit implementation? What is the fallback
  if a required person, approval or essential evidence is missing?

If these are not established, propose a contract for confirmation; the meeting can
still explore options. Never present a proposed decision rule as already agreed.
An urgent deadline does not silently remove an approval gate. A documented
emergency exception must itself have applicable authority, scope and evidence.

| Local rule | What closure requires | Common false shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Consultation followed by owner decision | Owner considers input and explicitly decides within remit; retain dissent and required approvals | Advisers voting does not transfer authority |
| Unilateral delegated call | Verified remit/delegation and explicit call; conditions and separate approvals remain | Seniority or speed does not establish delegation |
| Consensus | Shared definition of agreement, whose agreement is required and an objection-resolution/fallback process | Silence, time expiry or no public challenge is not consensus |
| Consent | Agreed meaning of a qualifying objection and how it is handled; use the team's definition | Do not equate consent with enthusiasm or absence of speech |
| Vote or committee | Applicable electorate, quorum, threshold, abstention/tie treatment and remaining gates | A majority of those in the room may not be sufficient |

These are administrative distinctions, not a claim one rule produces better
outcomes everywhere. Do not apply formal board or legal procedures from this
summary; use the relevant governing rules or specialist help.

## Close without losing conditions

Read back: exact outcome, scope, rationale, required approvals obtained/pending,
conditions, dissent and any review trigger. Ask the authorized person/process to
confirm the status. “Preferred option B, security approval pending” is different
from “B approved for launch.” Reaching a preference can be a useful outcome even
when implementation remains blocked. Do not erase a valid unilateral call merely
because agreement was not unanimous; do not call it consensus either.

When a specific missing fact is already agreed to block the choice, defer that
dependent choice and target the evidence gap; do not reopen speculative ways to
decide without it unless a legitimate different scope is established.

If stalled, identify the specific missing fact, incompatible objective or authority
conflict. Propose a bounded next step (async evidence, narrow experiment, delegated
review, defer) with any owner/date clearly proposed until accepted. End circular
discussion when it cannot resolve that dependency. A later call belongs in a later
record; it does not retroactively change what the meeting decided.
