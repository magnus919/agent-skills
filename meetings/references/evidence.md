# Evidence, applicability, and limits

Original synthesis from a bounded source review on 19 October 2026. Retrieval
scopes and public locators below describe what was examined, not a systematic
review or independent replication.
Evidence about meetings, broader workplace experience, and accessibility guidance
answer different questions. None establishes a universal meeting protocol.
The workflow's consent, authority, truthful records and agreed-action safeguards
are design requirements, not claimed experimental findings.

## Universal design and the curb-cut effect

[NC State Center for Universal Design: Principles of Universal Design, version 2.0
(1997)](https://design.ncsu.edu/research/center-for-universal-design/) is primary
design guidance covering environments, products and communications. Its principles
support equitable access, flexible methods and pacing, understandable design,
multiple ways to perceive information, and privacy. Applying these principles
to meeting defaults is this skill’s synthesis, not a controlled meeting trial.
The guidance seeks broad usability and acknowledges that not every guideline
applies to every design; shared defaults cannot establish that all individual
needs have been met.

[Angela Glover Blackwell, The Curb-Cut Effect](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/the_curb_cut_effect),
Stanford Social Innovation Review, is a policy/practitioner essay. It describes wider benefits from removing barriers faced by excluded
groups, starting with disability access and curb cuts. This grounds the rationale
that access essential for some can benefit others; it does not prove a meeting
accommodation’s causal effect, guarantee benefit for everyone, or justify
withholding support whose benefit is specific to a disabled person.

Public locators: NC State “The Principles of Universal Design,” principles 1–4
and final applicability note; Blackwell, opening curb-cut account and “Access,
Opportunity, and the New Demographics.” Substantive page bodies were retrieved.
Public instructions are original synthesis. Offer ordinary access options
to everyone without disclosure, then preserve additional individual accommodations
and resolve conflicting preferences rather than imposing one format on all.

## Meeting research

| Source | Type and finding relevant here | Applicability and limits |
|---|---|---|
| [Thirty Years of Meeting Science: Lessons Learned and the Road Ahead](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-orgpsych-031925-091223), first online 2025, 2026 volume | Narrative review covering preparation, participation, leadership, stress and outcomes | Supports deliberate meeting design; heterogeneous methods and largely Western evidence do not identify one best format, duration or attendee count |
| [Exploring Respectful Inquiry in Leader-Team Interactions: The Differential Effect of Leader Question Asking and Listening on Team Interaction Dynamics in Decision Making](https://doi.org/10.1177/10596011241304079) | Laboratory hidden-profile decision task: 22 four-person teams with a confederate leader; questions without attentive listening produced the weakest performance | Supports pairing inquiry with attentive listening. Speaking share was held constant: this did **not** test whether talking less improves meetings or establish speaking quotas. Small lab study is not a direct test of workplace accessibility |
| [Nudging Attention to Workplace Meeting Goals: A Large-Scale, Preregistered Field Experiment](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791199), 2026 | Preregistered field experiment: 361 employees, 7,196 meetings; the specific pre-meeting goal-reflection nudge did not significantly improve effectiveness | One organization, self-reported outcomes and an active measurement control. Do not promise agendas or reminders always help; this null result does not establish that all preparation is useless |
| [Fatigued individuals show increased conformity in virtual meetings](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-69786-6), 2024 | Preregistered recall survey (N=287) and camera experiment (N=64); camera use was not related to fatigue or conformity | Counters a universal camera-fatigue claim; limited samples/tasks and no diagnosis-specific intervention test. Offer camera options on access, privacy and preference grounds |
| [Starting Your Day with Dread or Excitement? The Effects of Meeting Scheduling Cadences on Anticipated Daily Outcomes](https://doi.org/10.1177/10596011231223263) | Two vignette experiments, N=109 and N=222, on hypothetical schedules and anticipated affect/productivity | Consider meeting load and fragmentation; actual productivity was not measured, so do not promise a numerical productivity gain |

[Can science save meetings?](https://knowablemagazine.org/content/article/society/2026/can-science-save-meetings)
by Lesley Evans Ogden (14 October 2026), republished by Ars, is journalism.
DOI 10.1146/knowable-101426-2 identifies that article, not a study. Its ADHD
meeting findings are reported as unpublished and include informal/self-assessed
diagnoses; do not use them to assert established diagnosis-specific effects.

## Workplace experience and neuroinclusion

| Source | Evidence strength | Application and limits |
|---|---|---|
| [Stress and work-related mental illness among working adults with ADHD: a qualitative study](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-022-04409-w), 2022 | Qualitative interviews with 20 working adults in Sweden; describes organizational challenges, clarity, coping, breaks and disclosure dilemmas | Supports asking about individual barriers and structure/support preferences. | [Davies et al., Autistic adults’ views and experiences of requesting and receiving workplace adjustments in the UK](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0272420), 2022 | Online mixed-method survey of 181 autistic adults in the UK; barriers to requesting/receiving adjustments and reported consequences | Supports offering options proactively rather than placing the whole burden on individuals. | [Venter and Rossouw, Organisational support for employees with dyslexia: An explorative study in South Africa](https://doi.org/10.4102/ajod.v14i0.1597), 2025 | Qualitative study reporting 15 virtual interviews in South Africa; mixed experiences of organizational support and adaptive strategies | Broader workplace context supports individual support conversations; workplace accounts do not test meeting formats, readable fonts or any particular accommodation. Small South African sample; full-text extraction had a completeness warning |
| [JAN ADHD accommodation resource](https://askjan.org/disabilities/Attention-Deficit-Hyperactivity-Disorder-AD-HD.cfm) | Primary workplace accommodation guidance, including breaks, quieter environments, prioritization and written aids | Practical menu, not an experiment or legal advice. JAN explicitly emphasizes variation; no assumption that everyone needs every option |
| [Autistic Self Advocacy Network accessibility resources](https://autisticadvocacy.org/resources/accessibility/) | Disability-led guidance: participation in meetings affecting people, accessible event planning, sensory/cognitive/physical access and Easy Read | Grounds meaningful participation and attention to access barriers. Resource index was retrieved; do not imply every linked toolkit was independently examined or a controlled study |

Do not infer needs from ADHD, autism or dyslexia, or infer diagnosis from behavior.
The dyslexia options in the facilitation reference are adaptable reading and
communication supports, not claims of a tested dyslexia-specific meeting treatment.
A readable document is not necessarily sufficient: offer spoken explanations,
accessible text and time to process, and ask what the person prefers.

## General access guidance

[W3C remote-meetings Editor's Draft](https://w3c.github.io/remote-meetings/)
was retrieved as work in progress. It addresses platform selection, accessible
supporting materials, host/participant practices, and remote/hybrid access.
Its practical guidance supports advance accessible materials and checking access
features, including captions and participation arrangements. An Editor's Draft is
not a W3C Recommendation, member endorsement, or experimental proof; linked
normative standards have their own scope. Do not claim this skill certifies WCAG
compliance or legal compliance.

Options such as processing pauses, camera choice, private preference channels,
explicit turn-taking and separate organizer aftercare combine access-oriented
practice with this skill's judgment. Adapt them to the group and test whether
participants can contribute; do not attach invented effect sizes or scientific
certainty to them. Captions/transcripts require accuracy checks, appropriate
consent and privacy controls. Accessibility is wider than neuroinclusion: consider
hearing, vision, mobility, language, connectivity and assistive technology too.

The bounded research retrieval could not extract current published WAI/WCAG or
British Dyslexia Association pages, nor the linked ASAN event-planning PDF. Their
unread details are not used to substantiate instructions. The retained sources
do not establish one best dyslexia font, camera rule, advance-reading period or
break interval. A formal accommodation process may have jurisdiction-specific
rules; diagnosis-optional ordinary meeting options are not legal advice about it.

## One-on-one professional practice and original scaffolds

- [GitLab Handbook, 1-1](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/leadership/1-1/):
  primary organizational practice, retrieved substantive page body. Locators:
  “The 1-1 Agenda,” “Conducting a 1-1,” and “Career Development Discussion at the
  1-1.” Supports shared agenda contribution, employee voice, private discussion,
  expectations, support and career topics. Its cadence, speaking-share and
  preparation suggestions are company practice, not causal evidence or universal
  requirements. This skill does not adopt surprise questions or fixed speech quotas.
- [CIPD, Performance management: An introduction](https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/factsheets/performance-factsheet/),
  29 January 2026: professional factsheet, substantive body retrieved. Locators:
  “What is performance management?”, “Performance reviews,” and “Principles of
  good performance management.” Supports regular two-way discussion, context-sensitive
  expectations, support, feedback and development alongside formal processes.
  It is broader performance-management guidance, not proof of one best one-on-one
  cadence or a specific meeting script. Linked evidence reviews were not assessed.

The relationship-specific questions, purpose/scale flows, decision contracts,
live recovery phrases, cost arithmetic and aftercare status model are original
practice synthesis. No retained study validates that combined workflow or proves
its performance benefits. Formal performance and disciplinary procedures require
applicable organizational rules; this skill provides no legal assurance.
