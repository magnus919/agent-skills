# Live facilitation: intervene, check, recover

Use the purpose-specific flow in planning and the access reference. Before the
session, agree who chairs, facilitates, watches remote/written input and records
outcomes, combining roles for small meetings. These are proposed roles until
accepted. Establish whether the facilitator can redirect, pause or timebox, and
how the chair will support that authority. An assistant without those permissions
can suggest a move to the organizer; it must not pretend to control the room.

Keep the first intervention short and usable in the affected person's channel.
Pause, listen to the response, then choose the next move. Do not deliver a lecture
about good meetings while the original problem continues.

| Situation | Short opening | Move and contingency |
|---|---|---|
| A senior person interrupts or one voice dominates | “Let’s finish the operational constraint, then come back to you.” | Return the floor or offer written/private input without forced public dissent. Summarize the substantive evidence before closure. If the chair will not support the queue, name the process limitation to the organizer and use an agreed alternate channel or pause; do not promise protection from retaliation. |
| Disagreement repeats without new evidence | “Which fact or tradeoff would change this choice?” | Separate factual claims, values and constraints; summarize both positions for correction. Identify the missing evidence or decision rule. Timebox useful clarification, then propose a specific evidence step or owner call within authority, not more circular debate. |
| An unrelated topic consumes the agenda | “Does this change today’s outcome, or should we park it?” | If relevant, explicitly rescope with the chair and available time; otherwise capture a short thread and proposed disposition. A parking lot is not an assigned task or automatic future meeting. |
| Time expires with work unfinished | “We have five minutes: what can we close responsibly?” | Prioritize the essential question; choose a scoped call, defer, or propose an extension with availability and access checked. Do not assume overtime, waive approval gates or manufacture consensus. |
| Personal attack or coercion replaces discussion | “Pause. Let’s address the claim without attacking the person.” | Restate behavior/issue neutrally. If usable participation cannot be restored, pause or end within the agreed facilitation remit and consult the appropriate organizer/policy route. Do not investigate harassment, demand disclosure or promise confidentiality/retaliation immunity. |
| Access fails or someone missed a proposal | “Pause: the proposal was missed. Let’s restore access before proceeding.” | Provide an accessible restatement, processing time and a workable input route. Record the gap. Distinguish any verified unilateral call from agreement; preserve objections to process without inventing opposition to the substance. |
| Preparation or essential authority is absent | “Can we decide with what we have, or is this exploratory?” | Check the decision contract. Offer brief accessible reading, narrow discovery, async review or deferral as appropriate; do not surprise people with dense compulsory reading or pretend the approval is present. |

## Conflict, power and closure

Invite disconfirming evidence before the most powerful voice closes the question.
Ask “What constraint have we not accounted for?” rather than cold-calling a quiet
person to oppose their manager. A withdrawal after pressure is not proof the
concern was resolved. Preserve the known substantive concern and check privately
whether an appropriate channel can carry it; do not expose a private attribution.
Anonymous channels are anonymous only if their actual controls support that claim.

For a useful disagreement, distinguish “we differ on facts,” “we value outcomes
differently” and “we disagree about who can decide.” Each requires a different
next step. Summarize a proposed call with the authority, constraints and dissent;
verify actual status under the decision contract. Avoid treating facilitator
neutrality as equivalence between a documented approval gate and a preference.

Close with the smallest useful record: outcome or not-decided status; accepted
commitments and known timing; unresolved evidence/approval; and how corrections
will be made. A facilitator's proposed repair is not itself an agreed action.
