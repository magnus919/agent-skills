# Business development while delivering

Use for a solo consultancy, freelance practice, or other capacity-limited service
with a lumpy sales cycle. The trade-off is not sending more messages; it is
keeping the next buying decision moving without breaking present promises.
Keep the mechanics portable: a buyer, a reason to act, and a dated next step.
The sourcing channel, buying cycle, and useful proof vary by market.

## Choose how much time to protect

1. Subtract delivery, administration, support, and recovery from real working
   hours. Put a recurring non-client block early in the week; move it rather than
   dropping it if a client emergency intervenes. Business development is not client
   time. If nothing fits, trade off workload, timing, or the promise explicitly.
2. Use a **maintenance rhythm** when booked. Example if three hours actually fit:
   75 minutes for follow-up and a few warm touches; 75 for one or two buyer/partner
   conversations and recaps; 30 to review the pipeline. If only two hours fit,
   shrink the plan. Do not add unplanned evenings by default.
3. When underbooked, test a short, larger sourcing block; spend the extra time on
   buyer conversations, relevant introductions, and distribution of one proof
   asset rather than administration. Pick an actual time cap and review after
   several weeks. These schedules are hypotheses, not successful-consultant norms.

## Work the closest credible opportunity first

- **Current or former buyers:** discuss a specific follow-on outcome or renewal
  before the engagement ends. Work back from procurement lead time and the
  booked-through date to set a renewal/replace decision checkpoint. Do not assume
  an extension, and do not overbook on the assumption it will fail.
- **Trusted connectors:** tell a former colleague, complementary specialist, or
  referral partner the kind of buyer, visible trigger, and decision you can help
  with. Ask for one relevant introduction rather than a broadcast. Keep client
  obligations, conflicts, and confidentiality in view.
- **New buyers:** look for a concrete trigger such as a new mandate, an integration,
  or a stuck rollout. Show one approved or clearly synthetic example of the work.
  An AI adviser might show how a workflow assessment changes which pilot gets
  funded, rather than publish generic takes or do a free bespoke assessment.
  If a partner can bring actual buyers, a short briefing can teach one useful
  decision and point to one next step. Track interest, attendance, and paid
  follow-through separately. Do not treat audience size as demand.
  Adapt proof to the field and buyer: for a local photography service, a sample
  shot list and turnaround schedule could be more useful than a public article.
  Where prior customers are unlikely to buy again, work complementary referral
  partners or a buyer-relevant marketplace instead of inventing repeat demand.

A discovery conversation should establish the decision, owner, timing, and a
plausible paid next step. Log source, stage, next action, and date in a plain
table or existing CRM. Keep signed work, active opportunities, and relationships
without a current buying event separate. Review booked-through date and
concentration as well as relevant touches, qualified conversations, scoped
offers, and wins. Many touches with no conversations suggest revisiting buyer,
channel or ask. Good conversations with no scoped action suggest revisiting
urgency, authority, or the offer. Draft only unless external actions are authorized.
