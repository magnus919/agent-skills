# Models and worked decisions

These are synthetic worked examples, not observed founder outcomes. The equations
are planning aids; specialist financial work owns detailed modeling.

## Capacity-constrained service

A founder has 12 hours/week: 3 sales, 1 administration, 2 support/reserve leave
6 delivery hours. A fixed-scope report takes 3 hours including review, so capacity
is two reports. At $400 each and $40 cash variable cost, cash contribution is
$360/report before fixed costs and owner compensation. At a $60/hour labor
valuation, economic contribution is $180/report. Neither figure is net income.
If fixed cash costs are $100/week and desired owner draw is $1,000/week, the cash
requirement is ceil(1100/360) = 4 reports, beyond capacity. Revise price, promise,
income target, or resources; do not schedule four reports in six hours. A pilot
may still test demand at an explicitly capped loss; it does not validate the goal.

## Attractive revenue, impossible support

A subscription pays $30/month but uses $8 in infrastructure and $28 in measured
support/review labor. Contribution is -$6 before fixed costs. More identical
customers increase the loss. Test onboarding/scope/pricing changes with actual
support measurements, or stop this offer. Do not invent a positive break-even.

## Service to product

Five bespoke clients are not five repeatable workflows. Log the same repeated
problem, inputs, exceptions, review cost and willingness to buy a standard
outcome. Test one common deliverable with exclusions before building software.
A customer request that doubles review invalidates the old margin estimate.

## Automation versus a contractor

A repeated task takes 4 hours/week manually. An automation needs 10 hours setup,
then 1.5 hours review and 0.5 hours incident recovery weekly. It saves 2 hours/week
before tool cost and pays back setup after five weeks if quality is equivalent.
A contractor may reduce delivery time but still need onboarding and review.
Compare over the intended usage horizon, including failure/exit cost. These are
hypotheses until comparable accepted outcomes have been measured.

## Practical differences by model

Services can reach cash sooner but are constrained by delivery and selling hours.
Standardized services reduce variation only if exclusions are enforced. Products
can separate units sold from delivery hours but retain acquisition, support,
updates and reliability costs. Content and commerce add audience/platform or
inventory/fulfillment exposure. None is inherently passive or reliably low risk.
A lifestyle goal favors sustainable owner earnings and autonomy; a venture goal
requires a credible larger market and financing fit. Neither goal changes the
need to deliver the actual promise.
