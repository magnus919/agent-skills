# Site portfolio strategy

Use when coordinating domains, subdomains, regional sections, brands or directories. Domain count is an input cost, not an outcome. Common ownership and AI use do not by themselves invalidate a portfolio; purpose, truthful identity and distinct usefulness govern the decision.

## Plan ownership before production

Inventory existing properties, brands, URLs, redirects, canonicals, backlinks, query/page observations, conversion destinations, credentials/access owners, maintenance liabilities and retirement constraints. For each proposed site document its audience/problem, real offering, independent reason to exist, accountable owner, fulfillment, evidence and relationship to other properties. Use `templates/search-portfolio-plan.md`.

Assign a primary destination for each coherent intent and identify overlaps. Do not assume competing domains create incremental demand: they can split resources or compete for the same users. Record what each property adds; justify intentional overlap and measure it. Canonicals are signals for substantially equivalent URLs, not a guarantee or a substitute for a useful architecture. Consolidation and migration may outperform adding properties; use appropriate technical SEO and release planning for redirects, links, sitemaps, monitoring and rollback. Never delete a property or change DNS without authorization.

## Links and search-policy boundaries

Within a site, hub-and-spoke or other navigation should help the reader reach related services, locations and answers. Avoid compulsory link quotas. Between sites, link when it genuinely helps users and clearly represent commercial/ownership relationships. Do not build reciprocal/ring links primarily to manipulate ranking, disguise common ownership, or describe non-interlinking as immunity from detection.

Google's spam policies cover doorway structures (including similar regional sites funneling users onward), scaled low-value content made primarily to manipulate rankings, and link spam. Creating multiple sites to hide scaled content is explicitly within the policy concern. A useful independently complete destination is a better design objective, not a certification of compliance. Site-reputation abuse concerns exploiting a host's established signals with third-party content; it is not a blanket prohibition on multiple domains. Check current region-specific enforcement rather than copying a universal rule.

## Commercial operating model

Name whether this is the provider's own acquisition site, authorized client service, rent, accepted-lead fee or revenue share. Define lead acceptance, duplicate/spam handling, refunds, attribution, fulfillment expectations, complaints, reporting, collections and termination with the commercial owner. SEO cannot infer authority to contact providers or make contracts. Route agreements and jurisdiction-specific compliance to qualified review.

Calculate contribution using collected revenue, not contractor gross job value:
- Rent: collected rent minus hosting, content upkeep, calls, tooling, support and allocated acquisition costs.
- Per-lead: accepted nonrefunded leads × realized price minus all acquisition and handling costs.
- Revenue share: attributable completed jobs × collectible agreed share minus operating costs.

Include unsuccessful sites, labor, provider churn, rejected leads and dispute time. Include domains/renewal changes, phone numbers/minutes, voice AI, monitoring, forms, data, compliance, backups, security and subscriptions. Check hosting plan project, traffic, deployment and commercial-use limits. An illustrative $11 annual domain × 1,000 is $11,000/year before these costs, not a current quote or a viability forecast. Route fuller unit economics to `capacity-and-cost-engineering` and commercial assumptions to `go-to-market`.

## Pilot, scale and retirement

Agree a bounded pilot, budget, observation period, maintenance capacity and stop conditions before external actions. Separate technical readiness, indexation, ranking, qualified leads, collected revenue and incremental profit. No fixed month-one/month-three ranking promise. Record cohorts and failures, time to qualified lead, cost/accepted lead, complaints, collection rate and provider retention. A before/after rise does not establish causality; seasonality, concurrent changes and provider mix matter. Use `product-analytics-and-measurement` for tracking contracts and `data-scientist` for experiment design.

Use `templates/rollout-review.md` to decide expand, repair, consolidate, retire or insufficient evidence. A thousand sites sharing one acquisition channel, generator and provider assumption share correlated risk. Do not scale on impressions, a favorable screenshot or one successful site alone.

## Portfolio observability contract

Specify a shared property registry and metric definitions before a central dashboard. Keep search impressions/clicks, observed visits, outbound provider handoffs, accepted leads, completed jobs and collected revenue as separate stages. Record source system, property ID, date/time zone, attribution window, sampling/consent loss and join coverage. An outbound link click is not a sale. Downstream outcomes require an authorized partner-side event or reconciliation agreement; absent access means unknown, not zero or inferred conversion. Do not put personal data in referral URLs or join identifiers without appropriate permissions. Route instrumentation and dashboard implementation to the owning measurement/tool skills; the pilot must disclose missing coverage and comparability rather than imply a complete cross-property funnel.

For architecture cost control read `references/domain-architecture-economics.md`; for search collection read `references/search-portfolio-collection.md`. Use `product-analytics-and-measurement` for the cross-source portfolio contract instead of duplicating its event and privacy methodology here.
