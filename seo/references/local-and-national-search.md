# Local and national search

Use this reference for service-area, multi-location, regional, national, or local lead-generation work. Start with business reality, not a city × keyword multiplication target. Policy facts below were reviewed 2026-11-06; recheck first-party guidance for the relevant provider and jurisdiction before launch.

## Establish the operating footprint

Distinguish an actual staffed location, a service area, national online availability, and a third-party lead-generation service. Record the operator, brand owner, actual fulfiller, locations, service boundaries, opening/response hours and evidence owner in `templates/location-evidence-register.md`. A target market is not proof that a service is offered there. Unknown licensing, insurance, prices, financing and response promises stay unknown, never plausible generated facts.

Google organic results and Maps/Business Profiles are different surfaces. Google's local guidance names relevance, distance and prominence; a city website does not move the business closer to a searcher. Lead-generation companies are ineligible for Business Profiles. An authorized agency may manage an eligible real provider's profile with permission. Do not invent offices, practitioners or profiles for each site. Virtual mailing addresses do not qualify; coworking and service-business locations have specific staffing, signage and customer-contact conditions. The approximate service-area guidance for a profile is not a national website coverage rule.

## Research demand without assuming viability

For a named service, geography and audience, record observed queries, search date/location, result types, existing providers, seasonality, urgency, useful source data and fulfillment capacity. SERPs and vendor keyword volumes are observations or estimates, not proof of customer demand, attainable ranking or willingness to pay. Compare local organic, map results, national information and transactional tasks separately. Use `go-to-market` for customer/problem, commercial partner and channel validation.

Map query clusters to useful destinations with `templates/intent-ownership-map.md`. Do not generate the full service × city × audience matrix automatically. A location page earns its place through material differences: verified coverage, local projects/staff, service constraints, actual availability or jurisdiction-specific procedures that affect the answer. City-name swaps, unrelated trivia and invented landmarks do not create value. Merge equivalent intents; one page may answer many variants. A national page should address a national task rather than impersonating local presence.

## Architecture decision

Compare improving an existing site, adding service/regional sections, separate brands/domains, or a useful directory. Prefer the simplest architecture that serves the actual business and users. Separate sites need a positive reason (distinct offering, audience, entity, service experience or operating owner), not a presumed exact-match-domain advantage. Google limits excessive credit for exact-match domains. Age, static HTML, schema, indexing submission and a matching domain name are not ranking guarantees.

If a directory is useful, disclose ownership, paid placement, partner relationships and selection criteria. Do not imply an independent recommendation while secretly privileging the operator's own lead buyers. Route user journey and navigation to `product-design-and-ux`; brand identity to `brand-designer`.

## Completion and stop conditions

Deliver a sourced footprint register, intent map, architecture decision and unresolved claims. Hold publication of unsupported claims or unavailable services. Stop expanding a geography when reliable fulfillment, distinct usefulness or an accountable maintenance owner is absent. This is not a blanket rejection of large catalogs or legitimate multi-location organizations.

## Sources and scope

- [Google business representation](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177): profile identity, location and service-area requirements; not a rule for all organic pages.
- [Google profile eligibility](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en): ineligible lead-generation businesses and authorized representatives.
- [Google local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091): provider's stated local factors, not a predictor of rank.
- [Google ranking systems](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide): page-level systems and exact-match-domain system.
