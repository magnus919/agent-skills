# Content maintenance, consolidation and retirement

Use before deleting, noindexing, redirecting or substantially changing old content. Age, weak impressions or an anecdotal traffic win are not sufficient reasons to delete a page. Old material can retain historical, support, accessibility, backlink or contractual value.

## Inventory before action

For each URL capture purpose, current truth, business relevance, ownership, actual traffic window, qualified conversions, backlinks, internal links, support use, seasonal demand, index/canonical state and update effort. Record unavailable metrics as unknown, not zero. Separate low impressions, low clicks and low qualified outcomes. Preserve a dated baseline and content snapshot.

Classify with a reason:

| Choice | Appropriate use | Verification |
|---|---|---|
| Keep | Accurate, useful, distinct content, including historical material | Correct date/version context and links |
| Update | Valuable intent with stale facts or missing evidence | Revalidate claims, metadata, links and any schema |
| Consolidate | Overlapping pages answer substantially the same need | Preserve unique useful material and a genuine destination |
| Archive | Historical content remains useful but must not imply current service or advice | Explicit historical label, navigation and access policy |
| Retire | No remaining purpose, irreparable harm or no defensible maintenance path | Approved removal behavior and recovery plan |

Redirect to a genuinely relevant replacement when consolidating or moving content. If there is no replacement, deliberate removal can return an appropriate 404/410 rather than redirecting everything to the homepage. A canonical is a consolidation signal, not a removal mechanism. `noindex` requires crawl access to be observed and is not a privacy control; robots blocking alone does not guarantee removal from search. Use current provider documentation and the CMS/hosting owner for implementation.

## Approval and rollback

Present the exact affected URLs, rationale, user impact, intended response/redirect/noindex behavior, backup location, recovery procedure and approval owner. Research or an audit never authorizes deletion. Preserve user changes and obtain the required authorization before mutation. Sensitive content exposure may need a separate urgent remediation process; do not leave it public merely to collect an SEO baseline.

Execute a small cohort when risk allows. Update internal links, canonical targets and sitemap entries consistently. Test final public responses, redirect destinations, redirect loops/chains, rendered content and crawler signals. Watch affected user journeys and relevant query/page cohorts over an appropriate seasonal window. Roll back if predeclared usability, conversion or technical guardrails fail.

## Interpret outcomes honestly

Track simultaneous changes, campaigns, seasonality, provider/model changes, demand and index delays. Losing impressions while gaining clicks can reflect a changed query mix; it does not itself prove better authority, more revenue or that pruning caused improvement. A new client in the same week is not causal evidence. Do not reproduce a practitioner's deletion count, percentage or timing as a target.

Stop when the requested cohort has documented dispositions and approved final-boundary checks, or when evidence/access is insufficient. Deliver a review queue rather than bulk-removing uncertain pages.
