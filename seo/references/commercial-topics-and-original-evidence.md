# Commercial topics and original evidence

Use when deciding which questions deserve investment or replacing generic content with evidence the business can substantiate. Revenue alignment is a prioritization lens, not permission to make unsupported claims or ignore useful noncommercial content.

## Topic selection

Build a small initial portfolio from verified offers and customer decisions. The number of topics is a capacity choice, not a rule that every business needs three or four pillars.

1. Inventory actual offers, customer groups, eligibility, delivery capacity and commercial importance. Ask for aggregate ranges if exact revenue is unnecessary; keep confidential economics out of public source tools.
2. Gather consented/anonymized customer language from sales, support and interviews, then supplement with public questions and observed search results. Distinguish a frequent question from a profitable one.
3. Identify gaps between what the business sells and what its public information explains. Evaluate topic fit, distinct evidence, intent, maintenance cost, competition and conversion path. Do not turn current revenue mix into a fixed publishing quota.
4. Map coherent questions to existing canonical pages before proposing new ones. Give a new page distinct utility, not merely a new query phrase.
5. Choose a bounded pilot and write the business hypothesis, baseline, effort budget, owner and stop condition. Route market strategy to `go-to-market` and causal tests to `data-scientist` when required.

Use `templates/topic-proof-map.md`. An expensive high-revenue offer with no verified capacity may be a poor acquisition target. A small offer may matter as onboarding or retention; document the reasoning rather than optimizing clicks alone.

## Proof inventory

Prefer evidence a reader can inspect: original measurements, versioned benchmarks, worked examples, demonstrable methods, first-hand case studies, transparent trade-offs or qualified expert reasoning. Originality is not sufficient: the evidence must answer the target question and be reliable.

For each evidence asset record:

- source owner, collection period, population and sampling method;
- metric definition, denominator, baseline/comparator and exclusions;
- what was observed versus inferred, plus plausible confounders;
- client consent, confidentiality, data rights and permitted level of aggregation;
- limitations, relevant conditions and update trigger;
- the exact claims and canonical pages it supports.

A named testimonial reporting “10x ROI” is the customer's statement unless attributable measurement establishes that ratio. Do not upgrade a testimonial into a measured causal return. Security incident stories need privacy and security review; remove client identifiers and exploit-enabling details unless authorized and appropriate. Never invent customers, research, results or editorial independence.

## Write for comprehension and accurate extraction

Put a concise answer or summary near the relevant heading when it helps readers. Include scope and qualifications in the extractable passage, then method, evidence, counterexamples and useful next actions. A summary should agree with the full article; numeric claims need units, time windows and denominators. Preserve author expertise and substantive detail.

Do not force fixed word counts, keyword repetition, “citation bait,” statistics without context, or a universal first-third placement formula. Tables and bullet lists are useful only when they improve understanding. Topic expertise is demonstrated through useful evidence and maintenance, not a promised number of posts or proprietary “AI authority” score.

## Decision gate

Publish only with authorized approval and support for consequential claims. Measure the funnel separately: eligible/available content, observed retrieval, accurate citations or recommendations, visits, qualified inquiries, fulfilled sales and retention. Record assisted/referral/self-reported attribution separately. A story can be valuable without measurable AI citations, and more citations can be commercially useless.
