# Adapting a message without losing its meaning

Read when translating a message brief into brand voice or channel examples. Use
`references/customer-message-handoff.md` for intake and approval boundaries. For
complete source-to-copy examples, load `go-to-market` and its
[messaging examples](../../go-to-market/references/messaging-worked-examples.md).
These are fictional writing exercises, not customer or performance evidence.

## Decide what can change

Before adapting copy, mark the semantic anchors: audience, offer, mechanism,
differentiator, proof scope, prerequisites and action commitment. Tone, length,
rhythm, emphasis and explanatory order may vary. A shorter asset may omit details
that are irrelevant to its claim, but it must not contradict or overstate them.
An essential qualifier travels with the claim it qualifies.

| Choice | Preserve | May adapt |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | Useful offer or customer task | Lead with category, outcome or alternative according to intent |
| Supporting line | Mechanism and material claim limits | Vocabulary, sentence length and detail |
| Call to action | Actual commitment and consequence | Voice only where the action remains recognizable |
| Proof caption | What the evidence really shows | Presentation and plain-language explanation |
| Imagery | Actual product/service scope and illustration status | Composition, mood and style within approved identity |

Do not automatically rewrite everything into “We help you…” or a cheerful tone.
Choose expressions consistent with the supplied voice attributes. Explain how the
word choices demonstrate them; an attribute list without copy is insufficient.

## Example A: local service, warm and unhurried

Source brief: monthly maintenance for small enclosed Northfield gardens; written
care record; assessment before scope, price and availability; no tree surgery.

| Channel | Candidate expression | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage opening | Monthly care for your Northfield courtyard garden | Plain service and local relevance; no grand promise |
| Supporting line | A regular visit, a written care record, and an agreed plan for your small garden. | Warm cadence while keeping tangible deliverables |
| Assessment action | Request a garden assessment | Recognizable request, not an instant booking |
| Short service card | Small enclosed gardens. Monthly maintenance. A written record after each visit. | Compact, concrete language; assessment terms remain beside the action |
| Sample caption | An example care record showing visit tasks and follow-up notes | Explains the artifact; does not imply a real customer's result |

Misleading adaptation: “Relax forever. Book your perfect garden today.” It changes
an assessment into booking and ongoing care into a guarantee. Restore the service
and request language; do not merely remove the most extravagant adjective.

A brand card need not carry the whole website. Keep the audience, arrangement and
record in its summary; if it includes the assessment action, retain the relevant
scope/price/availability expectations nearby or omit the action from that artifact.

## Example B: technical product, precise and restrained

Source brief: shared control records linked to multiple review requests, with
approval history. Security review and migration required. Onboarding estimated at
six weeks subject to scope confirmation. No measured time saving.

| Audience/channel | Candidate expression | Same offer, different emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Analyst page opening | Reuse control evidence across review requests | Leads with the supplied activity improvement |
| Analyst supporting line | Link shared control records to multiple requests and keep their approval history. | Explains how it works |
| Security discussion opener | Inspect the approval history behind shared control evidence. | Emphasizes traceability without claiming full security conformance |
| Procurement handoff | Evaluate deployment scope before confirming onboarding terms. | Makes the next decision explicit; no invented price or SLA |
| Demonstration action | Request a scoped demonstration | Preserves evaluation rather than production commitment |
| Onboarding note | Estimated onboarding is six weeks, subject to scope confirmation; security review and migration are required. | Qualification remains adjacent to timing |

Misleading adaptation: “Audit-ready in six weeks, guaranteed.” It turns an onboarding
estimate into a compliance promise. Reject the new meaning even if the sentence
sounds confident and matches the typography.

## Review an adaptation

1. Place the supplied brief and channel drafts side by side. Identify each semantic
   anchor in the actual copy or document why it is not needed in that asset.
2. Check the most compressed asset first: headline, card or action label. Compression
   is where a tentative benefit often becomes a guarantee or the offer becomes vague.
   Preserve important relationships: “one questionnaire linked to many requests”
   must not become an ambiguous phrase such as “linked reviews.”
3. For each changed claim, record original → adaptation → evidence → verdict. If
   the meaning changes, return it to the message owner rather than call it a tone edit.
4. Check whether distinct voice choices are visible in sentences. Do not score voice
   by the presence of adjectives in the guidelines.
5. Provide the revised copy, explain the change, and retain open questions. Review
   status means reviewed documentation; comprehension still needs reader evidence.

brand templates. Route new market promises to `go-to-market`, and resulting page
