# Check final copy against the brief

Read before handing off customer-facing copy or revising claims after a message has
been shortened or adapted. A completed evidence table does not establish that the
headline, objection answer or action text agrees with it. Check the actual words.

## Establish the fact boundary

Use the supplied brief and referenced evidence as the authority, not a plausible
story about how a business of this kind usually operates. Classify each material
input or addition:

| State | What it permits in copy | What it does not permit |
|---|---|---|
| Supplied fact or scoped evidence | A claim within that exact scope | Stronger properties, results or guarantees |
| Proposed choice | An explicitly labeled option for the owner to decide | Presenting a proposed form, process, fee or deadline as an existing commitment |
| Unknown or not evidenced | An owner question; omission of an unnecessary claim | Claiming that the capability, certification or result does not exist |
| Explicit negative fact | The specific exclusion actually supplied | Broader exclusions or criticisms of alternatives |

“No certification information supplied” is unknown, not “uncertified.” “No measured
savings evidence supplied” does not establish zero savings. Conversely, a supplied
“transport is not offered” can be stated as an actual service limit. Keep these
states separate when translating a brief into polished language. This applies to
negative clauses and parenthetical captions too: if actor logging is unknown,
“does not log editor identity” is unsupported even beside a correct unknown-state
table. Inspect both positive and negative factual assertions in final copy.

Do not expand ordinary features into security or operational guarantees. A dated
record supports a date; it does not establish immutability, tamper evidence, signer
identity, encryption, permanence or audit acceptance. A sample report establishes
only the supplied report contents, not teardown procedures, detailed cost estimates
or an accredited service. Ask for those facts if they change the decision. A stakeholder wanting visibility
is not evidence of a product access permission, sharing feature or audience-specific
portal. Explain what the supplied record shows without granting unstated access.

## Plan the new claims before rendering final copy

For a copy-writing handoff, first provide a compact claim-plan table for the
material statements you propose to put in the NEW copy. Quote the proposed claim
and its literal supplied support; mark unsupported claims omit, narrow, or owner
proposal. Then render the requested final expressions from the accepted claims.
This is a concise editing artifact, not a request for hidden reasoning.

A before/after table about the user's old slogan does not perform this check. Nor
does a table saying “unknowns omitted” when your new caption denies a capability.
Audit new positive and negative claims, including parentheticals and action notes.

| Proposed NEW customer-facing claim | Literal supplied support / state | Disposition before rendering |
|---|---|---|
| “The record does not log editor identity” | Identity logging not supplied: unknown | Omit denial; describe only the dated record |
| “Methods are agreed at booking” | Method and resolution process not supplied | Owner question; omit invented resolution stage |
| “A written assessment before approving the larger project” | Written assessment precedes project approval; initial fee applies | Keep; retain fee notice separately |
| “A dated summary of submitted readings” | CSV input and dated summary export supplied | Keep without adding validation or security properties |

Do not fill an empty support cell with a plausible operating practice. Do not add
new mechanisms, terms or exclusions while rendering the final version. A proof
caption can describe what the supplied sample demonstrates without denying unknown
product capabilities. Put unresolved matters in a separate owner-note section.
For short tasks, a few compact rows suffice; still deliver the requested copy.

## Inspect and repair the final expressions

1. Finish the requested draft, then read the actual headline, supporting sentence,
   proof caption, objection answer, price/process note, action and compressed brand
   expression. Check every supplied output, not only the most detailed brief.
2. For each material factual claim, identify the input or evidence supporting its
   exact scope. Also check implications: “before spending anything” promises more
   than “before approving the later contract.” Do not hide a contradictory headline
   behind accurate fine print elsewhere.
3. Look for additions introduced while making the copy concrete: response times,
   payment timing, report contents, integrations, access rules, credentials,
   customer results, technical properties and claims about competitors. Remove or
   narrow unsupported assertions. Unknown methods or commercial terms must not be
   filled by promising they will be defined per review, confirmed at booking or
   supplied after inquiry unless that resolution process was itself supplied. Put optional improvements in a separate owner
   proposal, not in publishable copy as though they already exist.
4. Check cross-section consistency. A price, prerequisite or uncertainty recognized
   in the brief must not disappear or change meaning in the headline, answer or
   action. Separate a paid assessment from a later purchase where relevant. Do not
   invent when the assessment is paid or whether its fee is credited or refundable.
5. Preserve useful specificity. Replace “immutable audit trail” with the supported
   feature, such as “dated record of each update,” rather than with “a better
   solution.” Keep the actual audience, offer and meaningful difference visible.
   Unknown peripheral terms can remain owner questions without blocking the draft.
6. Repair the wording before handoff. Show the final replacements and a short audit
   of consequential changes; do not ship contradictory draft text with a warning
   elsewhere. Keep rejected passages clearly labeled as rejected examples.

| Location / exact draft claim | Support and state | Decision / final replacement | Open owner decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline: “Know before you spend anything” | Assessment has a fee; later contract requires approval | Replace: “Review a written assessment before approving the project” | Assessment fee amount if absent |
| Security pitch: “Immutable review history” | Only dated records supplied | Replace: “A dated record of each review update” | Integrity controls not supplied |
| Action note: “We respond within 24 hours” | Response time unknown | Remove timing; retain the actual request consequence | Follow-up terms |
| Claim table: “No certifications exist” | Certification evidence not provided | Do not assert absence; mark certification status unknown | Confirm status if relevant |

Audit exact snippets, including parentheticals and captions, rather than asserting
that unknowns were omitted while the published text actually denies a capability.

An audit row is evidence of an editing decision, not proof that every claim was
checked correctly. Do not label a draft “verified” merely because it has this table.

## Compact completion check

Deliver the requested copy and confirm:

- Required expressions are present and the supplied differentiator is intelligible.
- Material claims in the actual expressions stay within supplied facts/evidence.
- Hypothetical workflow improvements are labeled proposals outside final copy.
- Unknown facts remain unknown, including possible capabilities and credentials.
- Consequential edits show their input and replacement; unresolved owner questions
  name what is needed rather than replacing the whole answer with clarification.

Route unresolved positioning to `go-to-market`, approved brand adaptation to
`brand-designer`, and behavior decisions to `product-design-and-ux`. A desk check is
not a customer comprehension study, independent claim verification, or proof of
conversion improvement.

## Basis and boundary

This is an authored operational extension from observed synthetic writing failures
in the messaging comparison: fact tables coexisted with contradictory final copy,
and ordinary dated records became unsupported security guarantees. It is not a
formal industry standard or an independently validated grading method.
