# Complete customer messaging examples

Read for an end-to-end demonstration after the method and drafting reference. Both
briefs below are fictional supplied inputs, not interviews, actual customers or
proof of effectiveness. The examples demonstrate reasoning and artifacts, not a
mandatory page order. Brand voice adaptation belongs to `brand-designer`.

## Example A: local garden-maintenance service

### Supplied inputs and evidence map

Fictional service: Courtyard Care, monthly maintenance for small enclosed gardens in
Northfield. Customers currently arrange separate visits whenever work accumulates.
The supplied distinction is a fixed monthly visit with a short written care record.
An initial garden assessment determines scope and price. No service outside
Northfield, no tree surgery, no measured property-value or time-saving result.
Available proof: a sample care record and an actual description of the visit tasks.
Voice: practical, warm, unhurried. Intended page action: request an assessment;
availability is confirmed afterward, with no promised response time.

| Input | State | Copy consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Small enclosed gardens in Northfield | Supplied scope | Name garden type and location |
| Separate ad hoc visits | Supplied alternative | Explain the monthly arrangement |
| Fixed monthly visit + written record | Supplied distinction | Keep both in the selected message |
| Assessment before price | Supplied prerequisite | Do not imply instant fixed-price booking |
| Sample care record | Supplied demonstration asset | Show what is delivered; do not imply customer results |
| Feelings or amount of time saved | Unknown | Do not invent anxiety or a quantified saving |

### Brief and candidate selection

Goal: keep a small garden maintained through an ongoing arrangement. Obstacle:
repeatedly arranging individual visits. Useful help: a monthly maintenance service
with a record of the work. Intended benefit: a clearer ongoing care arrangement.

| Candidate | Headline | Supporting line | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offer-led | Monthly care for your Northfield courtyard garden | A fixed monthly maintenance visit, with a written record of the work. | Choose for unfamiliar local visitors: immediately identifies service and scope |
| Outcome-led | Keep your courtyard garden cared for, month by month | Monthly maintenance for small enclosed gardens in Northfield, with a written care record. | Useful ongoing-care emphasis; location appears later |
| Alternative-led | Arrange ongoing garden care instead of one visit at a time | A fixed monthly visit and written care record for Northfield's small enclosed gardens. | Directly addresses the supplied workaround; longer opening |

Chosen: offer-led. Tradeoff: less emphasis on the inconvenience of arranging visits,
but clearer first identification of the actual local service. No conversion claim.

### Homepage copy

**Monthly care for your Northfield courtyard garden**

A fixed monthly maintenance visit, with a written record of the work.

**Request a garden assessment**

We assess your garden, agree the tasks and price, then confirm availability before
setting up monthly visits. Requesting an assessment does not book a maintenance plan.

**Know what each visit includes**

See a sample care record showing the work completed and follow-up notes. The sample
illustrates the deliverable; it is not a customer testimonial.

**How care gets started**

1. Request an initial assessment of your small enclosed garden.
2. Agree the maintenance scope and price; confirm availability.
3. Begin fixed monthly visits and receive a written care record after each visit.

**Is this the right service?**

We serve small enclosed gardens in Northfield. Tree surgery is outside the service.
We determine the maintenance tasks and price after the assessment.

Secondary path: **View a sample care record** for visitors who want to inspect the
output before requesting an assessment. It supports evaluation rather than competing
with the primary request. Download or viewing behavior still needs UX definition.

### Revision record

Original: “Our passionate team has transformed outdoor spaces for generations.”
Problem: offers no location, arrangement or deliverable; the history claim is not in
the supplied evidence. Replacement: selected opening and supporting line above.

Original: “Book now. Never worry about your garden again.”
Problem: implies immediate booking and an unlimited outcome. Replacement: assessment
action and its explicit consequence. Preserve warmth without inventing a guarantee.

### Brand and UX handoff

Brief A, revision 1; draft for website review, owner unassigned. Stable meaning:
Northfield/small enclosed garden/monthly visit/written record/assessment before
price and availability. Brand designer may change rhythm and tone while keeping
these constraints. Imagery: service-task photograph or labeled sample record, not
an unsupported depiction of a client's results. UX must define assessment inputs,
submission confirmation, availability follow-up and sample-view behavior.

Validation plan: show the draft to intended local prospects and ask what is offered,
what makes the arrangement different, what they must do first, and whether requesting
an assessment books care. No participants or results exist in this example.

## Example B: enterprise compliance evidence service

### Supplied inputs and evidence map

Fictional product: Control Ledger. Analysts currently maintain control evidence in
separate spreadsheets. Reusable control records link evidence to multiple review
requests and preserve approval history. Security leaders need traceability;
procurement needs delivery scope and terms. Security review and data migration are
required, and onboarding is estimated at six weeks, subject to scope confirmation.
Proof: a sandbox demonstrating record reuse and approval history. No measured hours
saved, compliance certification guarantee or procurement terms supplied. Voice:
precise, restrained. Primary action: request a scoped demonstration, not buy now.

| Claim candidate | Support | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Reuse a control record across review requests | Supplied product capability; demonstrable in sandbox | Use; show the mechanism |
| Inspect approval history | Supplied capability | Use for traceability; avoid claiming audit success |
| Save 50% of analyst time | No measurement | Omit; request evidence if later desired |
| Launch instantly | Contradicts required review/migration | Reject; explain prerequisites |
| Six-week onboarding | Supplied estimate, scope-dependent | Retain qualification beside estimate |

### Brief and candidate selection

Audience: analysts evaluating how to manage evidence; security/procurement participate
in a larger purchase. Goal: support multiple review requests without maintaining a
separate copy of every control record. Mechanism: reuse linked records. Distinction:
shared records plus approval history rather than separate spreadsheets.

| Candidate | Opening | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Offer-led | Shared control records for compliance evidence reviews | Explicit category and task; needs supporting mechanism |
| Outcome-led | Reuse control evidence across review requests | Strong analyst relevance; support line must identify the product |
| Alternative-led | Move compliance evidence beyond separate spreadsheets | Relevant alternative; alone does not say how the product differs |

Choose outcome-led for analysts arriving from a review-workflow discussion. For an
unfamiliar visitor, test offer-led instead; no evidence establishes a universal winner.

### Homepage copy

**Reuse control evidence across review requests**

Control Ledger links reusable control records to multiple review requests and keeps
their approval history, so analysts can work from shared evidence instead of
maintaining separate spreadsheet copies.

**Request a scoped demonstration**

Bring a review workflow to discuss. We'll demonstrate record reuse and approval
history in a sandbox; production deployment requires security review and data
migration. The request is not a production rollout or purchasing commitment.
Scheduling and contact terms need confirmation before publication.

**Inspect the mechanism**

In the demonstration, follow one record into multiple review requests and inspect
its approval history. This demonstrates capability; it does not prove time savings
or guarantee a successful compliance assessment.

**A path from evaluation to deployment**

Discuss the workflow and scope → evaluate the sandbox → complete security review
and migration planning → confirm terms and onboarding scope. Estimated onboarding
is six weeks, subject to that scope confirmation.

**Questions by role**

- Analyst: Can a record support multiple review requests? Inspect the sandbox reuse
  workflow rather than accept a generic productivity promise.
- Security leader: What history is retained? Inspect approval history; retention,
  access-control and integration requirements beyond the supplied facts remain open.
- Procurement: What does onboarding cover and cost? Scope and terms need confirmation;
  the page must not invent a price, SLA or migration commitment.

Secondary action: inspect the sandbox workflow if access is available and approved.
Do not advertise a self-serve sandbox link until its access conditions are known.

### Pitch opener and brand handoff

“Control Ledger helps analysts reuse control evidence across review requests using
shared records with approval history. We can walk through your workflow in a scoped
demonstration before evaluating security review and migration.”

Brief B revision 1, unapproved draft. Analyst expression emphasizes reuse; security
expression emphasizes traceability; procurement expression explains scope decisions.
All retain the same capability and delivery limits. Brand designer owns those
expressions, not the underlying positioning or deployment commitments.

### Revision and validation

Replace “Instantly guarantee compliance with our transformative platform” with the
selected opening: the original invents timing and outcome, while the new message
names a usable capability. Keep the product name, but move it after the customer
task when that better fits the supplied visit intent.

Desk review: record reuse, alternative and proof are explicit; scope-dependent
onboarding is retained. Unknown access, retention and commercial terms remain
assigned questions. User study plan: ask each role to explain value, evidence and
next commitment; record differences before deciding whether separate pages help.
No study or conversion experiment is claimed here.
