# Outcome-linked acquisition and operating partnerships

Use when choosing between selling inquiries, appointments, closed work, collected
revenue, or a managed operating service. Start with the service and decision
rights, then price it. A microsite is a possible channel, not the customer product.
This is a decision method, not a contract or permission to operate a sales funnel.

## 1. Select an operating model

| Model | Deliverable to define | Control/dependency to resolve |
|---|---|---|
| Lead referral | Consented inquiry meeting agreed fit criteria | Duplicates, qualification evidence, replacement/refund rules; client owns sale and delivery |
| Appointment service | Qualified, accepted or attended appointment, explicitly distinguished | Calendar capacity, no-shows, rescheduling, provider acceptance |
| Sales assistance | Defined response, qualification and proposal support | CRM visibility, approved claims and pricing, licensed/human escalation |
| Managed operating partnership | Coordinated acquisition through delivered and paid work | Staff, coverage, service levels, collection evidence and remedies |
| Marketplace/merchant structure | Customer transaction and provider fulfillment | Customer contract, payments, refunds, tax, vetting and liability |

Do not silently move up this ladder. More scope can improve influence over an
outcome but increases costs, liability and dependency. Compare fixed, hybrid and
outcome-linked offers rather than assuming a percentage is better. Transaction-
linked compensation already exists; novelty, demand and defensibility remain
hypotheses, not findings. Sources [S1], [S2], [S9] establish mechanisms and risks,
not comparative profitability for a new local-service business.

## 2. Match responsibility to authority and evidence

Fill the role/control matrix in `templates/outcome-partnership-pilot.md`. For each
stage, identify the accountable party, permitted operator actions, evidence,
service level, dependency and failure remedy. In particular, identify who:

- Contracts with the end customer and represents the site's actual role.
- Responds, qualifies, schedules and estimates; sets price and approves discounts.
- Can make claims, bind either party or promise refunds and guarantees.
- Delivers, warrants and insures work; handles complaints and safety escalation.
- Invoices, collects, reconciles and bears nonpayment, refund and chargeback loss.
- Controls domains/content and personal data, including access and exit rights.

Assess partner fit using observed response times, genuine available capacity,
service geography, licensing, quote/close/collection history, customer outcomes,
financial viability and willingness to maintain records. Record unknowns. A
partner whose capacity is already full may divert existing demand rather than
create useful growth. Selection bias can also attract partners with poor sales or
service performance; do not assume risk-sharing fixes those failures.

If the operator cannot influence or verify the promised outcome, narrow the
service and pricing basis or stop. Set concrete response/capacity thresholds and
pause rules for missed contacts, unsafe work, repeated complaints, integration
failure and unpaid fees. Rerouting needs agreed customer notice/consent and a
qualified destination; it is not an automatic remedy. Keep research, drafts,
sending, sales commitments and payment actions separately authorized.

## 3. Write commercial definitions before projecting returns

Record a counsel-reviewed term sheet, not a ready-to-sign contract:

- **Fee event and base:** an inquiry, appointment, accepted order, completed job
  and collected cash are different events. Define eligible net cash if using a
  collected-revenue share, including taxes, tips, pass-through materials,
  discounts, financed proceeds, partial payments, refunds and chargebacks.
- **Payable credit:** new/existing customers, prior opportunities, duplicates,
  source precedence, multi-touch, repeats, renewals, windows and bounded
  post-termination tails. Contractual attribution decides payable credit; it
  does not establish incremental impact.
- **Reconciliation:** source systems, authorized access, close cadence,
  idempotent evidence, disputed/unattributed amounts, adjustment records,
  challenge period, proportionate audit rights, reserves, caps and due dates.
  Never treat an unreported payment as zero or as permission to estimate a fee.
- **Ownership and exit:** domain/content ownership, customer relationship,
  lawful data uses and retention, export/access removal, handover, outstanding
  service duties, reversals and final reconciliation. Avoid perpetual unbounded
  claims and coercive data lock-in.

Use `financial-modeling` for two-sided contribution and the cash/reversal
worksheet. Use `product-analytics-and-measurement` for the portfolio measurement
contract, opaque referral mapping, authorized callbacks/exports, status events
and missing-outcome handling. Do not replace that contract with a deterministic
search-impression-to-invoice join: Search Console visibility is aggregate.

## 4. Resolve jurisdiction-specific gates

Record jurisdiction, sector, recipient/customer type, exact services and funds
flow, responsible reviewer, current authority and review date. Unresolved gates
mean research/draft-only, not a conditional launch recommendation. These US
examples are screening triggers, not global legal conclusions:

| Trigger | Required specialist question |
|---|---|
| Healthcare remuneration/referrals | Does federal program business implicate the Anti-Kickback Statute, and do specific safe harbors or broader state rules apply? No generic patient-revenue-share default. [S5] |
| Mortgage settlement services | Does RESPA prohibit the referral fee or split? Actual-service exceptions do not allow adding referral value to fair-market service value. [S6] |
| Legal services | What does this jurisdiction allow, and does the operating control impair professional independence? NC Rule 5.4(a)(6) includes an administrative/marketing/platform exception with conditions; it is neither a nationwide ban nor nationwide clearance. [S8] |
| Real estate, insurance, finance or licensed trades | Check local licensing, solicitation, fee-sharing and authority to quote or sell; “home services” is not clearance. |
| Shared brand plus operating assistance/control and required payments | Check substance against franchise, business-opportunity and agency rules. A deferred commission or partnership label does not by itself avoid classification. [S7] |
| Checkout, personal data or outreach | Review merchant/funds-flow duties, tax, security, privacy, lawful data rights, consent, recording and channel rules. A payment API is not legal authorization. [S4] |

Public sites must disclose their actual role and commercial relationships, not
invent local offices, reviews, provider qualifications or exclusivity. Quality
and conversion claims need evidence [S3]. Route legal interpretation to
`legal-strategy` or qualified counsel and technical payment operation to `stripe`.

## 5. Validate the buyer problem and run a bounded pilot

1. Pick one service/customer segment and jurisdiction. Document the buyer's last
   lost jobs, current workflow, capacity, acquisition spend, close/collection
   evidence and what they already pay to fix. Use consented research through
   `product-discovery`; do not treat enthusiasm for a percentage as paid demand.
2. Record rival explanations: predictable fees/control may be preferred; service
   costs may erase margin; credited work may have happened anyway; poor partner
   selection may dominate results. Compare at least two bounded offers on
   comparable scope, including fixed or hybrid pricing. Seek paid commitment
   and operational/data cooperation rather than compliments.
3. Before intervention, define baseline, observation unit, cohort maturation and
   feasible counterfactual. Use a holdout or staggered rollout where appropriate,
   accounting for seasonality, geography, capacity and channel substitution.
   Route design to `product-experimentation`/`data-scientist`. A before/after
   increase alone is not causal proof. If no credible counterfactual is feasible,
   label the result attributed and do not claim incremental benefit.
4. Bound partners, sites, services, duration, acquisition investment, operating
   hours, cash-loss budget and authority. Cover a complete sale/delivery/
   collection cycle plus a stated reversal observation period; do not assume a
   universal sample size or short calendar window is enough.
5. Predefine scale, revise, extend and stop decisions with numeric thresholds,
   owner and review dates before seeing results. Require positive contribution
   for both parties after labor/refunds, sustainable cash exposure, reliable
   operations, auditable outcomes and acceptable customer outcomes/compliance.
   An inconclusive pilot is not proof of failure or permission to scale.
6. Stop or narrow for unobservable outcomes, chronic disputes, client losses,
   unacceptable service/compliance risk, or no incremental improvement where it
   can be credibly measured. Validate repeatability with more than one suitable
   partner before multiplying sites. Any extension needs a new bounded budget.

Complete with the filled pilot decision record, economics worksheet, evidence
limits, unresolved gates and next owner. Do not declare the business validated
until the agreed evidence exists. No direct comparative study in this review
proves that microsite outcome partnerships outperform lead sales. Domains,
generic AI content, a commission clause and willingness to lose money are not a
moat. Test possible advantages in service outcomes, authorized data, specialized
distribution and repeatable partner operations with observed evidence.
