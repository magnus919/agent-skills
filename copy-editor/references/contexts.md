# Contexts and responsibility boundaries

Titles and service levels vary. Agree an actual checklist rather than relying on “light,” “medium,” or “heavy” alone.

| Context | Priorities | Frequent traps and handoffs |
|---|---|---|
| Fiction, memoir, narrative nonfiction | Voice, dialogue, viewpoint, tense, chronology, names, character/world continuity; style sheet can include a timeline and fictional lexicon | Deliberate fragments and dialect are not errors. Query contradictions; do not repair a plot by inventing events. Distinguish fictional fact from claims about living people. Route major narrative work to the author/developmental editor. |
| Books and long reports | Front/back matter, chapter apparatus, cross-references, notes, references, art inventory and stable terminology | A chapter edit cannot establish whole-book consistency. Check recurring decisions across all chapters and supply the sheet to production/proofreading. |
| News and magazines | Attribution, faithful quotes, names, dates, headlines/captions, brevity and house style | A stronger headline must not exceed the article's evidence. Flag fairness, source protection, legal or factual problems to the responsible editor. Newsroom fact-checking and publication authority must be assigned explicitly. |
| Scientific, medical, scholarly | Discipline and journal conventions, statistical wording, units, symbol case, citation matching, ethical disclosures and technical queries | Preserve distinctions between association/causation, significance/importance, risk/rate, sample/population and uncertainty. Query implausible doses or contradictory results; never silently correct a treatment instruction or research finding. Scientific validity belongs to qualified reviewers. |
| Technical documentation | Exact commands, API identifiers, paths, placeholders, versions, warnings, preconditions, UI labels | Protect executable strings from typographic changes. Copyediting examples does not establish that commands run. Route execution and behavior checks to the technical owner. |
| Corporate, nonprofit, marketing | Audience, brand, consistency, names, claims, calls to action and necessary qualifications | Do not strengthen “may” to “will” or “up to” to a guaranteed outcome. Route regulated claims and institutional positions to their approval owner. |
| Web, email, UI and accessible content | Headings, descriptive links, concise labels, alt text, captions, localization and character limits | Review strings in context and preserve placeholders/markup. A grammar pass cannot certify accessible interaction or layout. Route rendered review to specialists. |
| Multilingual and international English | Agreed English variety, names/diacritics, locale dates, currencies, measurement, code-switching | Avoid automatic US/UK normalization or English rules applied to another language. Query ambiguous numeric dates; seek a qualified language reviewer when meaning depends on translation. |

Copyediting addresses mechanics and consistency with limited clarity and incidental accuracy checks as commissioned. Line/stylistic editing focuses on language and flow; developmental editing shapes organization/content; proofreading checks the composed/final form. These services can overlap by agreement. Exhaustive verification, specialist authenticity review, permissions clearance and translation require explicit scope and appropriate expertise.
