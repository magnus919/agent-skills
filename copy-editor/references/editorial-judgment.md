# Editorial judgment and difficult material

## Preserve meaning and voice

Before accepting an edit, compare who did what, to whom, when, under what conditions, and with what certainty. Watch negatives, only/except/unless, modal verbs, denominators and comparative baselines. “No evidence of harm” does not mean “evidence of no harm.” Preserve a writer's defensible rhythm and intended register. Plain language helps when it fits the audience; readability scores cannot measure scientific fidelity or artistic quality.

Distinguish conventions from universal correctness. Consult the agreed current source for disputed usage. When style sources conflict, document the project choice and apply it consistently; do not silently reverse an earlier editor's decision.

## Quotations and attributed material

Verify against supplied originals when within scope. Preserve quoted language, including unusual grammar, until you establish whether it is transcription error, source wording, or a permitted editorial adjustment. Query uncertain wording or a proposed ellipsis/bracket change. Never paraphrase inside quotation marks. Preserve context and attribution. Do not automatically insert “sic” or normalize quoted dialect. Translation fidelity needs a competent language reviewer.

## Numbers, units, tables and scientific claims

Check text/table totals, percentages and bases, ranges, dates, unit symbols, minus signs, decimal points, and figure/table callouts. State rounding assumptions; percentages may not sum to 100 because of rounding. Preserve significant digits and uncertainty unless authorized to adjust them. A simple arithmetic check is not statistical validation. Query a discrepant dose, denominator, total or conversion with both locations and ask the owner to resolve it. Do not infer missing values.

## Citations and completeness

Match in-text citations to references and vice versa using the applicable convention; account for exceptions such as personal communications. Compare author names, dates, suffixes and identifiers. Identify missing metadata and verify from the original source if commissioned. Never complete a plausible-looking reference from memory or invent a DOI. A functioning URL does not prove it supports a claim. When scholarly reference verification is commissioned, check correction/retraction status in the appropriate authoritative bibliographic record and query affected citations; citing a retracted work to discuss its retraction is different from relying on it as current evidence. Follow the journal’s instructions for units and reference order; never silently convert clinical values without the responsible expert checking the result. Flag permissions and missing credit lines for the responsible owner.

## Inclusive language and accessibility

Consider self-identification, community preference, context and audience. Avoid imposing a single identity-first/person-first convention on all people. Query harmful stereotypes or unnecessary demographic labels with a specific alternative and reason. Preserve quotation or characterization context while flagging concerns to the decision owner; specialist authenticity review remains separate.

For accessible content, edit informative heading/link text, meaningful image alternatives, understandable instructions and captions/transcripts within scope. Decorative images may need empty alternatives; complex charts need equivalent information beyond a short label. Coordinate heading semantics, reading order and rendered checks with design/accessibility owners. Do not certify compliance from text alone.

## Query examples (original teaching examples)

| Situation | Appropriate response | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| “The trial proved the drug may help” | Ask whether the evidence supports efficacy or only a possible effect; preserve uncertainty meanwhile | Changing “may help” to “helps” |
| Reference says 2021, citation says 2020 | Anchor both locations and ask which source/year applies | Inventing a second reference |
| Fictional speaker uses “ain't” | Leave if deliberate and consistent with voice | Standardizing all dialogue |
| Source quote contains an apparent typo | Check original or query with source location | Quietly rewriting the quotation |
| “03/04/25” for international readers | Query intended date and suggest spelled month once confirmed | Guessing March 4 or 3 April |
