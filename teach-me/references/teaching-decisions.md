# Choosing the next teaching move

These are operational design heuristics informed by the evidence ledger, not a
validated adaptive algorithm. Use the learner's actual attempt and preferences.

| Observation | Useful response | Next evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Novice cannot start | Show one complete worked example, name the relevant prerequisite, then leave one meaningful step to try | Completion of that step with support recorded |
| Rusty expert succeeds on a diagnostic | Skip redundant exposition; use a realistic application with a changed constraint | Independent method selection and justification |
| Wrong but confident | Locate the first divergence, verify the key, give a counterexample or contrasting case | Fresh explanation or prediction using the corrected distinction |
| Correct alternative solution | Check equivalence and assumptions; accept it when valid | Ask about a boundary only if useful to the goal |
| Correct only after hints | Record assisted success; offer a fresh parallel task with less support | Independent performance, not recall of the just-shown answer |
| Recites words without applying them | Change context, ask for a decision and its reason | Application with the same underlying principle |
| Repeated error despite correction | Check ambiguity, prerequisite, and the quality of your explanation; change one major feature of instruction | A smaller attainable task, not another identical demand |
| Explicit frustration or fatigue | Acknowledge the report, offer a concrete different approach or a pause | Learner's choice, not inferred emotion |
| Requests the answer/explanation | Provide a clear worked explanation, then offer optional practice | No claim of independent skill from reading it |
| Needs accessible presentation | Use requested text, audio-compatible notation, descriptions, or pacing; retain the learning goal | Demonstration in an accessible format, not speed or modality preference |

## Build layers without a remedial maze

Work backward from the target task to the prerequisites needed now. Probe only
uncertain dependencies that would change instruction. Example structure:
“choose the method → use its key distinction → apply the procedure → check the
result.” The actual dependency chain comes from subject sources and the task.
Avoid a full beginner course when one missing concept explains the failure.

Separate understanding from execution constraints. A typo, unfamiliar notation,
translation issue, inaccessible diagram, or unavailable tool may mask knowledge.
Ask a short clarifying question before diagnosing a conceptual gap.

## Productive practice

Use retrieval when remembering is part of the goal; allow a real attempt before
revealing the answer. For unfamiliar complex procedures, demonstrate first, then
fade support. Ask a focused self-explanation about why a step applies, rather
than demanding elaborate reasoning for every fact. Provide missing knowledge
when the learner cannot yet explain.

Once constituent procedures are usable, selectively mix confusable problem types
so the learner must choose a method. Do not confuse random topic switching with
useful interleaving. Check a changed context separately from repeated same-format
practice. Far transfer remains untested unless the learner actually demonstrates it.

Choose review timing based on the learner's goal and availability. A later
unassisted recall or application can test retention; no universal review interval
is optimal for every subject and retention horizon. Offer a review suggestion,
not an automatic calendar event or a promise to contact them.

## A compact progress note

Use only when helpful, not after every sentence:

> Goal: [observable task]. Sources: [scope/version].
> Observed: [specific attempt and result]. Support: [none/hint/worked solution].
> Independent application: [observed/not tested]. Transfer: [observed/not tested].
> Delayed retention: [observed after stated interval/not tested].
> Next: [smallest useful task or stop].

Do not turn this into a persistent psychological profile. Neither a simulated
learner's response nor the tutor's confidence is evidence about a real person.

## Decide when to stop

Choose a scope-appropriate fresh task and success criterion with the learner,
using the source-backed goal. For example, correctly choosing and explaining a
procedure on a new instance is different from reproducing a visible example.
The criterion should match the task, not a universal accuracy percentage. Once
met independently, close that goal and invite a break to digest the learning.
An optional question may then invite curiosity: what unanswered questions did this
raise, if they want to continue? Use their answer to define a new bounded goal and
research its subject content before teaching. Do not automatically prescribe a
harder topic. After an explicit stop, do not ask another question.

At a time boundary, summarize the observed evidence and unfinished work. If the
learner says stop, stop immediately; do not insert a final test. When repeated
pivots do not help, acknowledge the limit of this approach and offer a specific
alternative source or qualified human support, as appropriate to the subject.
Do not prescribe more practice merely to keep the conversation going. Pausing is
a valid outcome; it supplies no evidence of mastery or retention.
