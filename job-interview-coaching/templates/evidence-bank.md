For each example, distinguish a directly observed result, a result reported by someone else, an estimate, and an unknown. Do not add a metric merely to make the example sound impressive. Note what the example cannot prove.

Balanced coverage may include learning, a problem solved, a mistake, competing priorities, collaboration, and role-specific craft. Use relevant school, voluntary, caring, project, or paid-work evidence. Missing experience becomes a learning gap or a clearly hypothetical answer, not an invented accomplishment.

Practice: choose a question, select a relevant real event, answer without reading the full outline, then test another question. Do not memorize one script for all prompts.
