# Worked transcript case: candidate and interviewer learning

Read for a substantial example of diagnosis and evidence-to-practice conversion. This is a wholly synthetic interview for a service coordinator; it is not a real candidate record. Added turn IDs are the only locations; no timestamps are available. Model feedback should not treat this example as the answer to a different transcript.

## Role brief

The coordinator manages bookings, communicates changes, maintains records, and escalates decisions outside their authority. The advertised role includes training on the booking system. The candidate has volunteer scheduling experience. Entry criteria are accuracy, handling competing needs, learning, and useful handoffs. There is no requirement to have managed employees or produced revenue.

## Interview record

**T01 Interviewer:** Give me a brief introduction and explain why this role interests you.

**T02 Candidate:** I studied several subjects and then did a short course. My first volunteering was with a local group. There were a lot of different people and I helped in different ways. I am a people person, and I think your organization is a good place to work. I have coordinated volunteer rotas recently, which is probably the closest thing.

**T03 Interviewer:** Tell me about a time you handled a booking problem.

**T04 Candidate:** We were busy and something went wrong with the rota. We always worked together and tried to keep people happy. It worked out in the end.

**T05 Interviewer:** What did you personally do?

**T06 Candidate:** A volunteer could not cover a shift. I checked the current rota and called two people who had said they could cover. One agreed. I told the group lead and updated the shared sheet. Another volunteer contacted the event organizer. I was not responsible for approving new volunteers.

**T07 Interviewer:** How did you know the shift was actually covered?

**T08 Candidate:** I assumed the person would come because they said yes. Later, the group lead told me the shift was covered. I did not check attendance myself.

**T09 Interviewer:** What would you do differently?

**T10 Candidate:** I would make sure the confirmation was visible to the group lead and agree who checks the remaining gaps. We later used a confirmed/pending column. I suggested that, but the lead decided to use it and another person maintained it most weeks.

**T11 Interviewer:** Describe competing priorities.

**T12 Candidate:** I normally multitask. I can work under pressure. For example, I was checking a rota while a volunteer asked about expenses. I finished the rota because the lead was about to send it. I told the volunteer when I could help and checked with the lead where the expenses question should go.

**T13 Interviewer:** You would always put the manager's request first, right?

**T14 Candidate:** Yes, probably. Although if something affected today's event I would check which deadline really mattered and tell the lead what would be delayed.

**T15 Interviewer:** What is your biggest weakness?

**T16 Candidate:** I used to give long explanations when people asked a simple question. A colleague told me it was hard to find the answer. I have started giving the answer first and asking whether they need the details. I still do it when I am nervous.

**T17 Interviewer:** Do you know our booking software?

**T18 Candidate:** I have not used that system. I learned our shared sheet by practising a few common tasks and asking the lead to check my first updates. I would want to understand how your bookings and approvals work before making changes alone.

**T19 Interviewer:** What would you like to ask us?

**T20 Candidate:** What training do new coordinators get, and which changes can they approve themselves? Also, how does the team know a booking issue has been resolved?

**T21 Interviewer:** We will cover that once you join. We need someone flexible. Any other questions?

**T22 Candidate:** Not at the moment, thank you.

## Evidence-led review

### Overall pattern

The candidate has more relevant evidence than the initial generalities suggest. Concrete actions emerge after neutral probes. The highest-value improvement is to retrieve a relevant incident earlier and distinguish verified results from assumptions. The interviewer also leaves key job conditions unanswered and asks one leading question. There is no basis here to predict a hiring outcome or infer personality from text.

| Evidence | Observation and consequence | Intervention |
|---|---|---|
| T02: “I have coordinated volunteer rotas recently” arrives after unrelated background | The most relevant experience is late; listener has to find the connection | Practise an opening that starts with coordination and truthful motivation, then one example |
| T04: “We always worked together” versus T06: “I checked the current rota and called two people” | A neutral probe reveals personal actions; the first response undersells available evidence | Build the incident outline before practising, preserving the teammate's organizer contact |
| T08: “Later, the group lead told me the shift was covered” | There is second-hand confirmation, not no outcome at all; direct attendance verification is not shown | State the actual source of confirmation and avoid claiming a personal attendance check |
| T10: “I suggested that, but the lead decided” | Clear proposal, decision authority, and shared contribution | Retain this distinction; do not rewrite as single-handed implementation |
| T12 gives a deadline and communication action after “I normally multitask” | Specific prioritization evidence is present despite a generic opening | Lead with the time constraint and choice; probe consequences before judging appropriateness |
| T13 supplies the expected answer | The interviewer reduces the value of simple agreement in T14 | Replace with a neutral conflicting-priority scenario and probe the reasoning |
| T16 names feedback, change, and remaining difficulty | A credible development example with an honest limit | Keep the substance; test answer-first behavior rather than require a disguised strength |
| T18 distinguishes no tool experience from a prior learning method | Lack of software familiarity is real; ability to learn has some adjacent evidence | Do not fabricate proficiency; practise explaining the learning approach and checking work |
| T20 asks training/authority/closure questions; T21 does not answer them | Candidate diligence is relevant; role conditions remain uncertain | Prepare a respectful follow-up asking for the work requirement and authority boundary |

### Factual rewrite demonstration

A stronger T04–T10 answer using only supplied facts:

> A volunteer could not cover a shift. I checked the sheet, contacted two people who had offered to cover, and one agreed. I informed the lead and updated the sheet; another volunteer contacted the organizer. The lead later told me the shift was covered. I had not checked attendance myself. Afterward I suggested making confirmed and pending coverage visible, and the lead adopted that approach with another volunteer maintaining it.

This rewrite is a demonstration, not the candidate's demonstrated skill. It preserves both second-hand confirmation and shared credit. Do not add a response time, attendance statistic, or customer-satisfaction result.

## Complete coaching sequence to run interactively

1. **Baseline:** ask the candidate to answer the original booking question without the rewrite in view. Wait.
2. **Self-assessment:** ask where their personal choice and outcome were clearest. If this burdens the user, offer one observation instead.
3. **Feedback:** quote one actual sentence and explain the specific gap. Agree a target: incident, personal action, source of outcome.
4. **Retry:** ask for the same event in their own words. Do not supply every sentence again. Record whether an outline or example was visible.
5. **Transfer:** ask about learning an unfamiliar process, using a different event. If the candidate repeats the rota answer regardless of the question, explain why it is only partial evidence and seek the relevant learning steps.
6. **Challenge:** if the candidate says they personally checked attendance, ask whether this is new information or a correction to T08. Do not silently merge conflicting accounts.
7. **Debrief:** compare baseline and transfer evidence, name what improved, retain gaps, and stop at the agreed bound. Do not announce mastery from a rehearsed answer.

## Interviewer exercise

Redesign T13 as: “Two requests cannot both be completed by the requested time. How would you establish the priority and communicate the decision?” Define the relevant authority and information. Allow comparable neutral probes: deadlines, consequences, alternatives, decision owner, communication. Have interviewers rate the evidence independently before discussion.

For T21, an honest response might explain what is known about training and approval boundaries and identify who can clarify the rest. “We need flexibility” does not answer the candidate's concrete questions. Preserve their time for diligence and give a realistic next-step timeline.

## What this case does not show

Real job performance, actual vocal delivery, the candidate's behavior under a live deadline, technical booking-system skill, or the employer's private decision. A broader transcript review must not cherry-pick only weak openings when later probes contain the evidence.
