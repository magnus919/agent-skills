# What professional interview coaches do

Read when designing a coaching relationship, choosing an intervention, or explaining the reference basis. This comparison uses public descriptions and practitioner speech, not access to private client sessions. It is a critical comparison, not an endorsement or a reproduction of a paid curriculum. Research checked 2026-10-23.

## Publicly documented approaches

| Practitioner / material actually reviewed | Method visible in that material | Implementation consequence | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pamela Skillings / Big Interview, public Answer Builder documentation | Select answer type, outline, refine for the role, then record and review practice | Maintain an evidence inventory and choose an answer structure by question type; separate drafting from performance | Product documentation and commercial claims do not validate outcomes |
| Margaret Buj, public interview-coaching service description | Align experience with target role, run a focused mock, refine stories and delivery, tailor session scope | Diagnose using an actual attempt and prioritize the user's immediate stage or difficulty | A service page does not reveal a complete coaching curriculum or independent efficacy |
| Sarah Johnston, public Mac's List interview transcript | Understand the employer's needs, prepare a useful opening narrative and adaptable examples, rehearse, engage in reciprocal conversation | Teach relevance and truthful employer diligence alongside answer preparation | Rapport advice is culturally dependent; shared background must not become a hiring criterion |
| Andrew LaCivita, public Interview Intervention course outline | Begin with career needs, develop explanations, ask questions to evaluate the employer, close and follow up | Treat candidate decision quality as an outcome and prepare unanswered questions | The paid course and book were not read; advertised success percentages are not adopted |
| Lou Adler, public two-question interview article | Examine comparable accomplishments and reasoning about a real job problem | Connect retrospective evidence to prospective judgment; probe contribution and context | His commercial framework is not a universal or locally validated selection instrument |
| Gayle Laakmann McDowell, official book overview | Learn problem-solving strategies and underlying concepts, with progressively supported technical practice | Separate technical mastery from performance polish and label assistance | Overview reviewed, not the entire book or its question bank |
| Victor Cheng, public candidate-led case article | Candidate actively investigates an open problem and updates the direction of analysis | Rehearse clarification, hypothesis testing, synthesis, and adapting to new facts | Historical employer-format claims need checking against current invitations |

## Synthesis: a coach changes the cause of the difficulty

This skill's protocol is an original synthesis. A coach should not prescribe a more polished story until they understand whether the candidate has relevant experience, can retrieve it, understands the question, can reason through the task, and can communicate what they know under the interview conditions.

An effective session therefore alternates elicitation, instruction, demonstration, rehearsal, feedback, and reflection. Purely nondirective questioning can leave someone without an answer structure they need. Purely directive rewriting can leave them dependent on a script. Select the least support that helps them make the next useful attempt, then reduce support.

For interviewer coaching, assess elicitation before judging candidate evidence: a vague, compound, or leading question can produce a weak record. Help the interviewer define the capability, remove ambiguity, allow a neutral follow-up, and practise distinguishing missing evidence from negative evidence.

## Methods to reject or adapt

- Memorized scripts: retain a factual outline and flexible examples; test interruption and variation.
- Universal presentation rules: offer context-sensitive clarity feedback; do not impose eye contact, accent, personality, or fixed timing.
- Guaranteed outcomes: distinguish improved practice performance, interviewer ratings, offers, and actual job performance.
- Unlimited polishing: agree a target, make a bounded retry, and test transfer instead of continually moving the standard.
- Candidate sales tactics as interviewer criteria: rapport may help a conversation, but scoring requires job evidence.
