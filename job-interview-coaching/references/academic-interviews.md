# Academic, research, and fellowship interviews

## Basis and task selection

[Oxford interview-format guidance](https://www.ox.ac.uk/careers/careers-guidance/job-search-and-applications/interviewing/types-of-interview) describes academic presentations and teaching tasks. Use the actual institution's invitation and discipline criteria; this is job/fellowship coaching, not a universal student-admissions guide.

Distinguish research assistant, technician, postdoc, independent fellowship, teaching appointment, and faculty leadership. Obtain presentation audience and duration, research versus teaching emphasis, proposal requirements, and resource assumptions. Do not assume a permanent faculty research program is expected of a technician.

## Evidence to develop

Prepare contribution, methods, competing explanations, limitations, next research steps, feasibility, collaboration, supervision, and teaching where relevant. A research talk and a proposal defense ask different questions. The former explains evidence obtained; the latter must distinguish planned tests from completed results.

## Worked contrast

Original synthetic question: **What is the strongest limitation of your research result and what would you investigate next?**

Weak example: “There are no major limitations; I would just collect a bigger dataset.”

Stronger example using different, explicitly fictional facts: “The result comes from one recruitment setting, so I cannot assume the same relationship in other settings. I would first check whether the proposed mechanism survives a deliberately different setting, with the outcome and exclusion rules defined in advance. A larger sample in the same setting could improve precision but would not alone resolve that external-validity question.”

The stronger answer identifies the type of uncertainty and aligns the next study to it. It does not establish that the underlying design or statistical interpretation is sound. A discipline expert must assess that. Coach clarity without replacing the candidate's real intellectual contribution with an impressive invented research agenda.

Use this contrast to explain evidence quality, not to put fictional achievements into the user's answer. For a real transcript, any improved autobiographical answer must use only supplied or subsequently confirmed facts.

## Neutral probes

- What finding would challenge your explanation?
- Which part of the work did you design or execute personally?
- What resources and collaborations does the next step actually require?

## Candidate practice

Explain the contribution in two minutes to a nonspecialist and then defend one method with a specialist. Rehearse a question outside the prepared slide order. For teaching, specify the learning objective and a check for understanding; do not equate audience engagement with learning evidence.

Transfer task: Ask how the argument changes for another population or method, or how a teaching explanation would change for learners with different prior knowledge.

Keep the baseline, coached retry, and independent transfer attempt distinct. Ask one question at a time and wait. If the user wants a simulation, withhold coaching until the agreed debrief. If they want an explanation or plan, do not force a mock.

## Responsibility-level adaptations

Assistant/technician: careful execution, records, and appropriate escalation. Postdoc: independent analytical contribution and collaboration. Fellowship/faculty: coherent questions, feasible program, teaching and mentorship as required. Academic leader: allocation, research environment, integrity, and people development.

A title alone is insufficient. Ask what independence, complexity, impact, and decision authority the role actually requires. Early-career candidates may use relevant education, voluntary, caring, or project evidence; experienced candidates should explain the scope and limits of their contribution.

## Interviewer practice and candidate experience

Define whether you assess past research, future promise, teaching, or leadership. Do not use unstructured prestige comparisons as a substitute for evidence. Give candidates enough detail to prepare the correct talk and judge questions against role expectations. Respect unpublished and confidential work.

For interviewer coaching, rehearse the probes against both a vague and a concrete answer. Record what evidence the question elicited, what remains missing, and whether a hint supplied the response. A friendly conversation is not itself a complete assessment. Give candidates space to understand the work and ask informed questions.
