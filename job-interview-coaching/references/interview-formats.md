# Interview formats

Use the invitation and employer's current instructions to establish the format. Stage names such as “screen” and “final” do not determine what is assessed.

| Format | Candidate coaching | Interviewer design |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiter screen | Concise background, motivation, essential requirements, questions | Explain role and process; distinguish logistics from capability assessment |
| Behavioral / competency | A relevant real event, personal action, rationale, outcome, learning | Common core questions, neutral probes, anchored evidence |
| Situational | Clarify missing facts, priorities, proposed action, contingencies | Job-relevant scenario with sufficient information and acceptable alternatives |
| Technical / troubleshooting | Clarify, plan, reason or implement, test, acknowledge limits | Suitable problem, clear tools and assistance rules, separate correctness and reasoning |
| Business case | Clarify decision, tailor structure, analyze, synthesize | Consistent facts and staged information, realistic decision criteria |
| Portfolio / work history deep dive | Explain brief, constraints, authorship, alternatives, feedback, outcomes | Verify contribution; do not mistake production values for skill |
| Demonstration / audition / practical | Rehearse representative task under appropriate conditions | Safe, bounded, job-relevant sample with clear criteria |
| Teaching / research talk | Adapt to audience; support claims, methods, learning goals, limitations | Define audience, task, time, equipment, and how questions affect scoring |
| Panel | Address question clearly and handle different perspectives | Assign questions and note-taking; rate independently before discussion |
| Group exercise | Collaborate, listen, reason, help progress without monopolizing | Observe actual contribution, not loudness or dominance |
| Asynchronous video | Check preparation time, retakes, submission limits, access needs | Explain platform and criteria; avoid unsupported automated trait inference |
| Leadership / executive | Explain mandate, decisions, tradeoffs, governance, outcomes | Probe judgment and accountability rather than status or charisma |
| MMI / stations | Practise reasoning across separate bounded scenarios | Distinct station criteria and trained assessors; do not assume all healthcare jobs use MMI |

STAR fits many past-experience answers. It is not a mandatory wrapper for every technical explanation or case recommendation. Clarification, reasoning, action, checking, and reflection can appear in different valid orders.

For cases, prefer original synthetic scenarios and official public practice materials. Do not distribute confidential interview content. For practical tasks, a conversational mock can test explanation and judgment but cannot certify manual technique, safe equipment use, clinical competence, or live performance.

For take-home tasks, verify permitted tools and the user's expected authorship. Help with transparent preparation or permitted collaboration. Do not covertly complete a restricted assessment. For live interviews, support disclosed and permitted assistance rather than hidden answer feeding.

Sources: Oxford interview formats, Microsoft technical interviewing, McKinsey interviewing, Amazon interview loop, AAMC residency design and Medical Schools Council interview overview. Employer examples describe those organizations, not every employer.
