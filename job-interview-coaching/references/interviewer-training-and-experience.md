# Train interviewers and improve candidate experience

Read for a hiring team's process or interviewer coaching, beyond a one-off question list. These exercises are original adaptations of structured interviewing and public employer guidance.

## Make the job evidence explicit

Start with outcomes and critical situations. For each capability write what an entry-ready person must demonstrate, which facts distinguish weak from adequate evidence, and whether an interview can observe it. A manual skill may require a supervised demonstration; a job-specific knowledge claim may need a checked technical task. Do not expand the interview into a test of everything desirable.

Build a short interviewer guide: capability, core question, neutral probes, acceptable alternative approaches, behavioral anchors, assistance rules, time, and note-taking fields. Keep trainable-after-hiring skills separate from entry requirements. Test the question with fictional weak, adequate, and strong answers before using it.

## Rehearse elicitation

Give the interviewer a vague answer and ask for one follow-up. Compare “You escalated, right?” with “What did you do next?” The former supplies content; the latter elicits it. Then give a concrete answer and check whether the interviewer recognizes evidence without continuing to probe mechanically.

Practice recoveries: explain a misunderstood question without giving the answer, allow thinking time, handle a digression respectfully, and preserve the candidate's question time. Use documented probe opportunity, not identical wording regardless of relevance. Accommodations should preserve the assessed capability rather than enforce unnecessary mechanics.

## Calibrate using actual response evidence

Ask raters to identify excerpts and apply anchors independently. In discussion, locate the reason for disagreement: different facts noticed, different interpretation, vague anchors, or an unasked question. Revise a question or anchor when needed and reassess comparable candidates consistently. Do not let group agreement make an unsupported impression true.

Useful practice contrasts:

- Candidate speaks fluently but never explains a personal action.
- Candidate is hesitant but gives a concrete method and verified outcome.
- Candidate lacks an example because the interviewer changed topics.
- Candidate names a safety concern without the technical knowledge to resolve it alone.
- Candidate proposes a reasonable alternative to the interviewer's preferred approach.

Explain what each response demonstrates and what remains untested. “Not demonstrated” is an evidence state, not the lowest ability score. Record contradictory evidence separately and validate technical judgments with a domain owner.

## Candidate experience as part of the design

Before the interview, state format, duration, assessed areas, preparation burden, permitted tools/AI, equipment, logistics, and access contact. During it, explain the agenda and note-taking, listen, keep time, give accurate role information, and allow useful questions. After it, communicate the next step and reset expectations when a timeline changes. Draft communications within scope; do not send them without authorization.

Google re:Work's current public candidate-experience guide describes preparation, logistics, communication, and feedback throughout the journey. Its interviewer-training guide treats interviewer preparation as a trainable capability. These are employer practice sources; internal figures are not universal causal estimates. ## Measure useful changes

Use a short optional candidate feedback form: Were expectations clear? Did you have a fair opportunity to demonstrate relevant capability? Were questions understandable? Were access arrangements effective? Could you ask the questions you needed? Were next steps communicated? Do not infer satisfaction from an offer acceptance or a smiling conversation.

For interviewers, track capability coverage, evidence gaps, neutral-probe quality, rater disagreement, burden, and timeliness. A standardized question list that yields no useful evidence needs revision. Candidate satisfaction and job-performance prediction are different outcomes and should not be conflated.

## Completion

Deliver the interview guide, pilot findings, changed questions/anchors, and unresolved decisions with an accountable owner. An agent can support consistent evidence review; it cannot validate the whole selection system by assertion or independently authorize an employment decision.
