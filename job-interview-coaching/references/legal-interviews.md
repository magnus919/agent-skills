# Legal, compliance, and professional advisory interviews

## Basis and task selection

[Georgetown public interview preparation sheet](https://www.law.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Preparing-For-Your-Interview.pdf) connects background evidence, employer research, practice, and relevant questions. It is career guidance, not a professional-conduct code. Obtain current jurisdiction and specialty standards for technical questions.

Clarify jurisdiction, practice area, employer/client setting, qualification stage, scope of supervision, and interview task. Distinguish trainee, associate/practitioner, in-house adviser, compliance specialist, and partner/leader. Writing samples, case discussions, motivation, and client scenarios need different preparation.

## Evidence to develop

Elicit analysis under incomplete facts, source verification, clear advice, confidentiality, managing deadlines, professional boundaries, and supervision. Distinguish an internally recommended position from advice actually accepted or implemented. Do not require privileged matter details to make an answer specific.

## Worked contrast

Original synthetic question: **Tell me about a recommendation you changed when new information emerged.**

Weak example: “I am very detail-oriented and always give clients the right answer.”

Stronger example using different, explicitly fictional facts: “I initially recommended one approach based on the information we had. A later document changed a key assumption. I checked the source and significance, raised it with the supervising adviser, and revised the options and risk explanation. I can explain the analysis and my contribution in anonymized terms; the final decision belonged to the authorized adviser and client.”

The stronger answer demonstrates evidence-responsive reasoning and role boundaries. It does not certify legal correctness or prove the client outcome. Ask what assumption changed and how the candidate verified it, while allowing redaction. Do not penalize inability to disclose privileged material as a lack of experience.

Use this contrast to explain evidence quality, not to put fictional achievements into the user's answer. For a real transcript, any improved autobiographical answer must use only supplied or subsequently confirmed facts.

## Neutral probes

- What changed the analysis, and what did not change?
- How did you explain uncertainty to the person deciding?
- What was your own drafting or research contribution?

## Candidate practice

Explain a redacted matter to a specialist and then a non-specialist client. Practise identifying one missing fact before giving advice. For a writing sample, discuss audience, source support, revisions, and authorship rather than offering a generic claim of excellent drafting. For ethical scenarios, obtain the relevant professional rules before grading.

Transfer task: Introduce a new fact, a different audience, or an approaching deadline. Ask which recommendation changes and which additional checks or authority are needed.

Keep the baseline, coached retry, and independent transfer attempt distinct. Ask one question at a time and wait. If the user wants a simulation, withhold coaching until the agreed debrief. If they want an explanation or plan, do not force a mock.

## Responsibility-level adaptations

Trainee: research, accuracy, asking for review. Practitioner: independent analysis within authority and client communication. Senior: ambiguous risk, supervising work, managing matters. Partner/leader: judgment across work, people, clients, and business responsibilities without treating revenue alone as quality.

A title alone is insufficient. Ask what independence, complexity, impact, and decision authority the role actually requires. Early-career candidates may use relevant education, voluntary, caring, or project evidence; experienced candidates should explain the scope and limits of their contribution.

## Interviewer practice and candidate experience

Give a bounded hypothetical with enough information and domain-reviewed criteria. Do not fish for privileged disclosures or ask candidates to solve live client matters for free. Separate legal knowledge from communication and commercial preferences. If rules are jurisdiction-specific, provide or permit relevant sources.

For interviewer coaching, rehearse the probes against both a vague and a concrete answer. Record what evidence the question elicited, what remains missing, and whether a hint supplied the response. A friendly conversation is not itself a complete assessment. Give candidates space to understand the work and ask informed questions.
