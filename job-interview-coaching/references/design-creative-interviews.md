# Design, product, content, and creative portfolio interviews

## Basis and task selection

[Atlassian design interview guide](https://www.atlassian.com/company/careers/resources/interviewing/design) separates craft, product thinking, collaboration, and IC/manager emphasis. [Oxford creative application guidance](https://www.ox.ac.uk/careers/careers-guidance/job-search-and-applications/writing-applications/creative-portfolios-cvs-and-applications) identifies varied work evidence. Employer mechanics are not universal.

Identify craft and actual requested format: design critique, product case, portfolio, writing exercise, showreel, audition, or creative presentation. Establish audience, time, work permissions, and individual versus team ownership. A polished product-design deck is not the right default for a writer, performer, or design-operations specialist.

## Evidence to develop

Elicit the brief, audience/user, constraints, original contribution, alternatives, decision rationale, feedback, iteration, and observed outcomes. Product questions add prioritization and evidence for a choice; content questions add audience, information needs, and effectiveness. Ask what was shipped or performed and what remained a proposal.

## Worked contrast

Original synthetic question: **Show a project where feedback changed your approach. What changed and what did you decide to keep?**

Weak example: “Users loved it, so the final design was a success. Our team did everything together.”

Stronger example using different, explicitly fictional facts: “I ran the usability sessions and summarized where participants could not find the return conditions. Another designer owned the visual system. I proposed moving those conditions earlier in the flow and tested a revised prototype. The project was cancelled before launch, so I can discuss the usability findings and the design decision, but not conversion impact.”

The stronger response establishes authorship, a concrete observation, a decision, and limits. It does not prove all users benefited or that the whole product was good. If the portfolio itself is being assessed, review the requested craft quality separately instead of treating presentation polish as evidence of every capability.

Use this contrast to explain evidence quality, not to put fictional achievements into the user's answer. For a real transcript, any improved autobiographical answer must use only supplied or subsequently confirmed facts.

## Neutral probes

- Which alternative did you reject and why?
- What did the feedback actually show rather than merely suggest?
- What part would your collaborator say was yours?

## Candidate practice

Choose two portfolio pieces for different capabilities. Practise a short overview and a deep dive on one contested decision. Answer interruption questions without restarting the entire presentation. For writing or performance, use a permitted short sample and explain choices for the intended audience.

Transfer task: Give a new constraint—different audience, access need, deadline, or channel—and ask how the design or creative decision would change.

Keep the baseline, coached retry, and independent transfer attempt distinct. Ask one question at a time and wait. If the user wants a simulation, withhold coaching until the agreed debrief. If they want an explanation or plan, do not force a mock.

## Responsibility-level adaptations

Entry: process, craft potential, feedback response. Independent: decisions and delivery. Senior IC: ambiguity, influence, cross-functional tradeoffs. Manager/creative director: setting direction and developing others; distinguish their leadership contribution from the team's craft execution.

A title alone is insufficient. Ask what independence, complexity, impact, and decision authority the role actually requires. Early-career candidates may use relevant education, voluntary, caring, or project evidence; experienced candidates should explain the scope and limits of their contribution.

## Interviewer practice and candidate experience

State the medium and time, ownership expectations, and whether unfinished or redacted work is acceptable. Ask comparable craft and reasoning questions. Do not ask for confidential source files or treat a cancelled project as proof of failure. For take-home tasks, bound effort and do not extract free production work.

For interviewer coaching, rehearse the probes against both a vague and a concrete answer. Record what evidence the question elicited, what remains missing, and whether a hint supplied the response. A friendly conversation is not itself a complete assessment. Give candidates space to understand the work and ask informed questions.
