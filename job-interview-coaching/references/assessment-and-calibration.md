# Assessment, anchors, and calibration

## Separate three judgments

1. **What evidence did this response provide?** Observable actions, reasoning, result, artifact, or proposed action.
2. **How does that evidence compare with this role's defined criterion?** Use task-specific anchors.
3. **What decision should the organization make?** An accountable human integrates appropriately obtained evidence, policy, and uncertainty.

A coaching score does not establish predictive validity. An LLM's confidence is not a probability that a candidate can do the job. Do not call an authored rubric a psychometric instrument.

## Build anchors from critical incidents

Specify the capability, context, responsibility level, and observable differences before reviewing candidates. Example authored practice rubric for prioritizing customer work:

| State | Example evidence |
|---|---|
| Not demonstrated | No relevant decision or rationale available; seek evidence |
| Limited | Names a priority but gives little basis or misses a stated constraint |
| Adequate for this task | Uses relevant urgency and impact information, explains action and escalation within authority |
| Strong for this task | Compares competing needs, checks assumptions, communicates tradeoffs, and explains how to monitor and revise |

These anchors are illustrative, not universal. For a domain task, involve a qualified subject-matter reviewer. Do not average away a safety-critical failure using unrelated strengths. Equally, do not turn missing evidence into an adverse safety judgment.

## Evidence ledger

Use one row per candidate per criterion: response location, evidence, rating or evidence state, rationale, uncertainties, and follow-up. Keep candidate identities minimal. Do not add new criteria after seeing who seems impressive. If criteria legitimately change, document the change and reassess all relevant candidates consistently.

Require independent initial ratings before discussion. In calibration, compare the response and anchor that caused disagreement; do not merely negotiate a mean score. Track whether a finding arose spontaneously, after a neutral probe, after a leading prompt, or in a work sample.

## Comparable evidence

For cross-candidate comparison use the same role criteria and explicitly report materially different questions, time, assistance, accommodations, or missing rounds. Accommodation is not itself a negative signal. If evidence is insufficient, say the comparison is incomplete and propose a proportionate next step. Do not produce a confident ranking from unequal excerpts.

Replace “culture fit” with observable job-related behaviors and working conditions. Do not score accent, appearance, inferred age, disability, family circumstances, or other protected/personal characteristics. Review actual policy and current jurisdiction-specific guidance when necessary.

## Evaluate the interview system

Look at coverage of role capabilities, rater disagreement, candidate understanding, accommodation effectiveness, process burden, and which questions yield useful evidence. Hiring-outcome and job-performance validation require an appropriate study, privacy controls, and expert interpretation; a few good anecdotes do not validate a selection method.

Sources: OPM structured interviewing; Canada's interview guide; AAMC structured residency design; CIPD selection methods. Their scope does not establish this agent's validity.
