# Business, leadership, and public-service interviews

These are coaching adaptations, informed by official consulting and sales materials, UK Civil Service criteria, and structured-interview guidance. Employer frameworks are overlays, not universal selection rules.

## Consulting and business cases

Clarify the decision, objective, constraints, and available evidence. Build a case-specific structure, state assumptions, analyze quantities with units, check plausibility, and synthesize a recommendation with risks and the next evidence needed. Practise responding when new information changes the hypothesis.

Prompt: “A service has more customers but lower profit. What would you investigate?” Do not prescribe a single framework or invent missing data as fact. Provide synthetic figures explicitly during practice, and verify arithmetic before giving feedback.

Separate case reasoning from personal-experience questions. McKinsey's public materials describe both, but the invitation determines which formats apply to a particular role.

## Sales, account management, and fundraising

Elicit customer understanding, discovery, ethical persuasion, qualification, objections, coordination, follow-through, and learning from losses. Presentations and role plays may be relevant; Salesforce provides an employer example. Do not treat aggressive closing as universally desirable.

Prompt: “A prospective customer requests a feature you cannot promise. How would you handle the conversation?” Score listening, clarity, truthful boundaries, and next steps. For experience claims, clarify quota, territory, time period, team contribution, and data limits rather than rewarding a large revenue number alone.

Candidates should assess product suitability, targets, support, territory, and how success is measured. Do not give compensation or contract advice without appropriate current sources.

## Finance, operations, and professional services

Use the actual specialty to select relevant technical tasks: explain a forecast, identify an accounting assumption, assess risk, prioritize a control issue, or communicate an uncertain recommendation. General coaching can test reasoning and explanation; technical grading needs valid source material and domain review.

Prompt: “An important forecast assumption no longer holds. How would you assess and communicate its effect?” Do not infer financial competence from confident terminology or fabricate regulatory requirements.

## People management and executive leadership

Distinguish individual execution from enabling others. Probe hiring and development, delegation, feedback, resource allocation, priorities, conflict, and accountability. At executive level add mandate, governance, organizational risk, and external stakeholders.

Prompt: “Describe a resource decision that improved one outcome while making another harder. What did you own, who disagreed, and how did you review the result?” Avoid rewarding prestige and scale without judgment. Probe causal attribution and choices under the information available at the time.

Candidates should test mandate, authority, resources, sponsor alignment, and measures of success. A leadership interview is a mutual diligence conversation, not a charisma contest.

## Public service and mission-led work

Use the advertised selection framework and level. UK Civil Service Success Profiles distinguish several assessment elements and grade-specific behaviors; do not assume every public employer uses that system. A behavior example is not automatically the right response to a strengths or technical question.

Practise evidence of service delivery, stewardship, sound decisions, collaboration, and communicating tradeoffs as relevant to the actual role. Do not equate agreement with an organization's mission with proven ability to execute it.

Interviewers should keep criteria visible and distinct from political affinity, social familiarity, or vague mission fit. Use job-related questions and appropriate current policy guidance.
