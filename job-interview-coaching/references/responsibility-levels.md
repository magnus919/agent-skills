# Adapt to responsibility, not titles

Establish actual scope: independence, complexity, consequences, time horizon, influence, and accountability. Titles and years served are imperfect proxies. Senior individual contributors and people managers need different evidence; an apprentice should not be judged against executive expectations.

| Level / path | Evidence to elicit | Useful probe |
|---|---|---|
| First job / trainee | Learning, reliability, basic task understanding, asking for help | “Tell me about learning something unfamiliar. What did you do when you got stuck?” |
| Apprentice / supervised practitioner | Safe boundaries, using feedback, following and understanding procedure | “What would make you stop and ask your supervisor?” |
| Independent practitioner | Completing work, checking quality, handling routine exceptions | “How did you know the work was ready to hand over?” |
| Senior specialist | Complex judgment, alternatives, mentoring, influence without authority | “Which tradeoff did others disagree with, and how did you evaluate it?” |
| Team lead / manager | Delegation, workload, feedback, development, team outcomes | “What did you delegate and how did you check without taking the work back?” |
| Director / multi-team leader | Cross-team priorities, resource allocation, operating systems, succession | “Which team-level objective did you change for the wider outcome?” |
| Executive | Enterprise mandate, governance, risk, capital/people choices, external stakeholders | “What decision did you take to the board, and what authority did you retain?” |

These are authored coaching heuristics. A small organization's executive may remain hands-on; a public-service grade has its own defined framework. Use published employer criteria over this matrix. UK Civil Service behaviors provide a concrete example of grade-specific expectations; do not export those grades universally.

## Transitions

Career changers: distinguish transferable capability from unproven domain knowledge. Ask what a real example demonstrates and what needs additional learning. Do not relabel a personal project as professional tenure.

Returners: explore current readiness and relevant prior evidence without demanding private reasons for a gap. Offer concise boundary-respecting wording and practice.

Internal candidates: assess the target role rather than familiarity or current reputation. Management candidates need evidence about enabling others, not only doing more work themselves. Senior IC candidates need technical depth and influence; do not force headcount ownership into their answers.

Executive candidates: test contribution and causal claims, including counterfactuals and setbacks. Do not reward scale metrics without understanding decision authority and external conditions. Interviewers should clarify mandate and resources so they assess the actual job.
