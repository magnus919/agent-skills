# Adapt the skill to an unfamiliar profession

Read when no focused playbook fits, when a title is ambiguous, or when the task crosses fields. The method below is an authored adaptation procedure. It should produce useful coaching without pretending the agent already knows a specialty's technical standards.

## Establish the work before the question bank

Use the actual job description, invitation, work sample, and responsibility first. Extract concrete tasks, outputs, decisions, constraints, stakeholders, and supervision. Separate essential entry capabilities from post-hire training. If materials are missing, ask for the one piece of context that changes the next exercise; a reasonable provisional practice question can still proceed.

Supplement with an occupation-specific public professional or employer source when needed. O*NET is a useful starting taxonomy for many US occupations, but a generic occupational record is not the employer's rubric or a global licensure standard. Check local terms, standards, and interview instructions before making technical or regulatory judgments.

## Map tasks to observable evidence

| Task family | Evidence to elicit | Suitable interview activity |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnose or troubleshoot | Observations, hypotheses, discriminating checks, result verification | Fault scenario or retrospective investigation |
| Make or perform | Craft choices, quality criteria, execution, iteration | Portfolio, audition, bounded demonstration, work history |
| Care, teach, or advise | Understanding needs, communication, scope, adaptation, follow-through | Role play, teaching demonstration, de-identified example |
| Coordinate or operate | Dependencies, priorities, accuracy, exceptions, handoff | Scheduling/inbox scenario or incident example |
| Analyze or recommend | Decision, assumptions, sources, calculation, uncertainty, recommendation | Case, data task, briefing, research discussion |
| Lead or govern | Authority, tradeoffs, enabling others, accountability, review | Decision deep dive or mandate discussion |

Many occupations combine several families. A musician may need an audition, collaboration examples, and preparation for an artistic discussion. An agricultural supervisor may need practical knowledge, operational decisions, and people leadership. These examples identify plausible evidence families, not researched universal hiring formats for those professions.

## Build a small provisional coaching plan

For each important capability, define a task and observable evidence at the actual level of responsibility. Create one realistic question with enough context to answer, neutral probes, and an appropriate exercise. Label the criteria as inferred until the actual employer or domain owner confirms them.

A useful generic starting question is “Describe a time a result did not meet the expected standard. How did you identify the issue, decide what to do within your role, and check what happened next?” Replace “result” and “standard” with the real work once known. Do not substitute this question for an audition or practical test that the invitation actually requires.

## Keep technical correctness and coaching quality separate

The coach can assess whether a candidate explains a decision, supplies personal evidence, acknowledges uncertainty, or responds to a question. Correctness of a species-specific treatment, machine setting, statutory interpretation, musical technique, or specialized calculation may require a qualified reviewer or authoritative artifact. Mark it as not assessed rather than infer correctness from fluent language.

For interviewer use, have the relevant domain owner review scenario realism, entry expectations, acceptable alternatives, practical safety, and anchors. A verbal discussion cannot certify an unobserved practical skill. Do not make the assessment harder merely because the agent lacks domain knowledge.

## Test the adaptation

Run a baseline and a changed-constraint question. Ask whether the feedback concerned the actual work or could have been copied unchanged to any profession. If it remains generic, obtain a representative task or example before adding more advice. Preserve role-specific constraints and verify that the proposed exercise assesses the intended capability.

Completion is a role-grounded preparation or interview plan with explicit evidence needs and specialist limits. It is not a claim to have mastered the profession or its selection system.
