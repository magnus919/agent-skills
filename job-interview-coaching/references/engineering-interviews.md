# Physical engineering and laboratory technical interviews

## Basis and task selection

[O*NET mechanical-engineer record](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/17-2141.00) identifies design, installation, operation, and maintenance tasks. It supports occupational context; the coaching scenarios and anchors below are authored adaptations, not a licensed engineering standard.

Establish discipline, product/process, regulated setting, responsibility, and whether the format is calculations, technical presentation, design review, laboratory demonstration, or an experience interview. Ask what standards and review authority apply before judging specialist correctness.

## Evidence to develop

Probe requirements, assumptions, models, tolerances, uncertainty, test design, failure analysis, documentation, and design changes. For laboratory roles, distinguish following a protocol from creating and validating one. For field engineering, include configuration and handoff conditions. Interview explanation cannot certify a physical design or safe laboratory technique.

## Worked contrast

Original synthetic question: **A prototype test disagreed with your prediction. How did you decide what to investigate and what to change?**

Weak example: “The simulation was wrong, so I changed the design and it worked.”

Stronger example using different, explicitly fictional facts: “I checked whether the test setup matched the model conditions, reviewed measurement repeatability, and compared the discrepancy with the assumptions most likely to matter. The test engineer and I isolated a boundary-condition difference. I revised the model and proposed a design change for review. The next test met the stated acceptance criterion under those conditions; it did not establish performance in every environment.”

The stronger fictional response exposes checks, collaboration, authority, and scope of the result. It is not a template for claiming a fault that did not occur. Ask for actual measured quantities and applicable acceptance criteria when they exist; do not invent a tolerance or approve the design from prose.

Use this contrast to explain evidence quality, not to put fictional achievements into the user's answer. For a real transcript, any improved autobiographical answer must use only supplied or subsequently confirmed facts.

## Neutral probes

- What did you rule out before changing the design?
- Who approved the change and what was your responsibility?
- Which test condition remains outside the evidence?

## Candidate practice

Practise explaining one drawing, experiment, or failure investigation with no confidential identifiers. Make an assumption list and ask which assumption deserves a test. Rehearse a short technical explanation, then a cross-functional version with the same substantive limits. Use a domain-checked task for technical scoring.

Transfer task: Change an operating condition or measurement uncertainty and ask which conclusion still holds. A good answer can be “we need another test,” with a reason and a specific proposed check.

Keep the baseline, coached retry, and independent transfer attempt distinct. Ask one question at a time and wait. If the user wants a simulation, withhold coaching until the agreed debrief. If they want an explanation or plan, do not force a mock.

## Responsibility-level adaptations

Graduate/trainee: reasoning, method, and knowing review boundaries. Practitioner: design or test execution and verification. Senior specialist: uncertain requirements and cross-system tradeoffs. Engineering manager: review systems, resources, development, and accountability, distinct from personally performing each calculation.

A title alone is insufficient. Ask what independence, complexity, impact, and decision authority the role actually requires. Early-career candidates may use relevant education, voluntary, caring, or project evidence; experienced candidates should explain the scope and limits of their contribution.

## Interviewer practice and candidate experience

Publish relevant task conditions and provide necessary standards or allow reference material as appropriate. Have a qualified specialist establish the answer space. A live demonstration needs suitable supervision and safety arrangements; do not improvise hazardous tasks. Evaluate recognition of limits rather than rewarding unsafe certainty.

For interviewer coaching, rehearse the probes against both a vague and a concrete answer. Record what evidence the question elicited, what remains missing, and whether a hint supplied the response. A friendly conversation is not itself a complete assessment. Give candidates space to understand the work and ask informed questions.
