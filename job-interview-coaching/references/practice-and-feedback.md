# Mock interviews, feedback, and transfer

## Select the practice contract

| Mode | Agent behavior | Feedback timing |
|---|---|---|
| Coached practice | Ask one question; wait; probe as needed | After each answer, followed by a retry |
| Simulation | Conduct a realistic bounded round in interviewer role | After the round unless the user pauses |
| Micro-drill | Isolate one weakness and repeat with variation | Immediately after each attempt |

Establish a role and format with minimal intake. Default to a short coached round when the user has no preference. State the assumption and start. Do not present an answer before the user attempts the question. A user requesting a worked example is a different task.

## Run the loop

1. Choose one capability and task-specific evidence criteria.
2. Ask one realistic question and wait.
3. Probe neutrally to resolve missing evidence; do not leak the ideal answer.
4. Invite a brief self-assessment if it helps learning.
5. Show a specific strength and the most consequential improvement with evidence from the answer.
6. Give a targeted drill or an outline, then let the user retry.
7. Vary the question to test transfer, recording how much help was given.

Do not conclude mastery from a response the agent wrote. Separate an unassisted baseline, a coached retry, and an independent transfer attempt. They answer different questions about readiness.

## Useful micro-drills

- **Answer first:** give a direct opening before context; then add only the context needed.
- **Ownership:** explain one personal decision and one collaborator's contribution accurately.
- **Reasoning:** compare two plausible options under a real constraint.
- **Evidence:** replace an unsupported adjective with an observable action or result.
- **Recovery:** acknowledge a misunderstanding, clarify, and restart without a long apology.
- **Technical verification:** identify an edge case or failure mode and test the proposed solution.
- **Case synthesis:** give the recommendation, supporting evidence, major uncertainty, and next check.
- **Interviewer probe:** convert a leading question into a neutral evidence request.

Timing is a training parameter, not a universal interview law. Adjust to format and access needs. Progress can mean clearer reasoning or less assistance, not just shorter answers.

## Difficulty and emotional support

Increase ambiguity, competing constraints, depth, or audience sophistication as appropriate to the role. Do not simulate hostility as a default. If a user freezes, offer thinking time, an outline, a smaller question, or a pause; do not diagnose or demand a particular personality. Critical feedback should be usable and proportionate.

## Close and measure

At the agreed time or question count, stop. Summarize actual improvements, remaining evidence gaps, and the next two practice actions. Use a practice log to preserve questions, response evidence, support provided, and transfer results. Do not promise an offer or invent numeric readiness probabilities.

Evidence: ICF supports reflective coaching; Conversate explores interactive simulation and dialogic feedback. Those sources motivate the design but do not validate this agent's effectiveness. Interview-performance gains and real-job or hiring outcomes must be measured separately.
