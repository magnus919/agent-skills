# Public-service, civil-service, and mission-led interviews

## Basis and task selection

[UK Civil Service behaviors](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/success-profiles/success-profiles-civil-service-behaviours) supplies grade-specific expectations within Success Profiles. It does not describe every public-service or nonprofit interview. Use the advertised selection elements and current organization instructions.

Identify country/system, grade or actual responsibility, role, advertised behaviors/strengths/technical/experience elements, and assessment format. For nonprofit or mission-led roles, establish service users, funding/accountability, and constraints. Do not substitute political agreement or personal mission language for job evidence.

## Evidence to develop

Elicit stewardship, sound decisions, service delivery, collaboration, communication, integrity, and change where relevant. A strengths question, behavioral example, technical question, and policy briefing are different tasks. Match evidence to the level and the actual assessed element rather than force STAR onto everything.

## Worked contrast

Original synthetic question: **How did you make a recommendation when stakeholder priorities conflicted and evidence was incomplete?**

Weak example: “I support the organization's mission, so I knew the right answer.”

Stronger example using different, explicitly fictional facts: “I clarified the decision and the affected groups, separated established facts from assumptions, and compared the options against the agreed objective and constraints. I explained the uncertainty and consequences to the decision owner and identified what we should review after implementation. I would describe my own analytical role without claiming I held the final authority.”

The stronger answer explains a decision process and accountability. It does not establish that every stakeholder was treated well or that the policy succeeded. Ask for the actual evidence, alternatives, and observed result. If the task is a briefing, test the briefing itself rather than merely a past story about briefing.

Use this contrast to explain evidence quality, not to put fictional achievements into the user's answer. For a real transcript, any improved autobiographical answer must use only supplied or subsequently confirmed facts.

## Neutral probes

- What evidence did you have, and what was still uncertain?
- Which affected perspective changed your analysis?
- Who decided, and how did you know whether implementation worked?

## Candidate practice

Use an advertised criterion to select a truthful example, then explain why that example demonstrates the specified level. Practise a short synthetic briefing with recommendation, rationale, uncertainty, and next check. Vary the audience or constraints without importing a different jurisdiction's rules.

Transfer task: Change the evidence quality or reveal a new affected group. Ask what changes in the recommendation, the uncertainty statement, and the review plan.

Keep the baseline, coached retry, and independent transfer attempt distinct. Ask one question at a time and wait. If the user wants a simulation, withhold coaching until the agreed debrief. If they want an explanation or plan, do not force a mock.

## Responsibility-level adaptations

Entry/admin: accurate service and learning. Independent practitioner: reliable judgment within authority. Senior specialist: complex analysis and influence. Manager/director: resources, cross-boundary delivery, and accountability. Use published local grade expectations over generic title mappings.

A title alone is insufficient. Ask what independence, complexity, impact, and decision authority the role actually requires. Early-career candidates may use relevant education, voluntary, caring, or project evidence; experienced candidates should explain the scope and limits of their contribution.

## Interviewer practice and candidate experience

Keep the advertised criteria distinct, allow comparable opportunity to demonstrate them, and document evidence. Do not use political affinity, social similarity, or vague mission fit as proxies. Explain the process and next steps, use appropriate access arrangements, and distinguish genuine entry requirements from trainable skills.

For interviewer coaching, rehearse the probes against both a vague and a concrete answer. Record what evidence the question elicited, what remains missing, and whether a hint supplied the response. A friendly conversation is not itself a complete assessment. Give candidates space to understand the work and ask informed questions.
