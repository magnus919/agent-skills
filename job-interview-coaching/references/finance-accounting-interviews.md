# Finance, accounting, audit, and risk interviews

## Basis and task selection

[O*NET accountants and auditors](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/13-2011.00) provides task context for records, statements, analysis, and advice. Use current specialty standards and actual role tasks for technical correctness. General bank career pages do not establish a universal technical question set.

Distinguish accounting, audit, corporate finance, banking, investment research, financial operations, and risk. Establish analyst versus licensed/experienced responsibility, jurisdiction, tools, work sample, and expected technical framework. Do not coach every finance role as an investment-banking interview.

## Evidence to develop

Elicit assumptions, reconciliation, calculations, controls, evidence quality, communication, and review authority. Separate a model's output from the decision it informs. For audit/risk, test how the candidate investigates exceptions and distinguishes missing support from established error. For regulated advice, obtain the relevant current source and qualified reviewer.

## Worked contrast

Original synthetic question: **Your forecast changes substantially when one assumption is updated. How would you explain this to the person deciding?**

Weak example: “The spreadsheet says we will miss the target, so we need cuts.”

Stronger example using different, explicitly fictional facts: “I would identify the changed assumption, verify the input and calculation, and show its effect relative to the previous forecast. I would distinguish the forecast from the target and explain the range of plausible outcomes and controllable choices. I would flag what needs review before treating the revised result as a basis for action.”

The stronger explanation identifies verification and decision context instead of treating a spreadsheet as authority. It does not establish the accounting treatment or model is correct. Ask for the actual model or a synthetic task and evaluate calculations separately. Do not invent regulatory thresholds or guarantee a financial result.

Use this contrast to explain evidence quality, not to put fictional achievements into the user's answer. For a real transcript, any improved autobiographical answer must use only supplied or subsequently confirmed facts.

## Neutral probes

- What check could reveal a modeling or input error?
- Which assumption has the largest decision consequence?
- Who must review the recommendation and what uncertainty remains?

## Candidate practice

Use a small synthetic reconciliation or sensitivity table. Ask the candidate to explain both the arithmetic and the implication. Rehearse a discrepancy story with detection, investigation, escalation, correction, and verification. Then change an assumption to test whether the explanation updates coherently.

Transfer task: Change whether a discrepancy is a timing issue, an unsupported estimate, or a confirmed error. Ask what evidence and action differ without inventing a professional standard.

Keep the baseline, coached retry, and independent transfer attempt distinct. Ask one question at a time and wait. If the user wants a simulation, withhold coaching until the agreed debrief. If they want an explanation or plan, do not force a mock.

## Responsibility-level adaptations

Entry: accurate method and knowing review boundaries. Independent: reliable analysis and clear advice. Senior specialist: complex uncertainty, controls, and challenge. Manager/leader: resource allocation, risk governance, quality of team analysis, and accountability for decisions.

A title alone is insufficient. Ask what independence, complexity, impact, and decision authority the role actually requires. Early-career candidates may use relevant education, voluntary, caring, or project evidence; experienced candidates should explain the scope and limits of their contribution.

## Interviewer practice and candidate experience

Choose a task relevant to the specialty and specify permitted tools. Separate technical accuracy, reasoning, checks, and communication. Use synthetic financial data. Agree answer criteria with a domain owner; do not mark a confident conclusion as correct without checking the applicable method and assumptions.

For interviewer coaching, rehearse the probes against both a vague and a concrete answer. Record what evidence the question elicited, what remains missing, and whether a hint supplied the response. A friendly conversation is not itself a complete assessment. Give candidates space to understand the work and ask informed questions.
