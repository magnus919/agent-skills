# Teaching, training, and education interviews

## Basis and task selection

[Department for Education interview-lesson guide](https://teaching-vacancies.service.gov.uk/jobseeker-guides/get-help-applying-for-your-teaching-role/prepare-for-a-teaching-job-interview-lesson) addresses lesson planning, learner needs, equipment, checks for understanding, and adaptation. The scenarios below are original; safeguarding rules require current local sources.

Establish learner age, subject, institution, qualification level, job versus teacher-training admission, and whether the candidate teaches learners or talks a panel through a plan. Obtain topic, duration, prior knowledge, access needs, available resources, and whether a lesson plan must be supplied.

## Evidence to develop

Prepare a clear learning objective, explanation, activity, checks, adjustment, and reflection. For experience questions, elicit what the teacher noticed and changed, not merely whether a lesson was enjoyable. For leadership, examine curriculum and staff development rather than treating excellent personal teaching as sufficient evidence of leading a department.

## Worked contrast

Original synthetic question: **How would you know whether learners understood the idea during your interview lesson?**

Weak example: “I would ask if everyone understood. They would enjoy the activity.”

Stronger example using different, explicitly fictional facts: “I would ask each learner to apply the idea to a short new example so I could see their reasoning. If several answers showed the same misconception, I would revisit that step with a different example before moving on. I would finish with a brief check tied to the learning objective rather than assume participation meant understanding.”

The stronger explanation connects assessment to an instructional decision. It does not prove the actual lesson succeeded. If reviewing a real demonstration, distinguish planned checks from observed learner responses. An alternative valid checking method is acceptable; do not require this exact activity.

Use this contrast to explain evidence quality, not to put fictional achievements into the user's answer. For a real transcript, any improved autobiographical answer must use only supplied or subsequently confirmed facts.

## Neutral probes

- What misconception are you checking for?
- What would you change if the check showed mixed understanding?
- Which learners or needs does your plan currently overlook?

## Candidate practice

Rehearse the opening explanation, a learner misunderstanding, and the reflection separately. Then run the short lesson with an interruption or equipment failure. Reflect on observed evidence, one adjustment, and its expected purpose. For sensitive concerns, practise identifying the relevant procedure and professional boundary without inventing local safeguarding advice.

Transfer task: Change the learner prior knowledge, response pattern, or available equipment and ask the candidate to adapt the plan while retaining the learning goal.

Keep the baseline, coached retry, and independent transfer attempt distinct. Ask one question at a time and wait. If the user wants a simulation, withhold coaching until the agreed debrief. If they want an explanation or plan, do not force a mock.

## Responsibility-level adaptations

Training applicant: teachability, subject understanding, reflective potential. Newly qualified: planning and supported classroom decisions. Experienced educator: adaptation and learning evidence. Leader: curriculum, staff development, inclusion, resources, and evaluation of improvement.

A title alone is insufficient. Ask what independence, complexity, impact, and decision authority the role actually requires. Early-career candidates may use relevant education, voluntary, caring, or project evidence; experienced candidates should explain the scope and limits of their contribution.

## Interviewer practice and candidate experience

Supply a usable brief and accessible conditions. Decide how much prior knowledge candidates can assume and how questions affect time. Use subject-qualified review for correctness. Do not confuse a polished slide deck with effective instruction or assess an applicant against undisclosed school-specific routines.

For interviewer coaching, rehearse the probes against both a vague and a concrete answer. Record what evidence the question elicited, what remains missing, and whether a hint supplied the response. A friendly conversation is not itself a complete assessment. Give candidates space to understand the work and ask informed questions.
