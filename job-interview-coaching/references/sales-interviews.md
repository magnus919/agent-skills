# Sales, account management, customer success, and fundraising interviews

## Basis and task selection

[Salesforce public interview training](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/strategies-for-successful-sales-team-interviews/shine-in-your-sales-interviews) describes consultative capability and a presentation in that employer's process. Do not export its presentation or style conventions universally.

Establish prospecting versus closing versus account growth/customer success, customer segment, sales cycle, authority, and whether the task is discovery, presentation, objection handling, or experience discussion. For fundraising, clarify mission, donor relationship, and ethical constraints rather than treating donors as identical to commercial buyers.

## Evidence to develop

Elicit customer understanding, qualification, listening, truthful positioning, handling objections, coordination, follow-through, and learning from loss. When discussing results, clarify territory, quota, timeframe, inherited accounts, team contribution, and attribution. Revenue alone does not describe the quality or ethics of the work.

## Worked contrast

Original synthetic question: **A prospect says they will buy if you promise a feature that is not committed. How would you respond?**

Weak example: “I would promise it, close the deal, and persuade the product team later.”

Stronger example using different, explicitly fictional facts: “I would explore the need behind the requested feature and explain accurately what is available and what is not committed. I would involve the right product or technical colleague to clarify feasible alternatives, agree a next step, and avoid a promise outside my authority. If we cannot meet a critical requirement, that affects whether this is a suitable opportunity.”

The stronger answer connects discovery, credibility, qualification, and internal coordination. It does not prove a particular sales method or imply every uncommitted request requires rejection. Ask how the candidate would validate the underlying need and manage the relationship without misrepresentation.

Use this contrast to explain evidence quality, not to put fictional achievements into the user's answer. For a real transcript, any improved autobiographical answer must use only supplied or subsequently confirmed facts.

## Neutral probes

- What problem is the customer trying to solve?
- How would you check whether an alternative meets the requirement?
- What would you record and communicate after the call?

## Candidate practice

Run a discovery role-play where the user must ask before pitching. Then introduce an objection and require a clear truthful response. For past achievements, reconstruct the denominator, baseline, personal action, team help, and context. Practise a loss review that identifies learning without blaming the customer.

Transfer task: Reveal that the stated feature is only one possible solution to the underlying need. Ask the candidate to revise the discovery and proposal rather than repeat the same pitch.

Keep the baseline, coached retry, and independent transfer attempt distinct. Ask one question at a time and wait. If the user wants a simulation, withhold coaching until the agreed debrief. If they want an explanation or plan, do not force a mock.

## Responsibility-level adaptations

Entry: curiosity, organization, persistence within boundaries. Independent: credible discovery and follow-through. Senior account role: complex stakeholders, long-cycle judgment, account strategy. Manager: developing sellers, forecast integrity, resource choices, and a healthy performance system.

A title alone is insufficient. Ask what independence, complexity, impact, and decision authority the role actually requires. Early-career candidates may use relevant education, voluntary, caring, or project evidence; experienced candidates should explain the scope and limits of their contribution.

## Interviewer practice and candidate experience

Use realistic customer information and tell candidates what product knowledge can be assumed. Do not reward hidden feature claims or aggressive closing that conflicts with the role. Score listening, diagnosis, relevance, accuracy, and next steps separately from charisma. Explain presentation preparation burden.

For interviewer coaching, rehearse the probes against both a vague and a concrete answer. Record what evidence the question elicited, what remains missing, and whether a hint supplied the response. A friendly conversation is not itself a complete assessment. Give candidates space to understand the work and ask informed questions.
