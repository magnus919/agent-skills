# Logistics, operations, administration, and coordination interviews

## Basis and task selection

[O*NET logisticians](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/13-1081.00) and [administrative assistants](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-6014.00) establish different task contexts. The mock tasks below are original adaptations; they do not establish transport rules or executive-assistant expectations.

Clarify whether the role coordinates shipments, inventory, calendars, records, projects, or operational services. Obtain authority, systems, deadlines, confidential-data boundaries, and the requested work sample. A warehouse operator and a supply-chain analyst should not receive the same technical test merely because both work in logistics.

## Evidence to develop

Elicit accuracy, dependency management, prioritization, verification, communication, exceptions, and handoffs. Useful samples include a synthetic inbox, conflicting calendar requests, a shipment discrepancy, or a scheduling dependency. Specify what can be assumed and when the candidate can ask for information.

## Worked contrast

Original synthetic question: **Two people each say their request is urgent, but you cannot finish both by the requested time. How would you handle it?**

Weak example: “I would multitask and work harder so both are done.”

Stronger example using different, explicitly fictional facts: “I would establish the actual deadlines, consequences, dependencies, and available alternatives. If the priority was outside my authority, I would bring a clear choice and its consequences to the person who owns that decision. I would communicate the agreed plan to both requesters and track the remaining commitment rather than silently miss one.”

The stronger answer turns an urgency claim into a decision and a communication plan. It should not be interpreted as requiring escalation for every small task. Ask what the role is authorized to decide and how the candidate would act within that authority. A very simple task may need a simpler response.

Use this contrast to explain evidence quality, not to put fictional achievements into the user's answer. For a real transcript, any improved autobiographical answer must use only supplied or subsequently confirmed facts.

## Neutral probes

- What fact could change the priority?
- Which dependency makes a delay consequential?
- What would you record so the next person can follow through?

## Candidate practice

Give three fictional requests with deadlines and constraints; ask for a priority and rationale before revealing one new dependency. Rehearse a concise update communicating a delay and alternatives. For a real experience story, separate own coordination from the team's eventual delivery.

Transfer task: Reveal a delayed input or an incorrect record. Ask the candidate to revise the plan and tell affected people what changed, rather than defend the original priority.

Keep the baseline, coached retry, and independent transfer attempt distinct. Ask one question at a time and wait. If the user wants a simulation, withhold coaching until the agreed debrief. If they want an explanation or plan, do not force a mock.

## Responsibility-level adaptations

Entry: accuracy, following agreed procedures, asking for clarification. Independent: routine priorities and exception handling. Senior coordinator/analyst: cross-team dependencies and process improvements. Manager: capacity, systemic tradeoffs, escalation design, and whether changes improved reliability.

A title alone is insufficient. Ask what independence, complexity, impact, and decision authority the role actually requires. Early-career candidates may use relevant education, voluntary, caring, or project evidence; experienced candidates should explain the scope and limits of their contribution.

## Interviewer practice and candidate experience

Provide comparable information, clarify permitted tools, and score decision quality separately from typing speed or presentation style. Use fictional private data. Avoid an unpaid task based on a live operational problem with commercial value. For regulated transport or safety questions, obtain specialist criteria.

For interviewer coaching, rehearse the probes against both a vague and a concrete answer. Record what evidence the question elicited, what remains missing, and whether a hint supplied the response. A friendly conversation is not itself a complete assessment. Give candidates space to understand the work and ask informed questions.
