# Interviewer coaching and candidate experience

## Design from the work

Start with role outcomes, critical incidents, and what a person must know on entry versus can learn after joining. Select a feasible set of capabilities to assess in the available time. Consult job holders and relevant domain experts when creating specialist criteria. Do not infer an employer's hidden rubric from a job title.

For each capability define evidence, question or work sample, permitted probes, and rating anchors. Choose behavioral questions for actual experience, situational questions for proposed action, and demonstrations when verbal claims are insufficient. A structured interview is more than a fixed question list: evaluation and administration need structure too. OPM and Canada's guide are practical sources; AAMC provides a professional adaptation.

## Build a usable plan

For a proposed 40-minute round, an adjustable example is 4 minutes for opening, 24 for three capability discussions, 8 for candidate questions, and 4 for closure. Check feasibility; do not cram ten competencies into thirty minutes. Across a loop, assign ownership so coverage is deliberate and repetition purposeful.

Give candidates the format, duration, tools, expected preparation, permitted AI or other assistance, accessibility contact, and next steps. Do not claim a right to private records merely because a candidate is applying. Bound work samples, explain their use, and avoid extracting free production work.

## Ask and listen

| Weak prompt | Better approach | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| “You'd escalate immediately, right?” | “What would you do next, and why?” | Does not supply the desired action |
| “Are you a team player?” | “Describe a disagreement over how to complete shared work.” | Invites observable evidence |
| “Tell me about leadership, conflict, delivery, and failure.” | Ask one capability question, then neutral probes | Reduces ambiguity |
| “Would you fit our fast-paced culture?” | Ask about a specific workload or priority tradeoff | Replaces vague fit with work evidence |

Use common core questions and documented probe rules. Neutral clarification can vary because answers vary, but do not coach only some candidates toward missing criteria. Accommodations may change administration; preserve the intended job capability rather than rigidly insisting on identical mechanics.

Keep rapport separate from scored evidence. Avoid premature evaluative remarks, excessive interruptions, talking most of the time, or treating uncertainty as disqualification. For safety-critical answers, distinguish a proposed action, its rationale, and the applicable standard before grading correctness.

## Review your own technique

Use a transcript to examine whether questions were job-related, comprehensible, leading, compound, repetitive, or beyond the candidate's authority. Look for missed probes, unequal opportunities, premature judgments, and poor closure. Report where the interviewer failed to elicit needed evidence.

Candidate experience includes accurate role information and predictable follow-through. Explain next steps honestly. Do not make promises about timelines, offers, flexibility, or conditions that the interviewer cannot authorize.

## Debrief

Record individual evidence and ratings before group discussion. Compare with anchors, examine disagreement, and request proportionate additional evidence when material. Do not allow a senior panelist's impression to replace the record. Use the assessment reference for calibration and comparison limits.

Completion: deliver an interview plan, revised questions, or an evidence-linked interviewer critique. External scheduling, ATS updates, recording, and feedback delivery require separate authorization and the mutation gate in the parent skill.
