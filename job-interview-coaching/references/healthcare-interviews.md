# Healthcare, nursing, allied health, and care interviews

## Basis and task selection

[AAMC structured residency design](https://www.aamc.org/services/admission-interview-foundations/developing-structured-residency-interviews) provides one professional application of structured interviewing. [Mersey Care tips](https://www.merseycare.nhs.uk/interview-tips) describe that employer's values-based interviews. [RCN public overview](https://www.rcn.org.uk/library/sitecore/content/RCN/Home/Professional-Development/Your-career/Interviews) identifies preparation and coaching; detailed member content was not accessed.

Establish profession, jurisdiction, care setting, qualification/supervision level, advertised person specification, and interview format. A care assistant, newly qualified nurse, experienced specialist, and clinical director have different authority. MMI is only appropriate when the actual selection format uses stations.

## Evidence to develop

Probe communication, recognition of concern, prioritization within scope, handoff, escalation, dignity, collaboration, reflective learning, and service improvement as relevant. Clinical scenario correctness needs current applicable standards and a qualified reviewer. Do not substitute generic coaching for professional assessment.

## Worked contrast

Original synthetic question: **Describe a time information important to care was unclear during a handover. What did you do?**

Weak example: “I took control because patient safety is always my priority.”

Stronger example using different, explicitly fictional facts: “I noticed that the handover did not identify who was following up the concern. I asked for clarification within my role, repeated the agreed information back, and made sure the responsible practitioner had it before I completed the handover. I can describe the communication and follow-through without identifying the patient or claiming I made decisions outside my authority.”

This fictional contrast shows observable communication and boundaries instead of a values slogan. It is not a universal clinical protocol or sufficient evidence of safe care. Ask what the actual concern was in de-identified terms and what the relevant local procedure required. Do not invent treatment details to improve a story.

Use this contrast to explain evidence quality, not to put fictional achievements into the user's answer. For a real transcript, any improved autobiographical answer must use only supplied or subsequently confirmed facts.

## Neutral probes

- What information was missing and how did you verify it?
- What was within your role and what required another practitioner?
- How did you establish that the concern had reached the responsible person?

## Candidate practice

Practise a de-identified handoff or escalation story, then a different communication scenario. Separate the action actually taken from what hindsight suggests. For values questions, elicit behavior demonstrating dignity or teamwork rather than memorizing a slogan. Rehearse a concise explanation of uncertainty and supervision.

Transfer task: Change who is available, the ambiguity, or the communication channel. Ask for the next information needed and the appropriate scope boundary rather than prescribing treatment.

Keep the baseline, coached retry, and independent transfer attempt distinct. Ask one question at a time and wait. If the user wants a simulation, withhold coaching until the agreed debrief. If they want an explanation or plan, do not force a mock.

## Responsibility-level adaptations

Trainee/support: recognizing limits and following appropriate supervision. Newly qualified: applying training and seeking help. Specialist: complex judgment and coordination. Leader: staffing, quality systems, learning from incidents, and accountable improvement, not merely a larger heroic case.

A title alone is insufficient. Ask what independence, complexity, impact, and decision authority the role actually requires. Early-career candidates may use relevant education, voluntary, caring, or project evidence; experienced candidates should explain the scope and limits of their contribution.

## Interviewer practice and candidate experience

Use domain-reviewed questions and anchors, and make entry versus post-hire learning requirements explicit. Treat appropriate escalation as potentially positive evidence. Do not request identifiable patient records or infer personality/mental health from an answer. Practical or clinical competence requires suitable professional assessment.

For interviewer coaching, rehearse the probes against both a vague and a concrete answer. Record what evidence the question elicited, what remains missing, and whether a hint supplied the response. A friendly conversation is not itself a complete assessment. Give candidates space to understand the work and ask informed questions.
