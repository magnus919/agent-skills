# Consulting and business-case interviews

## Basis and task selection

[McKinsey's public interview materials](https://www.mckinsey.com/careers/interviewing) distinguish experience and problem-solving tasks. [Victor Cheng's candidate-led explanation](https://caseinterview.com/candidate-led-case-interview) illustrates active investigation. Verify the current invitation rather than relying on historical employer-format labels.

Establish case type, candidate-led versus interviewer-led expectations, data format, calculator/tools rules, and whether experience questions are a separate round. Clarify role and level; an entry candidate should not be expected to have led a client transformation.

## Evidence to develop

Clarify the decision and success criterion, build a case-specific structure, investigate priority hypotheses, calculate carefully, interpret evidence, and synthesize a recommendation with uncertainty. Ask for relevant data rather than list a generic framework and hope it fits. Use original synthetic cases or official public examples.

## Worked contrast

Original synthetic question: **A cafe's revenue rose from 100 to 120 units, but cost rose from 80 to 110. What can you conclude and what would you investigate?**

Weak example: “Revenue is growing, so it should expand. I would use the four Ps.”

Stronger example using different, explicitly fictional facts: “Profit fell from 20 to 10 in the stated units, despite revenue growth. I would clarify the owner's decision and compare price, volume, mix, and the cost changes relevant to that decision. I would not recommend expansion from these totals alone; I would investigate which costs changed, why, and whether the pattern is persistent.”

The stronger answer uses the supplied facts correctly and resists an unsupported decision. It is not a demand to recite those exact categories. A candidate may propose another useful structure. Probe whether the next data request would distinguish a plausible explanation or merely collect information.

Use this contrast to explain evidence quality, not to put fictional achievements into the user's answer. For a real transcript, any improved autobiographical answer must use only supplied or subsequently confirmed facts.

## Neutral probes

- What decision would this additional data help you make?
- Which assumption matters most to your recommendation?
- How would you explain the result in thirty seconds to the owner?

## Candidate practice

Practise case openings, arithmetic with units, chart interpretation, and final synthesis as separate micro-drills. Then run a short complete case with staged information. If giving a hint, label it and later test another case without it. Do not claim mastery from repeating a model recommendation.

Transfer task: Reveal that the cost rise was a one-off repair rather than recurring labor expense. Ask what changes in the recommendation and what still needs checking.

Keep the baseline, coached retry, and independent transfer attempt distinct. Ask one question at a time and wait. If the user wants a simulation, withhold coaching until the agreed debrief. If they want an explanation or plan, do not force a mock.

## Responsibility-level adaptations

Entry: clear structure, arithmetic, learning, and communication. Experienced hire: domain depth and delivery judgment. Manager: coordinating analysis and client decisions. Partner/leader: problem selection, trust, team development, and responsibility for recommendations and outcomes.

A title alone is insufficient. Ask what independence, complexity, impact, and decision authority the role actually requires. Early-career candidates may use relevant education, voluntary, caring, or project evidence; experienced candidates should explain the scope and limits of their contribution.

## Interviewer practice and candidate experience

Define the decision, sufficient facts, acceptable alternative approaches, hint policy, and evaluation criteria before the case. Keep difficulty comparable. Score reasoning, evidence use, calculation, and synthesis separately. Do not treat speed or an obscure named framework as the entire capability.

For interviewer coaching, rehearse the probes against both a vague and a concrete answer. Record what evidence the question elicited, what remains missing, and whether a hint supplied the response. A friendly conversation is not itself a complete assessment. Give candidates space to understand the work and ask informed questions.
